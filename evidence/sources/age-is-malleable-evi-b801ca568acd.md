## Curated excerpt

> *An essential component of the Fordist work paradigm relied on what Gratton and Scott call the three-stage life (first education, then work and finally retirement). For each chronological age, there’s a matching life stage. And welfare state “cradle-to-grave” institutions tend to put people of the same age together and prevent age mixing.*

## Atomic claims

### Claim 1

Industrial institutions organize education, work, and retirement as age-segregated life stages.

- Classification: `conceptual_claim`
- Scope: The Fordist three-stage life course described by the author.
- Date relevance: Concept articulated 2020-10-15.
- Qualification: Life courses vary across countries, classes, occupations, and family arrangements.

### Claim 2

Organizations and welfare institutions should enable more age mixing rather than treating chronological age as a fixed proxy for life stage.

- Classification: `normative_position`
- Scope: Work and welfare institutions structured around chronological age.
- Date relevance: Position published 2020-10-15.
- Qualification: Age mixing does not remove age-specific health, care, legal, or distributive needs.
