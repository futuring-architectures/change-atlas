## Curated excerpt

> *The productivity myth suggests that anything we spend time on is up for automation — that any time we spend can and should be freed up for the sake of having even more time for other activities or pursuits — which can also be automated. The importance and value of thinking about our work and why we do it is waved away as a distraction. The goal of writing, this myth suggests, is filling a page rather than the process of thought that a completed page represents.*

## Atomic claims

### Claim 1

The productivity myth treats any time-consuming activity as a candidate for automation so that the saved time can be reallocated to other automatable activities.

- Classification: `conceptual_claim`
- Scope: Critique of productivity narratives around generative AI.
- Date relevance: Source published 2024-08-29.
- Qualification: The claim describes a particular ideology of productivity, not every argument for automation.

### Claim 2

Automating writing as page production obscures that writing’s value may lie in the process of thought that produces the page.

- Classification: `normative_position`
- Scope: Use of generative AI in writing and knowledge work.
- Date relevance: Source published 2024-08-29.
- Qualification: Some writing tasks prioritize communication or routine output, so process value varies by context.
