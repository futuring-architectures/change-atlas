## Curated excerpt

> *Online, we enjoy a collective illusion that we are beholden only to the worlds we’ve chosen to join, the tribes we’ve chosen to defend, the games we’ve chosen to play. It is this half-illusion of choice that defines the success of so many of the technologies above. \[..\] As the technology gives us greater access and mobility, it upends a network of physical relationships and contingencies for the poorest and the rich—who often find themselves irrevocably tied to a fallen order.*

## Atomic claims

### Claim 1

The perceived freedom to choose online worlds, tribes, and games is a partial illusion that contributes to technology platforms’ success.

- Classification: `interpretation`
- Scope: Online environments that present users with abundant choices of communities and activities.
- Date relevance: Source published 2021-06-23.
- Qualification: The excerpt gives an interpretive account of perceived choice and does not establish how much platform success it explains.

### Claim 2

Greater digital access and mobility can disrupt place-based networks of relationships and contingencies while leaving people tied to declining orders.

- Classification: `interpretation`
- Scope: People moving between digital and place-based social and economic arrangements.
- Date relevance: Source published 2021-06-23.
- Qualification: The proposed disruption is contextual; digital access can also preserve or extend existing relationships.
