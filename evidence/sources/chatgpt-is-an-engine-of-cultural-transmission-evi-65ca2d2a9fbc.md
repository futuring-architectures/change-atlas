## Curated excerpt

> *Human beings learn in two kinds of ways \- by imitating and by innovating \[…\] LLMs are incapable of innovating, but they are good at imitating, and for some purposes at least, they are much better at it than human beings. That is why we can think of LLMs as a cultural technology. A lot of human culture involves imitation rather than innovation…*

## Atomic claims

### Claim 1

Human learning combines imitation and innovation, whereas current LLMs are strong at imitation but incapable of innovation in the relevant human sense.

- Classification: `conceptual_claim`
- Scope: A model of human and LLM learning as of January 2024.
- Date relevance: Source published 2024-01-11.
- Qualification: The boundary between recombination and innovation is contested, and capability claims are time- and definition-sensitive.

### Claim 2

LLMs function as cultural-transmission technologies because much of culture is reproduced through imitation.

- Classification: `conceptual_claim`
- Scope: The role of language models in transmitting cultural patterns.
- Date relevance: Source published 2024-01-11.
- Qualification: Transmission can transform, distort, homogenize, or selectively amplify culture rather than merely copy it.
