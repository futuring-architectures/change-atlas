## Curated excerpt

> In corporate strategy, people have long debated the relative importance of distribution and product quality […] Personal agents will, I think, act as a kind of structural subsidy for product quality. How would markets look different if individuals always spent at least 10 hours researching their purchases? On the margin, focusing on making a product better is, I think, going to become a more effective strategy for companies. This is good!

## Atomic claims

### Claim 1

Collison interprets firms as able to amortize substantial analytical capacity across consumers, sometimes exploiting individual inattention, limited information, and bounded rationality; booking-process insurance upsells are his example.

- Classification: `interpretation`
- Scope: Cognitive and informational asymmetries between companies and individual consumers.
- Date relevance: Argument published and edited by 2026-10-09, captured 2026-10-16.
- Qualification: The post supplies no comparison of analytical budgets or insurance prices. Calling firms an original superintelligence is an analogy for organized optimization, not a measured intelligence claim or a finding about every transaction.
- Raw lines: 16.

### Claim 2

The author predicts that personal agents will make consumers somewhat more rational in their dealings with firms by helping overcome limits on attention, information, and analysis.

- Classification: `prediction`
- Scope: Consumer decision-making assisted by personal agents.
- Date relevance: Prospective effect proposed 2026-10-09.
- Qualification: The source supplies no consumer experiment or welfare estimate. More analysis does not guarantee better decisions when information, objectives, or the agent's incentives are misaligned.
- Raw lines: 16.

### Claim 3

Collison predicts that some price discrimination will become less effective if agents search systematically for coupons and other available discounts.

- Classification: `prediction`
- Scope: Discounts whose use currently depends on consumers spending attention and search effort.
- Date relevance: Prospective mechanism proposed 2026-10-09.
- Qualification: Universal coupon hunting is hypothetical, not an observed adoption pattern. Sellers could change offers, eligibility, or base prices; the argument does not establish the disappearance of all price discrimination.
- Raw lines: 16.

### Claim 4

The author predicts that agents will alter customer cross-subsidies sustained by inattention, illustrating the mechanism with people who forget to cancel subscriptions subsidizing those who do not.

- Classification: `prediction`
- Scope: Subscription and other commercial arrangements that benefit from consumer forgetfulness.
- Date relevance: Forecast articulated 2026-10-09.
- Qualification: The post provides no cancellation, pricing, or cross-subsidy data and explicitly leaves economy-wide incidence uncertain. Reducing one source of revenue does not determine how firms reprice or which consumers ultimately benefit.
- Raw lines: 16.

### Claim 5

Collison predicts that personal agents will increase the relative strategic payoff from product quality by making thorough purchase research cheaper, weakening some advantages of distribution and habitual brand choice.

- Classification: `prediction`
- Scope: Competition among products that consumers or their agents can compare.
- Date relevance: Forecast proposed 2026-10-09 and selected by the curator.
- Qualification: The ten-hours-of-research example is a thought experiment, not observed agent behavior or a quantified equivalence. Structural subsidy is a metaphor for changing market rewards, not a fiscal payment; distribution is not predicted to become irrelevant. The source supplies no sales or quality-investment comparison.
- Raw lines: 16.

### Claim 6

The author predicts that information about product quality will become more valuable as agents aggregate and integrate it, making data a complement to cheaper intelligence during decisions as well as an input to model training.

- Classification: `prediction`
- Scope: Product information used in agent-mediated evaluation and purchasing.
- Date relevance: A corollary proposed 2026-10-09, conditional on the preceding quality argument.
- Qualification: Access, accuracy, relevance, comparability, and resistance to manipulation matter; a larger supply of claims about quality is not the same as trustworthy quality evidence. No data-market valuation or causal effect is measured.

### Claim 7

Collison identifies two competing demand-allocation mechanisms: agents might converge on the same product and amplify superstar effects, or elicit distinct preferences and spread purchases across a wider range of products.

- Classification: `conceptual_claim`
- Scope: Concentration versus dispersion in agent-mediated product discovery.
- Date relevance: Open question articulated 2026-10-09.
- Qualification: The post does not choose between the mechanisms or measure either. Shared options, information, ranking objectives, and genuine preference differences condition the outcome; the argument is not evidence of changed creator incomes or reduced within-niche inequality.
- Raw lines: 16.

### Claim 8

- Classification: `interpretation`
- Scope: Ranking, placement, and monetized routing of consumer demand.
- Date relevance: Assessment articulated 2026-10-09.
- Qualification: The post supplies no market sample, fee totals, or efficiency estimate. Routing can provide genuine discovery and information services as well as extract attention rents.
