## Atomic claims

### Claim 1

The essay divides work into physical and cognitive work and proposes the Industrial Revolution's externalization of muscular work as the governing analogy for AI's externalization of thinking.

- Classification: `conceptual_claim`
- Scope: A historical analogy for understanding AI and machine-performed cognitive tasks.
- Date relevance: Framework proposed in an article published 2026-09-02.
- Qualification: Physical force has a stable mechanical definition, while “cognitive work,” “thinking,” and the boundary between human direction and machine computation remain contested; the analogy foregrounds scale and price while potentially hiding meaning, agency, verification, and institutional differences.

### Claim 2

The article reports that physical work serving humans shifted over roughly two centuries from 99% biological to 99.9% machine-provided through steam, combustion, and electric motors.

- Classification: `observation`
- Scope: The author's global historical narrative and stylized “Share of Physical Work” chart.
- Date relevance: Retrospective account from the late eighteenth century through 2026.
- Qualification: The chart supplies no dataset, units, source, geographic boundary, definition of “work serving humans,” or method for assigning shares, so the percentages should be treated as illustrative rather than measured estimates.

### Claim 3

The author predicts that neural networks will accelerate an analogous but larger and faster transition in which machines perform 99.9% of cognitive work in the near future while humans may continue thinking in absolute terms.

- Classification: `prediction`
- Scope: Global machine and human cognitive activity across an unspecified near-future horizon.
- Date relevance: Forecast published 2026-09-02; the accompanying chart projects through 2070.
- Qualification: The forecast does not define a common unit of cognitive work, identify a measurement series, specify the transition horizon, or distinguish useful computation from low-value output. A rising machine share does not by itself establish productivity, employment, welfare, or human-capability outcomes.

### Claim 4

The essay predicts that electricity, compute, and algorithms will become the Cognitive Revolution's strategic inputs, concentrating corporate and national power around chip production, data centers, grid capacity, model development, and “intelligence output.”

- Classification: `prediction`
- Scope: AI infrastructure providers, hyperscalers, model laboratories, semiconductor supply chains, and nation-state power.
- Date relevance: Geoeconomic forecast published 2026-09-02 without a specified horizon.
- Qualification: The industrial-input analogy does not establish how power will be distributed across open models, applications, data owners, energy producers, states, labor, or users, and “intelligence output” is not operationally defined.

### Claim 5

The author reports that intelligence per watt has been improving by more than tenfold per year and interprets this as a faster cost collapse than occurred for mechanical work.

- Classification: `observation`
- Scope: An unspecified measure of machine intelligence, compute efficiency, and cost.
- Date relevance: Claimed current rate as of 2026-09-02.
- Qualification: No benchmark, model family, energy boundary, price series, baseline, citation, or distinction between training and inference is supplied. Capability per watt, useful task output, and delivered cognitive-service cost are not interchangeable measures.

### Claim 6

Applying a Jevons-style mechanism, the article predicts that cheaper machine cognition will expand demand by orders of magnitude because many potentially useful questions and analyses currently go unattempted when human thinking is scarce and expensive.

- Classification: `prediction`
- Scope: Research, professional services, medicine, small-business analysis, and other demand for cognitive work.
- Date relevance: Demand forecast published 2026-09-02 without a specified horizon.
- Qualification: Lower inference cost can reveal latent demand, but adoption, data, verification, trust, regulation, physical actuation, purchasing power, and attention can remain binding; more machine-generated analysis is not equivalent to more useful decisions or shared welfare.

### Claim 7

The essay interprets AI coding as the Cognitive Revolution's Spinning Jenny: in the author's account, the machine now performs most code production while the engineer directs, reviews, and corrects it.

- Classification: `interpretation`
- Scope: Contemporary AI-assisted software engineering treated as the first commercially mature skilled cognitive application.
- Date relevance: Practitioner interpretation published 2026-09-02.
- Qualification: The source supplies no usage sample, task boundary, repository evidence, quality comparison, prevalence estimate, or occupational outcome. The claim may fit some agentic workflows while overstating adoption in other organizations, codebases, and safety classes.

### Claim 8

The author predicts that the era's most consequential application has not yet been built and may emerge as machine-speed science, a personal chief-of-staff agent, or a new system for human connection and coordination.

- Classification: `prediction`
- Scope: Future applications enabled by abundant machine cognition.
- Date relevance: Open-ended product forecast published 2026-09-02.
- Qualification: The candidates are scenarios rather than observed products or a falsifiable market forecast, and the automobile analogy does not show which application will reshape institutions or daily life.

### Claim 9

The article reports that U.S. agricultural employment fell from more than 75% of workers in 1800 to about 1% today while mechanization eliminated tasks and occupations without producing economy-wide mass unemployment.

- Classification: `observation`
- Scope: Long-run U.S. agricultural employment and the creation of new occupations during industrialization.
- Date relevance: Historical comparison from 1800 to 2026.
- Qualification: The article provides no underlying labor series and uses present employment totals and the author's family history to support an aggregate argument. Population growth, working-time changes, labor-force participation, migration, policy, sectoral composition, job quality, and transitional unemployment are not analyzed.

### Claim 10

The author reports that demand for software engineers has recently accelerated or inflected upward despite the occupation's high AI exposure and announces forthcoming evidence of similar hiring changes in legal and financial services.

- Classification: `observation`
- Scope: Unspecified software-engineering labor-demand data, with future analysis promised for legal and financial services.
- Date relevance: Claimed contemporary labor-market change as of 2026-09-02.
- Qualification: The article does not reproduce the data, define demand, identify geography, separate postings from hiring or employment, state the comparison period, or control for macroeconomic recovery. The promised legal and financial evidence is not present and is not treated as an observation.

### Claim 11

The essay predicts that machine-performed cognition will change human tasks rather than end human cognitive work: a junior analyst may direct many models, and new occupations may arise that people cannot currently name.

- Classification: `prediction`
- Scope: Knowledge-work roles and economy-wide occupational formation under growing AI capability.
- Date relevance: Labor forecast published 2026-09-02 without a specified horizon.
- Qualification: Historical creation of jobs after mechanization does not determine the speed, quantity, distribution, quality, entry paths, or bargaining power of future work. Directing many models may augment some workers while eliminating roles, intensifying oversight, or narrowing apprenticeship for others.

### Claim 12

Using Robert Allen's account of Engels' pause, the article reports that British output per worker rose 46% while real wages rose 12% from 1780 to 1840, followed by gains of 90% and 123% respectively from 1840 to 1900, and argues that aggregate technological success can coexist with generational losses for displaced workers.

- Classification: `observation`
- Scope: British industrialization and the experience of handloom weavers used as a distributional analogy for AI.
- Date relevance: Historical statistics interpreted in a 2026 transition argument.
- Qualification: The article gives no citation to Allen's underlying series, and historical estimates and periodization are contestable. Later aggregate wage growth did not compensate the original displaced workers and does not forecast the timing or distribution of AI-era gains.

### Claim 13

Because the author expects AI-driven role change to occur much faster than industrialization, the article argues that education, retraining, and safety nets must be redesigned so people can adapt on the new clock rather than absorb a new Engels' pause.

- Classification: `normative_position`
- Scope: Public policy and labor-market institutions responding to rapid AI capability and occupational change over the next two decades.
- Date relevance: Transition-design position published 2026-09-02.
- Qualification: The contrast between forty years and five years is illustrative and unsupported, and “adaptation” can shift responsibility onto workers unless institutions also address job creation, bargaining power, income, ownership, displacement, care, geographic mismatch, and meaningful refusal.

### Claim 14

The essay interprets mass schooling as partly an industrial institution for standardized, synchronized factory work and predicts that factory-model education will be misaligned when machines perform more cognition and individualized AI tutoring becomes nearly free.

- Classification: `interpretation`
- Scope: Historical formation and future redesign of mass education.
- Date relevance: Historical argument and contemporary forecast published 2026-09-02.
- Qualification: Industrial labor demand was one of many influences on schooling, and low marginal inference cost does not make safe, accurate, motivating, culturally appropriate education free; devices, connectivity, curriculum, supervision, relationships, assessment, and governance remain costly.

### Claim 15

The article reports that hybrid human–AI tutors can be more effective and less costly than traditional education and predicts that such systems could expand education where public provision has been prohibitively expensive.

- Classification: `observation`
- Scope: An externally linked account of Stanford-affiliated hybrid tutoring and its potential extension to underserved education systems.
- Date relevance: Evidence summarized and extrapolated in an article published 2026-09-02.
- Qualification: The article provides no study design, population, outcomes, duration, comparison condition, or implementation boundary. One linked account cannot establish general superiority or affordability across ages, subjects, languages, institutions, and infrastructure conditions.

### Claim 16

The author predicts that cheaper cognition will accelerate drug discovery, make neglected long-tail diseases economically addressable, expand clinical capacity amid physician shortages, and help shift medicine from reactive treatment toward proactive care.

- Classification: `prediction`
- Scope: Biomedical research, rare or neglected diseases, and clinical healthcare in the United States and globally.
- Date relevance: Health-system forecast published 2026-09-02.
- Qualification: AlphaFold demonstrates a major structure-prediction capability but does not remove experimentation, trials, manufacturing, regulation, reimbursement, clinical judgment, patient trust, or care-delivery constraints. Lower analysis cost does not ensure research priorities or access are allocated to underserved patients.

### Claim 17

The essay predicts that continuously available asynchronous machine colleagues will loosen synchronized nine-to-five work and that ubiquitous cognitive tools will recombine home and work after industrial machinery helped separate them.

- Classification: `prediction`
- Scope: Work schedules, location, domestic boundaries, and coordination practices in AI-mediated organizations.
- Date relevance: Social forecast published 2026-09-02 without a specified horizon.
- Qualification: Asynchronous automation may reduce coordination constraints or intensify continuous availability, surveillance, and boundary erosion. Work location and schedules also reflect care, labor law, power, collaboration, facilities, customers, and worker preference.

### Claim 18

The author predicts that energy, compute, and algorithms will function as geopolitical raw materials, that intelligence will be dual-use like industrial capacity, and that countries which miss the transition will suffer durable disadvantage.

- Classification: `prediction`
- Scope: International power, trade, infrastructure, security, and access to AI capability.
- Date relevance: Geopolitical forecast published 2026-09-02 without a specified horizon.
- Qualification: The source offers an analogy rather than comparative national evidence and does not address software diffusion, alliances, regulation, supply-chain interdependence, open access, adaptation strategies, or the harms of treating adoption as an unavoidable race.

### Claim 19

The article reports that human chess remained popular after Deep Blue and that professional Go players produced more novel moves after AlphaGo, interpreting superhuman machine play as a possible stimulus to human creativity rather than the end of human practice.

- Classification: `observation`
- Scope: Chess participation and a cited but unidentified study of novelty in professional Go after AlphaGo.
- Date relevance: Historical game cases used in a 2026 argument.
- Qualification: Participation counts and the Go study are not supplied, and games are voluntary, status-rich practices with clear feedback; their response to superhuman systems may not generalize to paid work, education, medicine, or activities where automation removes access and livelihood.

### Claim 20

The essay argues that wanting, choosing, accepting accountability, and earning human trust will remain economically human longer than task cognition, and normatively concludes that the transition should be shaped to share prosperity and elevate humanity.

- Classification: `normative_position`
- Scope: Human roles, responsibility, trust, and the desired distributional purpose of AI transition.
- Date relevance: Philosophical and policy position published 2026-09-02.
- Qualification: Machines can mediate preferences and decisions, institutions can diffuse accountability, and humans may be retained as symbolic signatories or liability sinks without real authority. “Elevate humanity” and “share prosperity” require explicit rights, ownership, measures, and governance to become more than aspirations.
