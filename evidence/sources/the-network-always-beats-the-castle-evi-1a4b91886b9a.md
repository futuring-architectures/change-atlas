## Atomic claims

### Claim 1

The article presents packet switching as an initially lower-quality but ultimately more scalable and generative architecture than circuit switching because it moved intelligence and recovery to network edges and relied on simple protocols rather than centrally reserved paths.

- Classification: `interpretation`
- Scope: The historical transition from dedicated telephone circuits to packet-switched digital networks, used as the essay's foundational analogy.
- Date relevance: Historical interpretation published 2026-06-28.
- Qualification: The account compresses decades of technical, regulatory, commercial, and institutional change; modern networks retain substantial centralized control, hierarchy, quality guarantees, and capital concentration, so the contrast is not a clean binary.

### Claim 2

The author models conventional firms as circuit-switched systems in which org charts reserve paths between strategy and execution, managers route and translate work, and meetings, reports, one-to-ones, committees, and reorgs maintain those fixed paths.

- Classification: `conceptual_claim`
- Scope: Formal organizations whose coordination and accountability rely substantially on managerial hierarchy.
- Date relevance: Organizational analogy proposed 2026-06-28.
- Qualification: These activities also produce interpretation, trust, development, negotiation, care, and legitimate authority; treating them all as path-maintenance overhead can erase functions that routing automation does not replace.

### Claim 3

The article argues that earlier experiments with holacracy, self-managing teams, flat hierarchies, and network organizations often failed because they lacked affordable infrastructure for routing, protocols, addressing, error correction, capability discovery, observation, and accountability.

- Classification: `interpretation`
- Scope: Prior decentralized organization-design experiments, especially those discussed in management literature.
- Date relevance: Retrospective explanation published 2026-06-28.
- Qualification: No cases, comparison set, or failure analysis is supplied; governance, incentives, power, culture, labor relations, strategy, and task interdependence may explain failures independently of missing information infrastructure.

### Claim 4

Schillace reports that his teams are beginning to experiment with AI systems that route work to capabilities, but that these systems remain intermittent because plumbing, context, and trust are incomplete.

- Classification: `observation`
- Scope: Unspecified early systems used by the author's own teams.
- Date relevance: Current-practice report published 2026-06-28.
- Qualification: The organizations, systems, task volumes, performance, failures, and comparison conditions are not identified, so the account establishes experimentation rather than effectiveness or general adoption.

### Claim 5

The author predicts that inexpensive, widely available AI will make “packet-switched” organizations viable by pushing intelligence, decisions, and behavior toward organizational edges, increasing scale, creativity, responsiveness, and productive use of scarce human attention.

- Classification: `prediction`
- Scope: Businesses and, by extension, political, social, educational, and creative organizations.
- Date relevance: Forecast published 2026-06-28.
- Qualification: The article provides no comparative outcomes or adoption timeline; distributed execution can coexist with centralized objectives, surveillance, platform control, unequal bargaining power, coordination failure, and new attention burdens.

### Claim 6

The proposed routing layer lets AI interpret a work item and its constraints, then match it to suitable human capability without requiring a manager or requester to know the organizational topology.

- Classification: `conceptual_claim`
- Scope: AI-mediated task routing inside organizations.
- Date relevance: Design mechanism proposed 2026-06-28.
- Qualification: Matching quality depends on current and legitimate capability data, task decomposition, consent, workload, availability, development goals, bias controls, and recourse; removing a manager from routing does not remove managerial power from the rules.

### Claim 7

The proposed translation layer makes edge work continuously legible across organizational boundaries, reducing the human effort managers spend translating local activity for adjacent teams or central leadership.

- Classification: `conceptual_claim`
- Scope: AI-mediated organizational reporting and cross-boundary legibility.
- Date relevance: Design mechanism proposed 2026-06-28.
- Qualification: Automated legibility can flatten context, intensify surveillance, privilege measurable activity, or give recipients false confidence; translation also includes negotiation and meaning-making that cannot be assumed to be rote.

### Claim 8

The proposed error-correction layer monitors missing work, blockers, and off-target metrics continuously, replacing some follow-ups, status checks, escalation paths, meetings, objectives, and reports with detection and recovery loops.

- Classification: `conceptual_claim`
- Scope: AI-mediated monitoring and recovery in organizational workflows.
- Date relevance: Design mechanism proposed 2026-06-28.
- Qualification: Monitoring can create alert overload, Goodhart effects, privacy harms, punitive control, and correlated automated error; recovery requires causal diagnosis, authority, and escalation rather than detection alone.

### Claim 9

The proposed protocol-negotiation layer surfaces assumptions about format, constraints, interfaces, and success before work begins, then checks whether agreed goals were met.

- Classification: `conceptual_claim`
- Scope: Scoping, contracting, alignment, and acceptance between teams or systems.
- Date relevance: Design mechanism proposed 2026-06-28.
- Qualification: Some assumptions are tacit, contested, political, or discovered only through practice; formalizing them can improve coordination or prematurely freeze ambiguity and shift bargaining power toward whoever defines the protocol.

### Claim 10

The proposed capability-address layer combines skills graphs, project history, and AI matching so work can discover a suitable person or team independently of the org chart.

- Classification: `conceptual_claim`
- Scope: Organizational capability discovery and work allocation.
- Date relevance: Prospective mechanism published 2026-06-28; the author says no mature form yet exists.
- Qualification: Capability registries can become stale, reductive, discriminatory, or self-reinforcing; historical project assignment is not a neutral measure of ability, potential, interest, or fair access to development.

### Claim 11

The article proposes a protocol suite for distributed organizations: differentiated reliability classes for different work, capability registries, nested trust and permission boundaries, accountability domains, and peer protocols for coordination across autonomous teams or organizations.

- Classification: `normative_position`
- Scope: Organization design for AI-mediated decentralized work.
- Date relevance: Design proposal published 2026-06-28.
- Qualification: The networking analogy does not specify how human rights, purpose, conflicts, employment obligations, ownership, voice, strategy, or legitimacy become protocol; strict contracts can enable autonomy while also encoding invisible centralized power.

### Claim 12

The author argues that the architecture is emerging but its protocols remain immature, and warns that adopting decentralization as an aesthetic before building reliable contracts will produce “chaos with good marketing” rather than empowerment.

- Classification: `conceptual_claim`
- Scope: The present maturity and implementation risks of packet-switched organization design.
- Date relevance: Maturity assessment published 2026-06-28.
- Qualification: The “around 1972” analogy is rhetorical rather than a measured maturity level; distributed experimentation may discover protocols but can also externalize harms before shared standards, enforcement, and recourse exist.

### Claim 13

The article predicts that AI-native network organizations will eventually outperform centrally planned organizations and enable new forms such as problem-specific groups that dissolve after completion, expertise routed independently of headcount, and changed institutional memory, training, and job design.

- Classification: `prediction`
- Scope: Long-run organizational competition and institutional form across multiple sectors.
- Date relevance: Forecast published 2026-06-28 without a specified horizon.
- Qualification: “Networks always win” is a strong extrapolation from analogy rather than comparative organizational evidence; firms, hierarchies, markets, protocols, and networks often coexist, and outcomes depend on task interdependence, power, law, capital, governance, and distribution of gains and risks.
