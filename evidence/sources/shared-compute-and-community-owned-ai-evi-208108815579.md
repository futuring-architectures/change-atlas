## Atomic claims

### Claim 1

Shared compute could let a community pool capital and technical operation to run a capable open model on hardware it owns instead of requiring every member to buy hardware or rent intelligence individually from a model provider.

- Classification: `conceptual_claim`
- Scope: Community deployment of compute-intensive open models.
- Date relevance: Proposal published 2026-07-25.
- Qualification: Pooling does not remove hardware, energy, maintenance, networking, security, or specialist-administration costs, and the source provides no operating-cost comparison.

### Claim 2

A community-operated AI system could keep private group data within the community and use that accumulating context to become unusually capable in the group’s bounded domain.

- Classification: `prediction`
- Scope: Profession- or community-specific AI using locally controlled data.
- Date relevance: Prediction published 2026-07-25.
- Qualification: Model improvement may require retrieval, fine-tuning, evaluation, and data governance rather than passive “learning”; private data can be incomplete, biased, contested, copyrighted, confidential, or unsafe to aggregate.

### Claim 3

When capable base models are widely available, defensibility may shift from exclusive model access toward the private data, context, evaluation, and accumulated knowledge associated with a particular community.

- Classification: `conceptual_claim`
- Scope: Competitive advantage in vertical and community-owned AI.
- Date relevance: Strategic interpretation published 2026-07-25.
- Qualification: Data is neither automatically unique nor useful, competitors may reproduce relevant context through other relationships, and model, distribution, trust, workflow integration, and execution can remain differentiating.

### Claim 4

Pooling a machine across members could lower per-person compute cost, while renting otherwise idle capacity could turn the same infrastructure into an income-producing asset.

- Classification: `prediction`
- Scope: Economics of shared community compute.
- Date relevance: Business-model prediction published 2026-07-25.
- Qualification: Utilization, scheduling, performance isolation, depreciation, energy prices, demand, regulation, and security may prevent idle capacity from being reliably or profitably rented.

### Claim 5

Communities could become units of intelligence in which members contribute money and data to a shared AI asset, hold an ownership interest, and accumulate economic value as the system improves.

- Classification: `prediction`
- Scope: Collective ownership and governance of community AI.
- Date relevance: Institutional prediction published 2026-07-25.
- Qualification: The source does not specify legal ownership, governance rights, contribution valuation, benefit distribution, exit, liability, privacy, or protections for people affected by the system but outside the community.

### Claim 6

Combining open protocols with community-owned hardware, models, and data could reduce dependence on centralized labs by giving the group practical control over access, pricing, customization, and continued operation.

- Classification: `conceptual_claim`
- Scope: Technological self-determination in community AI infrastructure.
- Date relevance: Decentralization proposal published 2026-07-25.
- Qualification: Open protocols and local hardware do not by themselves distribute decision rights, prevent internal concentration, guarantee viable exit, or remove dependency on chips, energy, upstream models, software maintainers, and connectivity.

### Claim 7

A profession-specific “vertical brain” and marketplace for collective brains could turn pooled community knowledge into a difficult-to-copy AI service whose usage revenue flows back to members.

- Classification: `prediction`
- Scope: Vertical-AI and expertise-marketplace startup models.
- Date relevance: Business-model prediction published 2026-07-25.
- Qualification: The claim assumes effective consent, rights clearance, knowledge aggregation, quality control, market demand, revenue governance, and a defensibility that the source does not demonstrate.
