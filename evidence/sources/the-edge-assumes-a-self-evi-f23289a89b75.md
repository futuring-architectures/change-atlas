## Curated excerpt

> *Hierarchy did two things: It routed information — who knows what, who decides what, how context travels up and down the chain. And it routed meaning. It told people what mattered when nothing was clear. It held people inside a legible world. AI can plausibly replace the first function. But the second — the meaning-making scaffolding that hierarchy provided — is not a technical problem. It’s a developmental one.*

## Atomic claims

### Claim 1

Organizational hierarchy routes not only information but also meaning by interpreting context, priorities, and identity for participants.

- Classification: `conceptual_claim`
- Scope: The informational and developmental functions of organizational hierarchy.
- Date relevance: Concept articulated 2026-04-06.
- Qualification: Hierarchy can distort meaning and exclude local knowledge; meaning can also be produced through peers, practice, communities, and external institutions.

### Claim 2

AI may replace parts of hierarchy's information-routing function without replacing the human scaffolding through which people develop judgment and meaning.

- Classification: `interpretation`
- Scope: Organizational redesign around AI-mediated information flows.
- Date relevance: Interpretation published 2026-04-06.
- Qualification: AI may participate in coaching and sensemaking, while human hierarchy does not always provide effective developmental scaffolding.
