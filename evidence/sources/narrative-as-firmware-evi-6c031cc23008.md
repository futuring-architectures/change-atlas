## Curated excerpt

> *Most corporations treat narratives, if they have one at all, as software: easy to deploy, easy to tweak, updated often. They put out content marketing here, campaigns there, some boilerplate on a pitch deck, the ‘About Us’ section on a website, an elevator pitch, and maybe a shiny new rebrand. If it doesn’t work, they patch and move on, building up narrative debt over time. Superior narratives don’t work that way. They act like firmware.*

## Atomic claims

### Claim 1

Organizations accumulate narrative debt when they repeatedly patch campaigns, pitches, and brand language without a durable underlying story.

- Classification: `conceptual_claim`
- Scope: Corporate narrative and identity systems.
- Date relevance: Concept articulated 2025-04-25.
- Qualification: Plural organizations may need multiple evolving narratives; durability can become rigidity or propaganda.

### Claim 2

A strong organizational narrative should operate like firmware: deeply embedded, coherent, and slower to change than surface communications.

- Classification: `normative_position`
- Scope: Design and stewardship of corporate narratives.
- Date relevance: Position published 2025-04-25.
- Qualification: The firmware metaphor does not specify who authors the narrative, how dissent enters, or when foundational revision is necessary.
