## Curated excerpt

> *What AI does that previous generations of computers could not do is to respond in novel, not pre-programmed ways to stimuli. This opens up the opportunity that people with less training and lower skills are able to produce the outputs (in many cases, choices or decisions) that would have previously required extensive training and field practice \[…\] With answers to key factual questions available at the push of a button, that form of expertise is likely to become far less pricey.*

## Atomic claims

### Claim 1

AI can let less-trained people produce outputs that previously required extended professional education and practice.

- Classification: `prediction`
- Scope: Expert services exposed to generative decision support.
- Date relevance: Forecast published 2024-05-29.
- Qualification: Output equivalence does not establish judgment, accountability, or performance in edge cases.

### Claim 2

Cheap factual answers will reduce the price premium attached to some forms of expertise.

- Classification: `prediction`
- Scope: Markets for expert professional knowledge.
- Date relevance: Forecast published 2024-05-29.
- Qualification: Tacit knowledge, trust, regulation, responsibility, and complex synthesis may retain value.
