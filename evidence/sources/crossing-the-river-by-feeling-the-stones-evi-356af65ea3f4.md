## Curated excerpt

> *Not knowing has heavy implications. When you really do not know what’s coming next, there’s no logical calculus you can bring to bear, so the rational decision-making process gets ruled out. That means that the whole rational decision-making doctrine you’re taught in business school no longer applies. When you really do not know what’s coming next, there’s no logical calculus you can bring to bear … What’s needed in this situation is not rational calculation but resilience.*

## Atomic claims

### Claim 1

Under fundamental uncertainty, outcomes lack a calculable probability basis, so conventional rational decision calculus cannot determine the optimal action.

- Classification: `conceptual_claim`
- Scope: Business decisions involving true unknowns rather than estimable risk.
- Date relevance: Concept articulated 2023-08-01.
- Qualification: Many decisions mix uncertainty with measurable risks; abandoning calculation entirely can discard useful partial information.

### Claim 2

Organizations should emphasize resilience—the capacity to respond appropriately—when they cannot know what will happen next.

- Classification: `normative_position`
- Scope: Organizational decision posture under fundamental uncertainty.
- Date relevance: Normative position published 2023-08-01.
- Qualification: Resilience can conflict with efficiency and growth, and the excerpt does not determine the right portfolio of redundancy, options, or responses.
