## Curated excerpt

> I’m beginning to think we’ve been treating organisational design as an implementation problem when it may actually be the primary design problem. The model still matters, and the harness still matters. But perhaps we’ve misunderstood what a harness really is. We talk about it as though it’s another software layer. Increasingly, I think it’s the software expression of the operating model itself.

## Atomic claims

### Claim 1

Enterprise AI capability emerges from the system around a model—context management, routing, memory, tools, evaluation, and deterministic orchestration—so similar underlying models can produce materially different organizational outcomes.

- Classification: `interpretation`
- Scope: Enterprise deployment of generative-AI models through software harnesses.
- Date relevance: Interpretation published 2026-07-23.
- Qualification: The article offers a practitioner synthesis rather than comparative deployment data, and model differences can remain decisive for some tasks.

### Claim 2

An AI harness coordinates work, retrieves knowledge, invokes tools, routes decisions, escalates exceptions, and applies governance; these responsibilities originate in the operating model the harness serves rather than in the software layer alone.

- Classification: `conceptual_claim`
- Scope: Relationship between AI orchestration software and organizational operations.
- Date relevance: Conceptual model published 2026-07-23.
- Qualification: A harness can also introduce new workflows or constraints, and technical design may reshape rather than simply inherit the operating model.

### Claim 3

An operating model is the system through which work actually happens—including decision flow, accountability, information flow, coordination, and the division of responsibility between people and intelligent systems—not merely the organization chart.

- Classification: `conceptual_claim`
- Scope: Organizational design for AI-enabled work.
- Date relevance: Definition published 2026-07-23.
- Qualification: The definition foregrounds operational structure but does not exhaust culture, incentives, power, informal practice, labor relations, or external institutional constraints.

### Claim 4

AI capability is layered: model capability emerges through its harness, harness capability emerges through the organization’s operating model, and each upper layer constrains what the layer beneath can accomplish.

- Classification: `conceptual_claim`
- Scope: A model–harness–operating-model dependency structure for enterprise AI.
- Date relevance: Layered interpretation published 2026-07-23.
- Qualification: Influence can run in both directions, layer boundaries are porous, and the article does not test the relative contribution of each layer.

### Claim 5

Organizations making similar model and harness choices may achieve different outcomes because their operating models differ, making organizational design a primary AI-system design problem rather than a downstream implementation concern.

- Classification: `interpretation`
- Scope: Strategic design of enterprise AI systems and organizations.
- Date relevance: Interpretation published 2026-07-23.
- Qualification: The source supplies no paired organizational cases or outcome measures, and differences attributed to operating models may also reflect data, skill, resources, timing, or market context.

### Claim 6

As AI harnesses encode how work, knowledge, decisions, exceptions, governance, and accountability operate, designing intelligent systems and designing organizations increasingly become the same interdependent design problem.

- Classification: `conceptual_claim`
- Scope: Convergence of software architecture and organization design in AI-mediated work.
- Date relevance: Conceptual claim published 2026-07-23.
- Qualification: Organizations retain social, political, legal, and cultural properties that software cannot fully encode, while software can behave differently from the formal design it implements.
