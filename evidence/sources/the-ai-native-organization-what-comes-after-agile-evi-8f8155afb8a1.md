## Curated excerpt

> *The organizations that successfully become AI-native are precisely those that have built the surrounding infrastructure: the data foundations, the evaluation discipline, the governance structures and the learning loops that allow AI to be deployed with confidence rather than hope \[…\] An organization can’t flatten its structure around AI agents it doesn’t trust, and it can’t trust agents it can’t evaluate, monitor and correct.*

## Atomic claims

### Claim 1

Organizations that successfully become AI-native build supporting infrastructure for data, evaluation, governance, and continuous learning rather than merely adding AI tools to existing teams.

- Classification: `interpretation`
- Scope: Organizational design for sustained use of AI agents.
- Date relevance: Interpretation published 2026-06-22.
- Qualification: The source states an emerging design thesis; comparative outcome evidence across AI-native organizations remains limited.

### Claim 2

Trust in AI agents must be earned through evaluation, monitoring, and correction before an organization can safely flatten structures around them.

- Classification: `conceptual_claim`
- Scope: Trust and control in AI-native organizational design.
- Date relevance: Concept articulated 2026-06-22.
- Qualification: Evaluation can be incomplete or gamed, and high-risk decisions may require durable human authority even after strong observed performance.
