## Curated excerpt

> *We, as humans, \[…\] pause and consider: does this word really stand for the thing I want it to stand for? Does this sentence capture the real experience I’m having? Does the tether hold? Where a language model is concerned, words and sentences don’t stand for things; they are the things. All is text, and text is all.*

## Atomic claims

### Claim 1

Human language use includes checking whether words refer to intended things and whether sentences fit lived experience.

- Classification: `conceptual_claim`
- Scope: The relationship between human language, reference, and experience.
- Date relevance: Source published 2023-12-17.
- Qualification: Humans also produce language habitually or without careful referential checking.

### Claim 2

For a language model, words and sentences operate as text in relation to other text rather than as symbols tethered to experienced things.

- Classification: `conceptual_claim`
- Scope: A distinction between language-model processing and human reference.
- Date relevance: Source published 2023-12-17.
- Qualification: Tool-using and multimodal systems can connect text to external data, but that does not itself establish lived reference or experience.
