## Curated excerpt

> *Your brain will always try to save time when forming beliefs — it’s what it does — but the best way to save time is not to take a shortcut to “truth,” it’s to take no route at all. So if you want to stop being an NPC, simply say “I don’t know” to all the matters that don’t concern you. And that will give you the time to not be an NPC on all the matters that do.*

## Atomic claims

### Claim 1

The brain economizes when forming beliefs and therefore tends to use cognitive shortcuts.

- Classification: `conceptual_claim`
- Scope: Human belief formation under limited time and attention.
- Date relevance: Source published 2023-08-27.
- Qualification: Heuristics can be adaptive as well as error-producing; the excerpt focuses on their cost to independent judgment.

### Claim 2

People should suspend judgment on matters that do not concern them so they can devote time to thinking independently about matters that do.

- Classification: `normative_position`
- Scope: Attention allocation and epistemic restraint.
- Date relevance: Source published 2023-08-27.
- Qualification: What counts as relevant can change, and broad civic decisions may concern people indirectly.
