## Curated excerpt

> *In the era of superfast many-to-many communication, the ideas that spread are the ones which can “take over” the entire spectrum. Small ideas grow, wither, die. It's only the memetic megafauna that survive. In a sparse network you might have pockets which retain their individuality and survive for longer. Like Galapagos syndrome for ideas. Both good and bad.*

## Atomic claims

### Claim 1

In dense, high-speed many-to-many communication networks, ideas that can dominate the full information spectrum outcompete smaller ideas.

- Classification: `interpretation`
- Scope: The circulation of ideas in dense modern information networks.
- Date relevance: Source published 2024-06-19.
- Qualification: The “memetic megafauna” formulation is metaphorical and the excerpt does not define a measure of dominance or compare diffusion outcomes systematically.

### Claim 2

Sparse networks can preserve distinct pockets of ideas for longer than dense networks, with both beneficial and harmful consequences.

- Classification: `conceptual_claim`
- Scope: Information networks with relatively few connections among nodes or partially isolated clusters.
- Date relevance: Source published 2024-06-19.
- Qualification: Sparsity can protect variation but may also reduce learning, coordination, reach, and correction; the excerpt explicitly leaves the value ambiguous.
