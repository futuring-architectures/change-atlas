## Curated excerpt

> *Companies should design products with the expectation that bad actors will abuse them. If there is a “hero-use case,” the paradigmatic usage of a product by a well-meaning user, then there is also a “villain-use case”: core ways that a user with ill intent can abuse the same product. Because there is no way to eliminate this risk of abuse, companies should also mitigate the harms they facilitate.*

## Atomic claims

### Claim 1

Product design should assume that malicious actors will abuse the product.

- Classification: `normative_position`
- Scope: Technology products that can be repurposed by users with harmful intent.
- Date relevance: Source published 2022-09-16.
- Qualification: The claim does not specify the severity or probability threshold that should drive design decisions.

### Claim 2

A product's intended hero use case has corresponding villain use cases in which the same capabilities are abused.

- Classification: `conceptual_claim`
- Scope: Dual-use technology products and their core capabilities.
- Date relevance: Source published 2022-09-16.
- Qualification: The framework directs attention to abuse but does not imply symmetry between beneficial and harmful uses.

### Claim 3

Because abuse risk cannot be eliminated, companies should mitigate harms their products facilitate.

- Classification: `normative_position`
- Scope: Company responsibility for foreseeable abuse of technology products.
- Date relevance: Source published 2022-09-16.
- Qualification: The excerpt does not allocate responsibility among companies, users, governments, and other actors.
