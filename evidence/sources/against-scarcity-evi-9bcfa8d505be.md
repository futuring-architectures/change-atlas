## Curated excerpt

> *\[O\]ur online worlds should enable a post-scarcity infinitude of freely shared ideas, goods, and protocols, but of course that would only make sense if they were untethered to the actual world where we need money to sleep and eat. Is the web’s artificial replication of scarcity economics an early-stage quirk, or is it a more fundamental reflection of the fact that we can only visit virtual worlds, but ultimately have to live in one of rocks and trees?*

## Atomic claims

### Claim 1

Online worlds should enable freely shared ideas, goods, and protocols that take advantage of digital replicability.

- Classification: `normative_position`
- Scope: Digitally reproducible resources in online worlds.
- Date relevance: Source published 2021-06-30.
- Qualification: The position does not imply that all digital goods have zero production, coordination, or maintenance cost.

### Claim 2

Digital post-scarcity remains in tension with material scarcity because people ultimately depend on food, shelter, and a physical world.

- Classification: `conceptual_claim`
- Scope: The relationship between replicable digital goods and embodied material needs.
- Date relevance: Source published 2021-06-30.
- Qualification: The excerpt poses the tension as an open question and does not determine whether artificial digital scarcity is transient or structurally necessary.
