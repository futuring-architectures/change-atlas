## Curated excerpt

> *\[T\]echnology itself will return to the forefront: if the priority for an increasing number of citizens, companies, and countries is to escape centralization, then the answer will not be competing centralized entities, but rather a return to open protocols \[..\] Open technologies can be worked on collectively, and forked individually, gaining both the benefits of scale and inevitability of sovereignty and self-determination.*

## Atomic claims

### Claim 1

If participants prioritize escaping centralization, competing centralized entities will not provide the solution; open protocols will.

- Classification: `prediction`
- Scope: Internet services where users actively seek an alternative to centralized control.
- Date relevance: Source published 2021-01-12; this is a conditional forecast.
- Qualification: The outcome depends on participants actually prioritizing decentralization and on open protocols overcoming adoption and governance constraints.

### Claim 2

Open technologies allow collective development while preserving the possibility of individual forking, potentially combining scale with sovereignty and self-determination.

- Classification: `conceptual_claim`
- Scope: Open technological protocols and the communities that develop them.
- Date relevance: Source published 2021-01-12; the claim describes a structural affordance rather than a dated event.
- Qualification: The ability to fork does not guarantee that a fork will be viable, widely adopted, or meaningfully sovereign.
