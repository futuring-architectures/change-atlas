## Curated excerpt

> *I’m more concerned with Dickens-style harms: people losing jobs not because AI can do their work, but because people in charge will think AI can do other people’s work. Harms due to people misunderstanding what AI does and doesn’t do well and misusing it \[…\] The balance will depend on how well we collectively use it and educate each other, and how well we pay attention to what is happening.*

## Atomic claims

### Claim 1

Workers can lose jobs because managers overestimate what AI can accomplish, even when the technology cannot competently replace the work.

- Classification: `prediction`
- Scope: Managerial adoption of AI in workplaces.
- Date relevance: Forecast published 2025-03-17.
- Qualification: The excerpt proposes a plausible mechanism and cites concerns, but it does not estimate how often overestimation causes displacement.

### Claim 2

The employment effects of AI depend on how decision-makers use it, how well they are educated about it, and how carefully they attend to outcomes.

- Classification: `normative_position`
- Scope: Responsible organizational deployment of AI.
- Date relevance: Normative position published 2025-03-17.
- Qualification: Education and attention do not resolve incentives, bargaining power, competitive pressure, or broader labor-market policy.
