## Curated excerpt

> *Humans are not born at the “frontier” of knowledge; it takes us time to reach it. As we push the frontier further out, reaching it takes successive generations longer \[…\] The more we know, the more the next generation must learn to contribute at the cutting edge \[…\] Artificial intelligence, writ-large, does not suffer from the burden of knowledge. This technology does not die or degrade, it simply improves.*

## Atomic claims

### Claim 1

As the knowledge frontier advances, successive human generations must spend longer learning before they can contribute at the frontier.

- Classification: `conceptual_claim`
- Scope: Human education and frontier knowledge production.
- Date relevance: Source published 2023-06-25.
- Qualification: Learning time varies by field, tools, institutions, and specialization; the excerpt gives no longitudinal measure.

### Claim 2

AI does not bear the human cost of mortality and relearning, allowing accumulated capability to persist and improve.

- Classification: `interpretation`
- Scope: Comparison of human and machine accumulation of knowledge.
- Date relevance: Source published 2023-06-25.
- Qualification: Models can degrade, become obsolete, lose access to data, and require retraining; persistence is institutional and technical, not intrinsic immortality.
