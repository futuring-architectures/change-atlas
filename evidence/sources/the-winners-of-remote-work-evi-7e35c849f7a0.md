## Curated excerpt

> *When most companies hired only employees who lived within commuting distance of the office, the size of the labor market was capped. This put a ceiling on the employment options and earning capacity of employees with the most specialized, in-demand skills. It also put a floor beneath other professionals who enjoyed a decent salary and relative job security by virtue of living within commuting distance to a central business district or office park.*

## Atomic claims

### Claim 1

Office-based hiring constrained labor markets to commuting distance, limiting options for specialized workers while protecting some locally advantaged workers.

- Classification: `interpretation`
- Scope: Prevalent office-based knowledge-work labor markets before widespread remote work.
- Date relevance: Interpretation published 2021-08-31.
- Qualification: Remote eligibility, cross-border rules, language, time zones, networks, and employer policy continue to bound labor markets.

### Claim 2

Remote work can redistribute bargaining power unevenly by expanding opportunities for scarce talent while exposing other workers to broader competition.

- Classification: `conceptual_claim`
- Scope: Distributional effects of geographically expanded labor markets.
- Date relevance: Concept articulated 2021-08-31.
- Qualification: Observed wage and employment effects differ by occupation, seniority, location, and organizational policy.
