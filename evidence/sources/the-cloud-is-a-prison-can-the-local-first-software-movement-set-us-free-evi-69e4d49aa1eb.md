## Curated excerpt

> *If you and I wanted to work on a document together, we would no longer have to depend on some Google data center in the Oregon high desert to maintain the master copy. Instead, we would each have copies stored locally on our devices’ hard drives. I could edit my copy offline, and you could edit yours, and the two files would reconcile our changes anytime they connect, whether once a minute or once a week.*

## Atomic claims

### Claim 1

Local-first collaboration stores a copy on each participant’s device and reconciles changes when devices reconnect.

- Classification: `conceptual_claim`
- Scope: Collaborative software architecture.
- Date relevance: Concept articulated 2023-08-03.
- Qualification: Conflict resolution, access control, and multi-device reliability remain difficult.

### Claim 2

Local-first design can reduce dependence on a central cloud provider for the authoritative copy of shared work.

- Classification: `interpretation`
- Scope: Cloud collaboration and data ownership.
- Date relevance: Interpretation published 2023-08-03.
- Qualification: Many implementations still depend on hosted discovery, backup, or synchronization services.
