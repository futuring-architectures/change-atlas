## Curated excerpt

> *Consider AI, and how we describe it with terms like “intelligence”, “creativity”, and even “consciousness”. These are concepts that we barely understand when it comes to describing humans. Our understanding comes more from our lived experience than from precise definitions. Mapping these terms onto AI will always risk confusion, because they can never fully escape their human origins.*

## Atomic claims

### Claim 1

Terms such as intelligence, creativity, and consciousness remain imprecise even when applied to humans.

- Classification: `conceptual_claim`
- Scope: Human concepts used to characterize cognitive and moral capacities.
- Date relevance: Source published 2024-09-20.
- Qualification: Imprecision does not make the concepts useless, but it limits direct measurement and transfer.

### Claim 2

Applying human-origin cognitive concepts to AI inevitably risks confusion because their meanings arise partly from lived human experience.

- Classification: `interpretation`
- Scope: Anthropomorphic language used for AI systems.
- Date relevance: Source published 2024-09-20.
- Qualification: Carefully operationalized definitions can reduce, though not eliminate, the risk.
