## Atomic claims

### Claim 1

The author reports that, across conversations with more than 300 large-company CEOs, CIOs, and CFOs and his firm's implementation work, enterprise AI programs commonly add tools, licenses, tokens, or training to existing processes without producing material organizational change.

- Classification: `observation`
- Scope: Vas's self-reported executive conversations and Varick Agents engagements at enterprises said to range from $500 million to $100 billion in revenue and 1,000 to 100,000 employees.
- Date relevance: Contemporary practitioner account published 2026-09-04.
- Qualification: The source does not identify the organizations, sample selection, interview protocol, outcome distribution, failed and successful denominators, or underlying records; the author is CEO of a firm selling the recommended transformation services.

### Claim 2

The article interprets enterprise AI underperformance as a recurrence of Michael Hammer's 1990 warning about mechanizing old ways of working: making an inherited task faster can leave queues, handoffs, decision rights, incentives, and the end-to-end process unchanged.

- Classification: `interpretation`
- Scope: Enterprise AI point solutions and productivity programs layered onto inherited workflows.
- Date relevance: Historical analogy applied to enterprise AI in September 2026.
- Qualification: Process design is one of several possible causes; model quality, interface design, training, task suitability, data, demand, cost, regulation, and organizational strategy can also limit value. The article cites Hammer but does not provide a bibliography or direct link to the original paper.

### Claim 3

The source reports that a 2025 UK Department for Business and Trade Microsoft 365 Copilot trial issued 1,000 licenses for three months, averaged 1.14 Copilot actions per user per day, found mixed or negative task-quality results and no robust productivity evidence, while 72% of users still reported satisfaction.

- Classification: `observation`
- Scope: One government-department Copilot trial as summarized by the author.
- Date relevance: Reported 2025 trial cited in an article published 2026-09-04.
- Qualification: This is a secondary account that compresses multiple task measures and does not describe study design, comparison conditions, participant selection, confidence intervals, or the distinction between perceived usefulness and measured output. The primary report should be consulted before using the figures comparatively.

### Claim 4

The article uses a historical insurance application that required 17 minutes of touch time across 22 elapsed days and an unnamed client case requiring 25 minutes of work across two days to two weeks to argue that organizational delay often resides in queues and handoffs rather than task execution.

- Classification: `observation`
- Scope: One case attributed to Hammer and one unnamed Varick client process said to be measured from system logs.
- Date relevance: Historical case paired with a contemporary practitioner case in September 2026.
- Qualification: The source supplies no case identifiers, workflow distributions, counterfactuals, or records; the examples demonstrate a possible mechanism rather than the prevalence of queue-dominated delay across enterprises.

### Claim 5

The author predicts that firms which redesign work around AI will gain cost, scale, and outreach advantages over firms that retain headcount-linked processes, creating competitive pressure to transform rather than merely deploy tools.

- Classification: `prediction`
- Scope: Large enterprises and competitors able to use agentic systems in operational workflows.
- Date relevance: Competitive forecast published 2026-09-04 without a specified horizon.
- Qualification: Claims that competitors will “crush” incumbents or scale infinitely are rhetorical extrapolations; the source supplies no comparative firm survival, market-share, headcount, or revenue-per-employee study.

### Claim 6

The article recommends defining a coherent target process before building agents, while rejecting the premise that every system of record must first be consolidated; it proposes an agentic orchestration layer that tolerates disagreement across existing systems.

- Classification: `normative_position`
- Scope: Multi-entity enterprises with heterogeneous ERPs, CRMs, data platforms, and acquired workflows.
- Date relevance: Enterprise architecture recommendation published 2026-09-04.
- Qualification: A single global process can erase legitimate regional, regulatory, product, or customer variation, while an orchestration layer can conceal rather than resolve data semantics, ownership conflicts, and system-control risk. The cited migration failures do not establish that orchestration is generally safer or cheaper.

### Claim 7

The proposed discovery method starts at department-level workflow bundles, maps happy paths, exception volume and cost, upstream and downstream dependencies, systems-of-record authority, organizational variation, touch versus elapsed time, and worker AI capability and ownership, then combines process mining with operator interviews.

- Classification: `normative_position`
- Scope: Discovery and process redesign for enterprise AI initiatives.
- Date relevance: Practitioner playbook published 2026-09-04.
- Qualification: The method is presented as the author's practice rather than evaluated against alternative discovery approaches. Department boundaries may reproduce existing silos, capability scoring is subjective, and interviews require safeguards for candor when redesign may alter employment.

### Claim 8

The article proposes assigning each workflow step to deterministic software, an agent, or a human-in-the-loop path according to whether judgment is required, sufficient outcome-labeled examples exist, consequences are tolerable, and the verification burden justifies automation; human decisions should receive assembled evidence rather than raw search work.

- Classification: `normative_position`
- Scope: Operational enterprise workflows such as invoice matching, conflicts routing, deal-desk approvals, general-ledger coding, and exception handling.
- Date relevance: Automation design proposal published 2026-09-04.
- Qualification: Historical examples do not guarantee future validity, output form is not a complete proxy for action difficulty, and nominal human gates can become rubber stamps or liability sinks. Independent checks, recourse, drift monitoring, and retained skill remain necessary.

### Claim 9

The author recommends baselining workflow KPIs before implementation and prioritizing workflows with many handoffs rather than simply those with the highest transaction volume so that post-deployment value can be compared with a documented starting state.

- Classification: `normative_position`
- Scope: Sequencing and measurement of enterprise agent initiatives.
- Date relevance: Implementation recommendation published 2026-09-04.
- Qualification: Handoff count is not sufficient for priority: consequence, reversibility, data quality, strategic importance, worker and customer effects, maintenance cost, and selection bias also matter. Before-and-after KPIs without a control or external baseline do not isolate agent causality.

### Claim 10

The author reports redesigning a global monthly reconciliation workflow covering more than 300 bank accounts and 12,000 open items from 12 steps into three: statement feeds, deterministic matching plus agent-assembled exceptions, and human review of prepared evidence, eliminating chasing, waiting, re-keying, and regional roll-ups.

- Classification: `observation`
- Scope: One unnamed Varick client reconciliation workflow spanning an offshore team, two other regions, three formats, spreadsheets, and multiple systems of record.
- Date relevance: Self-reported client case published 2026-09-04.
- Qualification: The source does not identify the client, implementation period, error rate, operating cost, retained manual work, worker outcomes, audit results, or independent confirmation. The narrative says the redesign saves weeks but does not provide a post-change cycle-time figure for this specific case.

### Claim 11

The article argues that cross-department process redesign requires executive authority while successful adoption also requires participation, trust, and tacit knowledge from operators whose work will change; current incentives often give workers downside risk without an equivalent reward for success.

- Classification: `conceptual_claim`
- Scope: Large-enterprise AI transformation crossing departmental ownership boundaries.
- Date relevance: Governance and incentive account published 2026-09-04.
- Qualification: Top-down authority can unblock process ownership while suppressing dissent or worker agency. Operator inclusion is not equivalent to decision power, benefit sharing, job security, or meaningful refusal, none of which the source specifies.

### Claim 12

The article interprets the high reported failure rate of 1990s business-process re-engineering as partly a failure to account for its human consequences, then argues that more adaptable AI-native tooling can now absorb coordination work such as chasing, waiting, escalating, and triaging while leaving people with higher-judgment work.

- Classification: `interpretation`
- Scope: Historical business-process re-engineering compared with contemporary agentic process redesign.
- Date relevance: Historical interpretation and present-day claim published 2026-09-04.
- Qualification: The source does not trace the 50–70% estimate or compare contemporary outcomes. Coordination work can carry context, learning, relationship, care, and accountability, and removing it does not ensure that only high-leverage work remains or that displaced workers share the gains.

### Claim 13

The author argues that model-provider changes in pricing, rate limits, quantization, retirement, and behavior make switching costly because organizations must rerun evaluations and retune model use, motivating model-agnostic harnesses rather than dependence on one lab.

- Classification: `interpretation`
- Scope: Enterprise production-agent stacks using proprietary or replaceable models.
- Date relevance: Architecture and vendor-risk interpretation published 2026-09-04.
- Qualification: “Marry their models for life” overstates lock-in, and model-agnostic layers introduce their own abstraction, compatibility, performance, and maintenance costs. The source gives no migration case or comparative total-cost evidence.

### Claim 14

The article recommends multidisciplinary delivery teams combining operating-domain leadership, enterprise software engineering, AI engineering, process redesign, evaluation design, and governance rather than relying on conventional consultants or model labs alone.

- Classification: `normative_position`
- Scope: Vendors and internal teams responsible for enterprise AI transformation.
- Date relevance: Service and capability recommendation published 2026-09-04.
- Qualification: The author sells precisely this combined service and makes broad unsupported claims about competitors and forward-deployed engineers. The source does not compare team compositions, delivery quality, cost, or long-term client independence.

### Claim 15

Across unnamed Varick deployments, the author reports improvements in time, revenue, cost, and risk—including month-end close falling from 18–22 days to 7–9, deal idle time from 23 days to 6, accounts-payable exceptions from 600–800 to below 50, payroll corrections falling 90%, and total client-co-measured value above $100 million.

- Classification: `observation`
- Scope: An unspecified set of Varick deployments, described as roughly half at public companies, across finance, sales, supply chain, marketing, payroll, and employee support.
- Date relevance: Cumulative commercial case claims published 2026-09-04.
- Qualification: Clients, periods, denominators, baselines, cost of implementation, attribution methods, adverse outcomes, selection criteria, and independent audits are absent. The same vendor authored, selected, aggregated, and markets the examples, so the figures are leads for verification rather than generalizable effectiveness evidence.
