## Curated excerpt

> *As a society we have abstracted everything. Work itself is abstracted. We don’t make anything concrete around these parts. We find ways to make and remake money, which has itself been abstracted into the tap of a phone and a signature on a screen \[…\] The question is not about AI and its tools. It is about the culture that created a market for this.*

## Atomic claims

### Claim 1

AI digital twins replace the possibility of reciprocal encounter with an interactive representation assembled from a person's archive.

- Classification: `conceptual_claim`
- Scope: Public use of AI-generated personal replicas.
- Date relevance: Concept articulated 2026-05-26.
- Qualification: A twin can be disclosed and bounded as an interface or reference tool rather than presented as the person or relationship.

### Claim 2

Demand for AI replicas reflects a culture that rewards abstraction, narration, distribution, and scalable pseudo-expertise over accountable practice.

- Classification: `interpretation`
- Scope: The cultural market for digital twins and guru-like expertise.
- Date relevance: Interpretation published 2026-05-26.
- Qualification: The argument is cultural critique, not adoption data; replicas can also support access, memory, translation, and continuity.
