## Curated excerpt

> *Many leaders seem to have adopted a view that the main purpose of technology is efficiency. Following the Law of the Hammer (“to a hammer every problem looks like a nail”), they see AI as a cost-cutting mechanism. Any efficiency gains must be turned into cost savings, even before anyone in the organization figures out what AI is good for.*

## Atomic claims

### Claim 1

Leaders often frame AI primarily as an efficiency and cost-cutting technology before their organizations understand its productive uses.

- Classification: `interpretation`
- Scope: Early organizational adoption of generative AI.
- Date relevance: Interpretation published 2024-06-28.
- Qualification: The excerpt does not establish prevalence, and some organizations pursue learning, quality, growth, or capability alongside efficiency.

### Claim 2

Treating every efficiency gain as an immediate cost saving can suppress the experimentation needed to discover what a new technology is good for.

- Classification: `conceptual_claim`
- Scope: Organizational learning during general-purpose technology adoption.
- Date relevance: Concept articulated 2024-06-28.
- Qualification: Experimentation still requires goals, evaluation, budgets, governance, and eventual decisions about resource allocation.
