## Curated excerpt

> *Reaching for LLMs rather than people when we need a pair programmer, someone to ping pong solutions with, prototype, sketch architectures with, or help answer expert questions about esoteric parts of the codebase. We no longer require onboarding buddies, mentors, or peers; instead, we can talk to machines. With LLMs, avoiding human contact is so easy that it might just become the norm. The future really is bright…*

## Atomic claims

### Claim 1

Programmers may increasingly use LLMs instead of colleagues for pairing, mentoring, architectural discussion, and questions about unfamiliar code.

- Classification: `prediction`
- Scope: Software-development collaboration under widespread LLM use.
- Date relevance: Source date unresolved; this forecast is not eligible for temporal Trend support.

### Claim 2

Cheap machine assistance can make avoidance of human contact a default and thereby weaken onboarding, mentorship, and peer learning.

- Classification: `conceptual_claim`
- Scope: Social effects of AI-assisted software development.
- Date relevance: Source date unresolved.
- Qualification: AI can also lower help-seeking barriers and free colleagues for higher-value interaction; outcomes require observation.
