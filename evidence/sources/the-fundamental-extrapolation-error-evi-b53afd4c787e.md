## Curated excerpt

> *When a person passes a bar exam, or a medical board exam, we think this is meaningful because we know there is a strong correlation between those results and being able to do the work of a lawyer or a doctor. When an LLM passes such an exam, it’s interesting, it’s remarkable, but there is no such correlation. Instead it can do a few fragmentary parts of those jobs very well, and the rest of them not at all.*

## Atomic claims

### Claim 1

Human professional-exam success is meaningful partly because it correlates with ability to perform the corresponding work.

- Classification: `conceptual_claim`
- Scope: Use of licensing examinations as proxies for professional competence.
- Date relevance: Source published 2023-09-29.
- Qualification: The strength of the correlation varies by profession and exam.

### Claim 2

LLM exam success should not be extrapolated to professional competence because the model may perform fragments of the work well and fail at the rest.

- Classification: `interpretation`
- Scope: Evaluation of language models using professional examinations.
- Date relevance: Source published 2023-09-29.
- Qualification: The categorical absence of correlation would require job-level evaluation; the excerpt’s central warning is against unsupported extrapolation.
