## Curated excerpt

> *The broad point here is one we keep having to re-learn: that when creative destruction happens, it’s always easier to see the destruction than the creation. When powerful automation technology appears, it’s easy to foresee that jobs will disappear as rapidly as the technology can spread, and harder to see how humans can add enough value to keep being employed in large numbers.*

## Atomic claims

### Claim 1

Observers see jobs destroyed by automation more easily than they see new work created around it.

- Classification: `conceptual_claim`
- Scope: Public forecasting of creative destruction.
- Date relevance: Concept articulated 2024-06-19.
- Qualification: Visibility asymmetry does not establish that creation will offset destruction.

### Claim 2

Automation will not produce massive unemployment because humans will find new ways to add value.

- Classification: `prediction`
- Scope: Economy-wide employment effects of current automation.
- Date relevance: Forecast published 2024-06-19.
- Qualification: Timing, distribution, wages, transition costs, and policy can make aggregate employment an incomplete metric.
