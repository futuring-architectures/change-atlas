## Atomic claims

### Claim 1

The author reports that forward-deployed engineering has moved from a criticized services-heavy practice toward a common answer for difficult AI deployments, with many seed-stage companies in his network hiring for the role.

- Classification: `observation`
- Scope: The author's conversations with seed-stage AI companies and reported industry hiring activity in 2025–2026.
- Date relevance: Contemporary market account published 2026-08-11.
- Qualification: The source supplies no job-posting dataset, denominator, sampling method, or independent verification; the author leads an enterprise AI company with its own deployment strategy.

### Claim 2

The source interprets Palantir's forward-deployed work as a product-discovery mechanism that converted bespoke field problems into reusable platform primitives, enabling later standardization and reduced custom work.

- Classification: `interpretation`
- Scope: Retrospective account of Palantir's Gotham-to-Foundry product development and deployment model.
- Date relevance: Historical interpretation used as an analogy for 2026 AI startups.
- Qualification: The source relies on selected founder and executive accounts, does not independently establish causality, and may understate other contributors to Palantir's products, margins, sales motion, and government contracting context.

### Claim 3

In a genuinely new AI product category, neither builder nor customer may know the workflow or user journey in advance, making on-site observation and failure discovery part of product definition rather than merely delivery.

- Classification: `conceptual_claim`
- Scope: Early enterprise AI products for workflows without an established human–agent interaction pattern, illustrated with an accounting agent.
- Date relevance: Product-discovery mechanism proposed in August 2026.
- Qualification: Novel technology does not make all surrounding work unknown; accounting rules, controls, responsibilities, and existing processes remain sources of requirements, and field presence is not the only discovery method.

### Claim 4

The author recommends using forward-deployed engineers early for workflow discovery and deliberately reducing their role once recurring user journeys become understood.

- Classification: `normative_position`
- Scope: Early-stage enterprise AI companies building genuinely new product categories.
- Date relevance: Deployment recommendation published 2026-08-11.
- Qualification: Exit timing depends on risk, regulation, customer heterogeneity, integration burden, and product maturity; reducing field staff too early can sever learning and support relationships.

### Claim 5

The source argues that retaining forward-deployed engineers after discovery can defer product-boundary and architectural decisions, keep cost to serve and margins tied to labor, and prevent one deployment from making the next easier.

- Classification: `conceptual_claim`
- Scope: Product companies using bespoke field engineering as a continuing deployment model.
- Date relevance: Business-model mechanism proposed in August 2026.
- Qualification: High-touch service can itself be a viable business model, and heterogeneous or high-stakes environments may contain irreducible work whose recurrence should not be mistaken for product failure.

### Claim 6

The author distinguishes forward-deployed discovery of an unknown workflow from implementation work that executes against a known specification.

- Classification: `conceptual_claim`
- Scope: Enterprise software deployment organizations and role design.
- Date relevance: Role distinction proposed in August 2026.
- Qualification: Discovery and implementation often interact recursively, and a known specification can still expose product assumptions or previously hidden workflow variation.

### Claim 7

The source predicts that model capability will automate much of the implementation and last-mile deployment work formerly performed by human teams, eventually including workflow observation and customer interviews.

- Classification: `prediction`
- Scope: Enterprise AI product implementation and deployment.
- Date relevance: Forward-looking claim published 2026-08-11, with 2023 implementation work as the comparison point.
- Qualification: The source gives no timing, reliability threshold, assurance design, or evidence that automated observation and interviewing can replace situated judgment, trust, accountability, and tacit access.

### Claim 8

The author reports that Decagon's product-driven strategy reduced customer-specific engineering, moved approximately two-thirds of deployment work into its Duet product, and shortened initial AOP launches to a few days on average even for large enterprises.

- Classification: `observation`
- Scope: Founder-reported deployment work at Decagon, including configuration, iteration, and long-tail tuning for large enterprise customers.
- Date relevance: Current company account published 2026-08-11.
- Qualification: The source does not define the measurement period, denominator, AOP, autonomy criteria, baseline, remaining human work, launch-quality outcomes, customer selection, cost, or independent verification.

### Claim 9

The author argues that continuously tuned AI agents require a product-driven deployment model that converts escalations into requirements, enables customer teams, and limits dependence on vendor engineering resources.

- Classification: `normative_position`
- Scope: High-volume, repeatable, decomposable enterprise customer-service workflows.
- Date relevance: Product and deployment recommendation published 2026-08-11.
- Qualification: Product ownership by the vendor and giving customers operational keys do not by themselves establish portability, data sovereignty, switching ability, or freedom from model and platform dependence.

### Claim 10

The source proposes evaluating field deployment by asking whether bespoke work reflects customer-environment variation or product gaps, whether the last mile is irreducible or merely unbuilt, and what reusable product was created from the previous engagement.

- Classification: `conceptual_claim`
- Scope: Enterprise AI product organizations using forward-deployed engineers.
- Date relevance: Evaluation test proposed in August 2026.
- Qualification: The questions create a learning discipline but do not specify thresholds, accounting methods, worker and customer outcomes, or how to govern legitimate exceptions.
