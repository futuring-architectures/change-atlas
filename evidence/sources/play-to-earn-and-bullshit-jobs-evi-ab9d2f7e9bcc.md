## Curated excerpt

> *Ultimately, in-game labour is just a re-branding of gameplay designed to be dull enough that rich players will pay to outsource it to poor players. In spite of being presented as the future of work by some venture capitalists, the incentives just don’t make sense. Floors don’t have to be swept in the metaverse unless they’re designed to need sweeping.*

## Atomic claims

### Claim 1

The source interprets some in-game labor as gameplay deliberately made dull enough for wealthier players to outsource to poorer players.

- Classification: `interpretation`
- Scope: Play-to-earn games where repetitive game tasks can be delegated for payment.
- Date relevance: Source published 2021-12-28.
- Qualification: The claim applies to the criticized design pattern, not necessarily to all compensated play or all play-to-earn games.

### Claim 2

The future-of-work case for play-to-earn is undermined when compensated chores exist only because game design creates them.

- Classification: `interpretation`
- Scope: Claims that play-to-earn game activity represents desirable future employment.
- Date relevance: Source published 2021-12-28.
- Qualification: The critique depends on whether the work produces independent value or merely services an intentionally imposed game constraint.
