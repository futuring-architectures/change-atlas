# Intelligence-to-productivity pathways

## Purpose

Use this Framework when a capability forecast moves directly from “more intelligent” models to economic or scientific transformation. It asks what information is measurable, which machine-specific advantage is doing the work, what complementary system converts it into outcomes, and where the next constraint appears.

The Framework turns one author's three-part forecast into an auditable map. It does not establish that intelligence has reached a ceiling or that any pathway will deliver the predicted gains.

## Boundary: the measurable-information frontier

Before assigning value to more inference or compute, identify:

- what phenomenon is being measured and at what resolution, frequency, and cost;
- which relevant state remains unobserved, latent, private, tacit, or inaccessible;
- what uncertainty comes from finite samples, stochasticity, chaos, changing behavior, or unclear objectives;
- whether new experiments, sensors, simulations, or institutional access can create additional information;
- which decisions require explanation or causal understanding rather than predictive correlation alone.

Noah Smith's hypothesis treats finite measurement and recoverable information as a possible bound on intelligence: [[what-will-more-intelligence-actually-do-for-us-evi-6d1521adbc8d#Claim 3|What will more intelligence do? — claim 3]]. [[what-will-more-intelligence-actually-do-for-us-evi-6d1521adbc8d#Claim 4|Claim 4]] adds stochastic and chaotic limits. These are boundary hypotheses, not proof of a universal ceiling.

## Pathway 1: replicate cognition

Value comes from running many capable processes quickly and in parallel rather than making one process incomparably wiser than a person.

Trace:

1. the bounded cognitive unit being replicated;
2. compute, energy, memory, latency, and coordination cost per useful result;
3. verification, integration, exception, and demand capacity;
4. physical actuation required through devices, robots, logistics, or people;
5. ownership of the capital and distribution of gains and risks.

The source predicts productivity through parallel agents and robotics: [[what-will-more-intelligence-actually-do-for-us-evi-6d1521adbc8d#Claim 5|claim 5]] and [[what-will-more-intelligence-actually-do-for-us-evi-6d1521adbc8d#Claim 6|claim 6]]. More generated cognitive work is an input, not the outcome measure.

## Pathway 2: instrument tacit practice

Value comes from observing distributed work at higher resolution, finding recurring process differences, and accelerating experiments that participants could not fully articulate individually.

Trace:

1. where practical knowledge resides across workers, tools, materials, routines, suppliers, and relationships;
2. what sensors capture and what contextual meaning they omit;
3. who consents to collection and who owns the resulting data and models;
4. how proposed improvements are tested, adopted, rejected, and locally revised;
5. whether diffusion preserves learning and agency or converts workers into extractive data sources;
6. whether faster diffusion improves competition or strengthens the actor controlling the instrumentation layer.

[[what-will-more-intelligence-actually-do-for-us-evi-6d1521adbc8d#Claim 7|Claim 7]] supplies distributed tacit knowledge; [[what-will-more-intelligence-actually-do-for-us-evi-6d1521adbc8d#Claim 8|claim 8]] and [[what-will-more-intelligence-actually-do-for-us-evi-6d1521adbc8d#Claim 9|claim 9]] supply the instrumentation, diffusion, and excludability hypotheses.

## Pathway 3: exploit complex regularities

Value comes from finding patterns too diffuse to compress into simple human-readable laws but stable enough to predict or control under specified conditions.

Trace:

1. the domain, distribution, intervention, and success criterion;
2. evidence that the pattern generalizes beyond training and retrospective fit;
3. independent tests, uncertainty estimates, monitoring, and stop conditions;
4. whether explanation is required for scientific understanding, legitimacy, debugging, or safety;
5. who can inspect, challenge, reproduce, and govern an otherwise opaque regularity.

Smith calls these patterns “cloud laws”: [[what-will-more-intelligence-actually-do-for-us-evi-6d1521adbc8d#Claim 10|claim 10]]. [[what-will-more-intelligence-actually-do-for-us-evi-6d1521adbc8d#Claim 11|Claim 11]] predicts scientific reach beyond human communication bottlenecks, while leaving measurement and verification intact as dependencies.

## Conversion layer: from capability to outcomes

For every pathway, map the complementary work between technical performance and productivity:

- workflow and organizational redesign;
- infrastructure, energy, sensors, robotics, and supply chains;
- domain knowledge, evaluation, maintenance, and recovery;
- demand, capital allocation, market structure, rights, and legitimacy;
- environmental and distributional consequences;
- the time between capability availability, adoption, learning, and aggregate measurement.

The source's own opening observation—rapid capability gains with limited broad transformation by mid-2026—shows why this conversion layer cannot be skipped: [[what-will-more-intelligence-actually-do-for-us-evi-6d1521adbc8d#Claim 2|claim 2]].

## Decision record

For a forecast or investment, record:

1. the outcome in scope and its baseline;
2. the measurable-information frontier and sources of irreducible uncertainty;
3. the claimed pathway or combination of pathways;
4. the machine-specific advantage beyond head-to-head task intelligence;
5. complementary assets, people, institutions, and physical systems;
6. migrated bottlenecks and total-system costs;
7. owners of data, models, instruments, capital, decisions, and surplus;
8. verification, explanation, recourse, and failure conditions;
9. evidence and date at which the forecast should be revised.

## Applicability and limitations

- The source is a 2026 synthesis and forecast, not a comparative productivity study.
- “Intelligence” is defined narrowly as information extraction for part of the argument; planning, action, objective formation, social coordination, embodiment, and scientific instrumentation remain distinct.
- Finite current measurement does not imply permanently finite data; agents can design experiments and institutions can enable or block access.
- Replicability can multiply errors, low-value output, energy use, and concentration as well as useful work.
- Instrumenting tacit practice may aid learning or enable surveillance and extraction.
- Opaque regularities may be exploitable without being robust, causal, safe, or publicly legitimate.
- Aggregate productivity statistics can lag, miss quality changes, or conceal sectoral and distributional effects.
