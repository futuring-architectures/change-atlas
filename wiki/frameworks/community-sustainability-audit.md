# Community sustainability audit

## Purpose

Use this Framework to test whether a creator community converts participation into durable mutual capability or quietly consumes its creative core.

## The six checks

1. **Contribution:** What do members supply—work, money, data, taste, care, reputation, or distribution?
2. **Attention load:** Who reviews, answers, moderates, teaches, and absorbs conflict? Where does demand exceed renewable capacity?
3. **Decision rights:** Who sets boundaries, rules, priorities, and membership conditions? Can contributors contest decisions?
4. **Rewards and ownership:** Who receives income, status, access, upside, and credit? Does ownership follow capital, labor, or participation?
5. **Care and continuity:** Which practices prevent burnout, preserve knowledge, develop newcomers, and maintain quality?
6. **Exit and recourse:** Can people leave with their identity, work, relationships, and value? What remedy exists for abuse or exclusion?

## How to apply it

Map each check for creators, maintainers, contributors, audience members, platform owners, and sponsors. Identify transfers where one party's “engagement” is another's unpaid workload. Reassess after growth, monetization, or governance changes.

## Limitations

- The audit does not assume maximum participation is desirable.
- Formal ownership does not guarantee usable voice or fair distribution.
- Strong boundaries can sustain a community or reproduce exclusion.
- Community health needs participant-defined outcomes, not retention alone.
