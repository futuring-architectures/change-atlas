# 0–50–200 productivity allocation

## Purpose

Use this Framework after a credible AI capability gain is identified and before treating one organizational response as inevitable. It makes explicit how the productivity dividend is allocated among unchanged operations, lower labor cost, expanded output, workers, customers, owners, and other affected parties.

The labels are mnemonic rather than comparable measurements: **0%** refers to organizational change, **50%** to a stylized cost level, and **200%** to a stylized output level.

## Preconditions

Before choosing a posture:

1. Verify the capability gain across the full workflow, including quality, verification, integration, exceptions, maintenance, and coordination.
2. Separate task speed from worker, team, firm, and customer outcomes.
3. Map whose work changes, who bears risk, and who can influence the allocation.
4. Treat any multiplier as a scenario variable, not an established universal effect.

## The three postures

### 0%: leave the organization unchanged

The organization neither equips workers nor redesigns work around the new capability. Individual adoption may still occur privately, producing uneven access, hidden practices, and locally captured benefits.

Ask:

- Which employees are already experimenting, and why is their use hidden?
- What learning, security, quality, or coordination is lost when adoption stays private?
- Does “no change” actually preserve the baseline, or allow an informal operating model to emerge?

### 50%: hold ambition constant and reduce cost

The organization seeks the same goals with fewer workers or lower labor expense. Owners may capture the most visible savings while displaced workers, remaining employees, customers, or downstream reviewers absorb other consequences.

Ask:

- Are savings real after integration, rework, retained expertise, transition, and risk?
- Which capabilities or entry paths disappear with headcount?
- Who receives the productivity dividend, and who has recourse?

### 200%: retain capability and expand ambition

The organization reinvests greater human–AI capability in more or better products, service, quality, responsiveness, experimentation, or market reach.

Ask:

- Is there demand and a credible revenue or public-value path for additional output?
- Does growth mean useful betterment or merely greater volume and work intensity?
- How are gains shared with the people whose skills and adaptation produced them?

## Decision record

For the selected posture, record:

- the verified productivity premise and its uncertainty;
- the ambition, demand, and outcome target;
- expected headcount and role effects;
- total workflow costs and new bottlenecks;
- distribution of gains, risks, authority, and learning;
- employee participation and the signal leadership is sending;
- review dates and conditions for changing posture.

Hybrid allocations are expected. An organization can reduce low-value work, invest in quality, shorten hours, lower prices, raise compensation, expand output, and preserve slack in different proportions. The Framework prevents those choices from being collapsed into a binary “adopt AI or not.”

## Limitations

- The source is a strategic thought experiment, not a comparative company study.
- Output, cost, revenue, quality, employment, and human flourishing are different denominators.
- Growth can intensify extraction or ecological burden rather than distribute benefit.
- Cost reduction can be necessary for survival, while retained headcount does not guarantee good work.
- Announced ambition can become rhetoric unless budgets, decision rights, measures, and employment practices implement it.
