# Recursive form-finding loop

## Purpose

Use this Framework when an inquiry begins with a hunch, tension, or partly tacit structure rather than a fully specifiable deliverable. It treats model output as material for judgment, not as an answer to accept or merely polish.

## The loop

1. **Externalize the hunch:** state the fragment, tension, causal relation, or provisional skeleton without pretending the thesis is complete.
2. **Generate a provisional form:** ask the model to make relationships visible enough to inspect.
3. **Read diagnostically:** identify recognition and dissonance—what feels faithful, flattened, falsely resolved, misplaced, or absent.
4. **Apply resistance:** test causal leaps, hidden assumptions, category errors, exclusions, and the norms governing the audit itself. Use the model against the human narrative as well as human judgment against model gravity.
5. **Rotate the object:** change scale, genre, audience, representation, or domain to reveal different contours and obligations.
6. **Recompose and compress:** integrate what survived, remove temporary scaffolding, and require the argument to carry its own weight.
7. **Stabilize provisionally:** release the form for its present purpose while retaining the judgments, rejected alternatives, and open tensions that make later challenge possible.

The stabilized output returns as material for another cycle. The unit of work is the evolving object plus its sequence of judgments, not one prompt and answer.

## Diagnostic prompts

- What did this version reveal about the inquiry rather than merely about its prose?
- Which tension disappeared when the output became fluent?
- What inherited category or standard is pulling the idea toward a familiar answer?
- Which counterforce would test the human's preferred narrative?
- What becomes visible in another genre, scale, or representation?
- Which assumptions, disagreements, and closure decisions must remain inspectable?
- Who holds the stakes and responsibility for letting this form become consequential?

## Applicability and limitations

- The loop fits exploratory conceptual work better than tasks with a fixed, testable target.
- Recognition can reflect taste and tacit expertise, but also bias, motivated reasoning, or attachment to novelty.
- Recursive generation can create motion without progress; explicit stopping conditions and external evidence remain necessary.
- Preserving every revision can overwhelm collaborators or expose sensitive reasoning. Process legibility should be proportionate to consequence.
- The Framework derives from one practitioner's reflective account and remains a candidate until tested across users, models, and work settings.
