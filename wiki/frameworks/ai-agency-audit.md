# AI agency audit

## Purpose

Use this audit whenever a product claims to democratize expertise, automate work, or let people build for themselves.

## Six dimensions

| Dimension | Agency test | Failure pattern |
|---|---|---|
| **Access** | Who can use the capability at acceptable cost and risk? | A demo is universalized while real access remains narrow. |
| **Control** | Can participants inspect, shape, stop, export, and reverse the system? | Users supply prompts but providers retain consequential control. |
| **Capability** | Do participants gain transferable understanding and judgment? | Output rises while skill, apprenticeship, or problem comprehension falls. |
| **Work** | Where do review, exception handling, data preparation, and care move? | “Automation” hides outsourced or downstream human labor. |
| **Capture** | Who owns the infrastructure, data, audience relation, and surplus? | More people create while an intermediary captures the aggregate value. |
| **Recourse** | Who can challenge errors, exclusions, surveillance, or harm? | Responsibility is assigned to users without authority or remedy. |

## How to apply it

Map each participant—including hidden workers and affected nonusers—across all six dimensions before and after adoption. Record both the capability gain and the institutional terms. A system distributes agency only when improvement is not confined to access or output.

## Limitations

- Agency is plural: one group’s control can constrain another’s.
- Some high-risk domains appropriately restrict user programmability.
- The audit identifies distribution questions; it does not prescribe one governance form.
