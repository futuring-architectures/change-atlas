# Culture influence stack

## Purpose

Use this Framework to inspect the levers through which leaders influence culture without treating culture as a directly installable artifact.

## The six layers

### 1. Enacted example

What do powerful people repeatedly do, especially when values conflict with deadlines, revenue, status, or risk?

### 2. Incentives and sanctions

Which behavior receives promotion, pay, attention, protection, or punishment? Include informal rewards and career penalties.

### 3. Selection and exit

Who is hired, included, heard, marginalized, promoted, or driven out? Track whether dissent and difference survive contact with the system.

### 4. Resources and autonomy

Who receives time, tools, information, decision rights, and room to experiment? Declared priorities without resources are weak cultural signals.

### 5. Amplification and repetition

Which local behaviors are copied, standardized, celebrated, or allowed to compound? Small decisions become path-dependent routines here.

### 6. Narrative and interpretation

Which story explains the work, its beneficiaries, and its trade-offs? Test whether the narrative clarifies operating reality or accumulates debt by concealing contradictions.

## How to apply it

1. Choose a consequential decision rather than a values statement.
2. Record evidence at all six layers from multiple participant positions.
3. Compare the declared culture with the behavior the stack actually reinforces.
4. Identify reinforcing loops and contradictions between layers.
5. Change a leverage point, observe resulting behavior, and revisit the audit.

## Applicability and limitations

- The stack diagnoses influence; it does not promise control or cultural homogeneity.
- A layer can contain multiple competing mechanisms and subcultures.
- Strong alignment can support shared purpose or suppress pluralism and dissent.
- Outcomes should be assessed for workers, customers, partners, and affected communities, not only leaders.
