# Agency rupture cycle

## Purpose

Use this Framework to diagnose how a person or group is interpreting an AI capability that suddenly performs work central to its identity. It separates the visibility of model output from the less visible human work around it, then asks whether authorship and agency are being reconstructed around a changed practice.

## Structure

### 1. Initial agency rupture

The model performs something previously associated with the person's effort, expertise, or identity. Attention centers on the model, while prompting, problem selection, context, evaluation, integration, and other human contributions become hard to see. The result may be described as something “AI did,” and the change can feel like personal redundancy rather than a narrower task transition.

### 2. Seeing human scaffolding

Repeated use exposes capability boundaries and the work required before, around, and after generation. The person can now see the context, guidance, verification, repair, and responsibility that make an output useful, but may still regard this work as secondary to the model's visible production.

### 3. Agency reconstruction

Direction, judgment, evaluation, integration, and closure become understood as the substantive practice rather than auxiliary scaffolding. The model recedes into an ordinary tool for accelerating repetitive production, authorship recenters on the human-tool system, and expectations of quality can rise. A later capability jump may restart the cycle.

## How to apply it

1. Name the capability change and the task, status, or identity it disturbs.
2. Record attribution language: who or what is said to have done the work?
3. Map the visible and hidden human scaffolding around the output.
4. Test whether the newly central human work carries meaningful judgment, authority, learning, and accountability.
5. Distinguish reconstructed agency from mere familiarity, dependence, or concealed labor.
6. Revisit the diagnosis when model capability or organizational conditions change.

## Worked example

A software practitioner first experiences generated implementation as evidence that “AI built the feature.” With use, they notice that framing the problem, supplying repository context, testing behavior, diagnosing failures, and deciding what counts as complete determine the result. Agency reconstruction occurs only if those activities are recognized and supported as meaningful work—not simply if the practitioner becomes accustomed to an opaque system or remains responsible without control.

## Applicability and limitations

- The cycle is a practitioner hypothesis, not a validated psychological stage model.
- Stages may overlap, reverse, recur, or differ across people and institutions.
- Subjective agency can recover while income, status, bargaining power, skill, or decision rights still decline.
- Re-centering the user can hide data labor, infrastructure, model-provider power, and contributions made elsewhere in the system.
- Calling AI a tool does not settle whether the surrounding system is reliable, governable, or part of an [[extended-mind|extended cognitive system]].
- The framework diagnoses adaptation; it does not imply that every rupture should be accepted or metabolized.
