# Organizational transformation test

## Purpose

Use this Framework to test whether a change described as transformation alters the organization's operating reality or only its formal arrangement. It is an evidence checklist, not a maturity score or guarantee of success.

## The five tests

### 1. Constraint and value logic

What changed in the environment, and how has the organization reinterpreted the value it creates? A transformation should name the obsolete assumption or changed constraint, not only a growth target.

### 2. Work design

What do people, software, partners, and customers now do differently? Look for changed workflows, decision units, interfaces, and division of labor rather than renamed functions.

### 3. Authority and boundaries

Who can decide, allocate resources, set standards, and stop work after the change? Test whether authority, autonomy, and the boundary between inside and outside the firm actually moved.

### 4. Operating assumptions and mindset

Which inherited beliefs, incentives, and measures no longer govern? A new org chart operating under the old success logic is reorganization, not transformation.

### 5. Learning and evidence

What observations would show that the new model works, fails, or needs correction? Transformation requires feedback and adaptation, not an announcement treated as completion.

## How to apply it

1. Record the public claim and the baseline organization.
2. For each test, distinguish **announced**, **implemented**, and **observed in outcomes**.
3. Identify whose perspective supplies the evidence: leadership, workers, partners, customers, or affected communities.
4. Search for unchanged incentives and shadow processes that preserve the prior model.
5. Reassess after enough time for operating consequences to appear.

## Worked example

A conglomerate breakup passes a legal-boundary test but may fail the Framework if work, authority, incentives, and learning remain unchanged. A platform transition may leave the legal firm intact while changing value creation, participant roles, interfaces, and governance; it can therefore be more transformational than a visible breakup.

## Applicability and limitations

- The Framework distinguishes depth of change; it does not judge whether the change is desirable.
- Mindset is difficult to observe and should not become an unfalsifiable explanation.
- Some transformations are emergent and become legible only retrospectively.
- Passing all five tests does not establish fair distribution, resilience, or improved performance.
