# Intent-to-accountability loop

## Purpose

Use this Framework when a person or team directs an AI system to produce work that will affect other people, operations, or decisions.

## The loop

1. **Frame:** state the problem, purpose, affected parties, and what must not be optimized away.
2. **Contextualize:** assemble relevant history, data, examples, exceptions, and decision rights.
3. **Generate:** produce multiple bounded options rather than mistake the first plausible answer for completion.
4. **Select:** apply explicit criteria, taste, and tradeoffs; record why one path deserves commitment.
5. **Verify:** test claims, behavior, provenance, edge cases, and failure consequences with methods suited to the domain.
6. **Integrate:** connect the result to real workflows, dependencies, maintenance, and human coordination.
7. **Own:** name who can approve, stop, correct, and answer for the outcome.

The output of each cycle becomes context for the next. Discovery during verification or integration can force reframing.

## Diagnostic prompts

- Whose intent is executable, and whose needs are only inferred?
- Which context is absent because it is tacit, contested, or expensive to collect?
- What makes an alternative selectable rather than merely plausible?
- Can verification keep pace with generation?
- Does the accountable person have matching authority, skill, time, and recourse?

## Limitations

- The loop can become bureaucratic for low-stakes, reversible tasks.
- Explicit context can never capture an entire situation.
- Verification can reproduce institutional bias.
- Naming an owner does not distribute voice to everyone affected.
