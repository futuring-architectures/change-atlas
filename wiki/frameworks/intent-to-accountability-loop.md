# Intent-to-accountability loop

## Purpose

Use this Framework when a person or team directs an AI system to produce work that will affect other people, operations, or decisions.

## The loop

1. **Frame:** before asking the model for an answer, state the problem, purpose, affected parties, what must not be optimized away, and the person's or team's provisional belief. Preserve a genuine prior without pretending certainty.
2. **Contextualize:** assemble relevant history, data, examples, exceptions, and decision rights.
3. **Generate:** produce multiple bounded options rather than mistake the first plausible answer for completion.
4. **Select:** apply explicit criteria, taste, and tradeoffs; record why one path deserves commitment, what is expected to happen, and what observable result would revise the view.
5. **Verify:** test claims, behavior, provenance, edge cases, and failure consequences with methods suited to the domain.
6. **Integrate:** connect the result to real workflows, dependencies, maintenance, and human coordination.
7. **Own:** name who can approve, stop, correct, and answer for the outcome.
8. **Reconcile:** after an appropriate outcome window, compare what happened with the pre-assistance belief and selected prediction. Record the gap, update the weighting of evidence, and carry the learning into the next frame.

The output and reconciliation record of each cycle become context for the next. Discovery during verification, integration, or consequence can force reframing.

## Diagnostic prompts

- Whose intent is executable, and whose needs are only inferred?
- What did the person or team believe before the model supplied an answer?
- Which context is absent because it is tacit, contested, or expensive to collect?
- What makes an alternative selectable rather than merely plausible?
- What outcome is expected, what would count against the view, and who will return to compare?
- Can verification keep pace with generation?
- Does the accountable person have matching authority, skill, time, and recourse?

## Limitations

- The loop can become bureaucratic for low-stakes, reversible tasks.
- Explicit context can never capture an entire situation.
- Verification can reproduce institutional bias.
- Naming an owner does not distribute voice to everyone affected.
- Recording a prior can improve learning or harden anchoring; the loop requires permission to revise and should not reward theatrical certainty.
- Real consequences are not always an ethical training environment. High-stakes, irreversible, or imposed risks require simulations, staged exposure, safeguards, and affected-party governance.
