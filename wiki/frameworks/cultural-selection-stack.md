# Cultural selection stack

## Purpose

Use this Framework to explain why one idea, style, creator, behavior, or technology travels while another disappears. It prevents “culture,” “the algorithm,” “the market,” or “the audience” from becoming a single-cause explanation. The stack follows cultural variation through six selection layers and makes the distribution of attention, legitimacy, and reward visible.

## Structure

### 1. Generation

What variation can enter the system? Inspect who can create, which tools and resources are available, what permission is required, and whether the system favors professional production, participation, remix, or automated abundance.

### 2. Legibility

What can the system recognize and compare? Examine formats, metadata, categories, metrics, identity, provenance, stylistic conventions, and the compressibility of an idea into a meme, story, score, or profile.

### 3. Allocation

Who or what grants exposure? Distinguish professional gatekeeping, explicit subscription, search, social propagation, recommendation, paid distribution, institutional status, and user-controlled filtering.

### 4. Reinforcement

Which responses change future reach or behavior? Trace clicks, shares, purchases, imitation, citations, reputation, token prices, sanctions, and the positive or negative feedback loops attached to them.

### 5. Transmission and retention

What survives across people, contexts, and time? Inspect imitation, teaching, archives, standards, stories, compatibility, tacit knowledge, and dependence on devices, platforms, or specialist maintainers.

### 6. Capture and consequence

Who receives attention, money, status, control, learning, or risk? Record not only what is selected but who owns the channel, who performs hidden work, who can appeal, and whether value accumulates with creators, participants, platforms, capital owners, or publics.

The six-layer stack is an Atlas synthesis. The sources separately establish selection for memeability, narrative soft power, gatekeeper and engagement allocation, audience demand, automation distribution, and the preservation problem. The exact layering is a diagnostic organization of those mechanisms.

## How to apply it

1. Name the bounded cultural outcome without assigning a cause.
2. Describe the current mechanism at each layer.
3. Identify which attributes become legible and which disappear.
4. Trace the feedback loop from allocation through reinforcement back to generation.
5. Compare intended selection criteria with observed rewards.
6. Map who captures gains and who bears hidden labor or risk.
7. Specify a counter-case that would disconfirm the proposed mechanism.

## Worked example

For a blockchain-linked meme, creation may be permissionless; a simple and visible idea is more legible; market and social channels allocate exposure; purchases and sharing reinforce one another; the meme transmits more readily than a complex proposition; early creators and buyers may capture gains while later buyers bear downside. Calling the outcome “hype” would miss the distinct layers that produce it.

## Applicability and limitations

- The Framework diagnoses selection; it does not assume that popular, profitable, truthful, or socially useful are the same criterion.
- Layers interact and can be controlled by different actors.
- Cultural outcomes also depend on history, identity, law, material infrastructure, and events outside the selected system.
- A system can amplify variance at generation while concentrating allocation or capture.
- The stack does not provide a universal standard of legitimate selection; those values must be stated and contested.
