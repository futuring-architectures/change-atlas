# Bubble–crash–rebuild map

## Purpose

Use this Framework when a technology boom is overbuilding capacity or when leaders are treating a correction as either pure destruction or automatic renewal. It separates the assets that may persist from the financial repricing, the institutional response, the distribution of productive power, and the people who absorb the loss.

The map is diagnostic. It does not forecast a crash or endorse one as necessary.

## The map

### 1. Identify the installed residue

Inventory what could remain useful if valuations, vendors, or hardware generations change:

- physical capacity such as energy, grid access, buildings, networks, and logistics;
- technical capacity such as models, standards, tools, interfaces, and interoperable components;
- human capacity such as practiced workflows, maintenance knowledge, communities, and judgment;
- institutional capacity such as procurement paths, safeguards, rights, and coordinating bodies.

Separate genuinely reusable capacity from stranded assets, locked dependencies, ecological liabilities, and habits whose value exists only under boom conditions. Pande's durable-residue thesis is a prediction about AI, not yet an observed outcome: [[let-it-crash-how-to-steer-what-comes-after-evi-46e9cb77dc3d#Claim 2|bubble-built infrastructure may outlive failed investors]] and [[let-it-crash-how-to-steer-what-comes-after-evi-46e9cb77dc3d#Claim 3|the proposed durable AI residue]].

### 2. Separate repricing from prolonged breakdown

Distinguish the moment financial expectations reset from the duration and distribution of the downturn that follows. Record:

- which paper values changed;
- which operating capabilities still function;
- which households, workers, firms, and public services lose income or access;
- which recovery levers depend on state, market, community, or international capacity.

The source treats institutional response as a variable between crash and depression: [[let-it-crash-how-to-steer-what-comes-after-evi-46e9cb77dc3d#Claim 6|rebuild capacity and direction shape the downturn]].

### 3. Detect the institutional window

Map which governance questions were deferred during the boom and which become newly actionable after it: competition, interoperability, standards, financial rules, social protection, ownership, and recourse. Do not assume crisis produces reform. Test whether concentrated actors, weak state capacity, emergency politics, or public distrust could close or capture the window instead.

This preserves the source's proposed opening while making capture a failure condition: [[let-it-crash-how-to-steer-what-comes-after-evi-46e9cb77dc3d#Claim 5|a correction can open blocked institutional choices]].

### 4. Audit what is already on the shelf

List the plans, institutions, narratives, leaders, coalitions, and technical alternatives ready to operate under pressure. For each, ask:

- What account of human purpose and public value does it encode?
- Who authored it, and who can contest or revise it?
- What evidence or event would activate it?
- Which futures does belief in it authorize or exclude?
- Can multiple legitimate alternatives remain available?

This step operationalizes the temporal mechanism in [[future-narratives-allocate-permission|Future narratives allocate permission]]: slow-built cultural and institutional alternatives may become the specifications used in a fast-moving rebuild.

### 5. Choose how means and proceeds are distributed

Treat access, ownership, governance, income support, public investment, taxation, and bargaining power as distinct levers. “Spread the means” and “redistribute the proceeds” are not mutually exclusive. Test whether formal access to models or compute creates usable capability, whether ownership includes decision rights, and how reconcentration is prevented.

Pande advocates distributed productive assets: [[let-it-crash-how-to-steer-what-comes-after-evi-46e9cb77dc3d#Claim 8|local models, compute, tools, and equity]]. The map turns that position into questions rather than accepting the distributional result.

### 6. Assign readiness before urgency

Name who is responsible for:

- guardrails, competition, and a survivable floor;
- broad, affordable, interoperable technical access;
- practical exit, portability, and recourse;
- plural visions of a worthwhile life and work;
- ownership, learning, and maintenance capacity that persist after the opening.

Record the budget, authority, trigger, handoff, and review condition for each responsibility. The source's proposed government–company–civil-society allocation is one starting configuration, not a universal division of labor: [[let-it-crash-how-to-steer-what-comes-after-evi-46e9cb77dc3d#Claim 9|the proposed role allocation]] and [[let-it-crash-how-to-steer-what-comes-after-evi-46e9cb77dc3d#Claim 10|the pre-crisis preparation argument]].

### 7. Keep a harm ledger and counterfactual

Track lost jobs, savings, firms, output, public revenue, learning paths, and regional effects alongside reusable infrastructure and later gains. Compare the proposed correction with plausible alternatives: gradual reform, managed investment slowdown, pre-emptive standards, or no intervention. A later benefit does not retroactively make concentrated losses fair or necessary.

The source itself supplies the limiting evidence: [[let-it-crash-how-to-steer-what-comes-after-evi-46e9cb77dc3d#Claim 11|a crash can scar without producing a golden age]].

## Decision record

For each use, record:

1. the boom or transition in scope and the evidence that a correction is plausible;
2. durable, depreciating, stranded, and harmful residues;
3. the difference between financial repricing and operating breakdown;
4. reforms and actors likely to open, block, or capture the institutional window;
5. the alternatives already prepared and the values they encode;
6. distribution of access, ownership, income, decision rights, risk, and recourse;
7. readiness owners, triggers, budgets, and review dates;
8. harms, counterfactuals, and evidence that would revise the map.

## Applicability and limitations

- The principal source is a venture capitalist's 2026 essay drawing on Carlota Perez; it is not a comparative historical study.
- Historical railway and fiber analogies do not establish that AI has the same asset lives, demand pattern, energy footprint, labor effects, or institutional trajectory.
- Overinvestment can create public capacity, stranded assets, monopoly control, ecological costs, or all four at once.
- Crisis can enable reform, authoritarian capture, delay, or institutional collapse.
- Preparing for one forecast can crowd out other risks and can make a predicted crash appear more inevitable than the evidence warrants.
- Distributed access and ownership do not by themselves produce competence, voice, fair value capture, safety, or self-determination.
