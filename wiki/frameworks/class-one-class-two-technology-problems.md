# Class 1 / Class 2 technology problems

## Purpose

Use the framework to ask whether a technology's central challenge is still access and technical effectiveness, or whether successful diffusion has produced system-level social consequences that require a different response portfolio.

## Structure

### Class 1: insufficient access or performance

The technology is unavailable to many people or does not work well enough. Typical responses improve capability, reliability, distribution, or market provision.

### Class 2: consequences of ubiquity and effectiveness

The technology is widely available and works well, but its success creates systemic challenges. The source proposes extra-market levers including cultural norms, regulation, education, and reframing.

### Transition

The key shift is from “not everyone has a technology that does not work very well” to “everyone has a technology that works very well.” The classes can overlap; the Evidence does not establish a clean threshold or inevitable sequence.

## How to apply it

1. Define the technology and population rather than treating “technology” as a single domain.
2. Test whether access and functional performance remain binding constraints.
3. Identify harms or coordination problems produced specifically by broad, effective use.
4. Separate improvements to the product or market from extra-market interventions.
5. Assemble a response portfolio across norms, regulation, education, and problem framing.
6. Recheck for mixed cases: a Class 2 problem for one population may coexist with a Class 1 problem for another.

Steps 1–6 are an Atlas operationalization of the source's distinction. They should be treated as a candidate application method, not attributed directly to Kevin Kelly.

## Worked example

For a mature online platform, increasing access or improving recommendation accuracy may solve a Class 1 problem while worsening a Class 2 problem such as concentration, information overload, or social externalities. The framework redirects attention from product performance alone toward institutional and cultural responses.

This example is illustrative; the current Rules Evidence does not establish its outcome empirically.

## Applicability and limitations

- The framework is most useful when technical success and diffusion are themselves part of the problem.
- “Works very well” depends on whose objective is measured; provider performance and public value may differ.
- The source lists extra-market levers but does not specify how to choose or combine them.
- The interpretation that slow, soft responses are under-attended is not independently measured.
