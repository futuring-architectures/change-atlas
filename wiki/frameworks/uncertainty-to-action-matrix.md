# Uncertainty-to-action matrix

## Purpose

Use the matrix to choose an action posture when evidence is incomplete.

| | **Reversible / contained** | **Irreversible / system-wide** |
|---|---|---|
| **Evidence comparatively strong** | Deploy with monitoring and explicit stopping conditions. | Stage deployment, require independent review, recourse, and accountable authorization. |
| **Evidence weak or mechanisms contested** | Run probes that maximize learning and preserve alternatives. | Delay scale, build evidence, reduce exposure, and invest in safer substitutes. |

## Five checks

1. **Reversibility:** Can effects, data flows, dependencies, and rights actually be restored?
2. **Exposure:** Who is involuntarily included in the experiment?
3. **Observability:** Will failure become visible before it compounds?
4. **Recourse:** Can affected parties stop, contest, and repair the system?
5. **Information yield:** Is the probe cheap enough to discard and specific enough to make the next decision more trustworthy?

When the real-world action sits in an irreversible or high-consequence cell, first ask whether a lower-consequence representation can test the disputed mechanism. A sandbox, simulation, prototype, or developmental system should reduce blast radius without erasing the information needed for the eventual decision.

## Limitations

- Powerful actors may label their preferred action reversible when lock-in is already forming.
- Waiting also has consequences and can preserve harmful incumbency.
- Evidence strength is domain- and value-dependent, not a neutral score.
- A low-fidelity substitute can appear safe while teaching the wrong lesson about the consequential system.
