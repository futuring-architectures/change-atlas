# Adaptive futuring cycle

## Purpose

Use this cycle when uncertainty is structural and a single prediction would create false confidence.

## The cycle

1. **Curate:** design a signal environment with high-quality, heterogeneous, and dissenting sources.
2. **Perceive:** describe what is happening now; expose labels, assumptions, incentives, absences, and affected groups.
3. **Historicize:** compare at least as much relevant past as projected future; identify mechanisms rather than surface resemblance.
4. **Pluralize:** build multiple evidence-backed scenarios, including desired, feared, and neglected possibilities.
5. **Rehearse:** inspect what each scenario permits, who gains or loses, what breaks, and which capabilities would matter.
6. **Probe:** run bounded, reversible experiments that reveal the system without requiring the scenario to be right. Prefer a small blast radius and large information radius.
7. **Revise:** compare results with expectations, preserve surprises and failures, update both the map and the action, and decide whether to probe again or commit because the evidence has earned conviction.

## Output

Each cycle should produce a decision record: signals used, assumptions challenged, scenarios considered, probe boundaries, expected information, observed effects, resulting belief change, the threshold for commitment, and the next revision date.

## Limitations

- Scenario plurality can become avoidance of commitment.
- Historical analogies can import false mechanisms.
- Experiments can externalize risk to people who did not consent.
- Signal curation can reproduce the curator’s worldview.
- Cheap generation can prolong exploration without improving belief or decision quality.
