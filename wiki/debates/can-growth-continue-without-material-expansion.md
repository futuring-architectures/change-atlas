# Can growth continue without material expansion?

## Central question

Can societies sustain meaningful growth through quality, freedom, knowledge, safety, and capability while reducing or stabilizing material throughput?

## Position A

Betterment is not identical to more stuff. Knowledge, design, institutions, efficiency, options, and human capability can improve without proportional resource use. A mature economy can shift from producing more units toward better outcomes.

## Position B

Intangible systems depend on data centers, housing, energy, devices, logistics, and labor. Efficiency can trigger rebound, qualitative gains can be difficult to distribute, and calling growth intangible may hide material extraction elsewhere.

## Additional positions

- Material throughput can contract in some regions while rising where unmet basic needs remain.
- Diffusing proven health, energy, and institutional knowledge may create more welfare than frontier invention without requiring the same novelty race.
- The relevant objective may be flourishing within biophysical limits rather than preserving a growth label.

## Sources of disagreement

- Which outcomes count as growth and how betterment is measured.
- Whether relative dematerialization becomes absolute reduction.
- Distribution across people, places, and generations.
- The material footprint attributed to digital and service activity.

## Evidence

- [[type-2-growth-evi-c87ff36c1ba2#Claim 1|Type 2 Growth — claim 1]] defines qualitative betterment; [[type-2-growth-evi-c87ff36c1ba2#Claim 2|claim 2]] forecasts indefinite continuation.
- [[overhyped-and-never-delivered-a-brief-history-of-innovations-that-werent-evi-29faa5f0035a#Claim 1|Overhyped and never delivered — claim 1]] argues for broader diffusion of mastered achievements.

## Current assessment

Type 2 growth is a useful distinction but not evidence of absolute decoupling. A defensible claim needs material accounting, distributional measures, and longitudinal indicators of the specific betterment being asserted.

## Open questions

- Which betterment indicators can avoid becoming narrow optimization targets?
- Where has quality improved while absolute throughput declined?
- How should benefits and externalities be allocated across borders?
