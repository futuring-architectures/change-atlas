# Does AI expand expertise or hollow it out?

## Position: AI expands expertise

- Early task evidence suggests AI can raise productivity and narrow disadvantages for less-skilled workers.
- End-user programming and cheap exploration let individuals attempt work once reserved for coordinated specialist teams.
- Experts can chunk work at a higher level and use models as sounding boards, generators, and managed junior collaborators.

## Position: AI hollows expertise out

- Abstraction can remove the need to understand the full problem and turn workers into exception handlers.
- Model output can become a new minimum without supplying the practice needed to exceed it.
- Consensus-seeking models may be actively unhelpful in research that depends on challenging accepted views.
- Novices can use AI as a crutch while entry-level production—the path through which judgment was learned—contracts.

## Synthesis

The contradiction is conditional rather than rhetorical. Prior competence, task feedback, interface design, incentives, authority, and whether the user must verify consequences determine whether abstraction becomes leverage or dependency. Output improvement is not sufficient evidence of expertise growth.

## Evidence needed

- Longitudinal novice-to-expert learning with and without AI assistance.
- Measures of transfer to unaided or novel tasks, not only immediate output.
- Role-level evidence about autonomy, task breadth, wages, error ownership, and apprenticeship.
