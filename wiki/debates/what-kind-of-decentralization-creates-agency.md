# What kind of decentralization creates agency?

## Central question

Which distribution of technical capability, ownership, and decision rights gives affected people meaningful power to communicate, govern, exit, and coordinate—and when does “decentralization” merely relocate or obscure control?

## Position A: open protocols and forkability create sovereignty

Open protocols allow collective development while preserving the ability to fork. On this account, alternatives to centralized services emerge from shared technical foundations that no single firm can fully control. A social network reaches a minimum threshold when willing users can continue to find and communicate with each other despite opposition.

## Position B: local decision agency matters more than technical distribution

Decentralization is valuable when people can act decisively within the social context where a decision matters. Globally distributed infrastructure can still place moderation, computation, identity, or governance far from affected groups. The relevant architecture distributes authority to contextual units and gives them ways to coordinate.

## Position C: ownership can align participation and community

Token or participant ownership may transform a protocol from a transaction venue into a community, distribute benefits, and make users co-stewards. Forkability and ownership can reinforce one another by making exit and voice economically meaningful.

## Position D: Web3 can reproduce incumbent power and financialization

Web3 systems can remain embedded in Web2’s virality, mass markets, celebrity promotion, and commercial platforms. Financial participation can add risk and exclusion rather than agency. Technical openness or token ownership may leave practical control concentrated in infrastructure, wealth, or governance intermediaries.

## Position E: locally shaped AI can distribute alignment power

An ecosystem of differently owned and locally shaped models may distribute the power to encode knowledge, preferences, and values. Community-owned compute adds a proposed institutional unit in which members pool infrastructure and data and hold an interest in the resulting AI asset. A separate rebuild proposal argues for distributing local models, compute, tools, and equity before the productive layer reconcentrates. This extends decentralization from infrastructure and governance into model behavior and economic ownership, while leaving unresolved how internal authority, contribution rights, safety, affected publics, and coordination across divergent models should work.

## Sources of disagreement

- Definition: distribution of machines, resistance to censorship, ownership, local decision rights, and public benefit are different variables.
- Unit of analysis: individual users, protocol participants, communities, and the wider public have different claims.
- Evidence standard: many pro-decentralization sources describe affordances or futures, while critical sources emphasize continuities and risks.
- Values: universal access, local autonomy, safety, coordination, privacy, and equitable influence can conflict.
- Timeframe: early technical possibility does not establish durable institutional outcomes.

## Current assessment

The positions are not direct logical contradictions because they measure different dimensions. The strongest synthesis is a multidimensional test: technical reachability, model customizability, local compute, shared ownership, and forkability can enable agency, but they are insufficient without usable decision rights, viable alternatives, governance, safety, and an account of people outside the system. The new rebuild proposal independently strengthens the normative case for distributing productive means, but remains a forecast and institutional design position. No current Evidence demonstrates a system that satisfies all dimensions.

## Open questions

- Which decision rights must be local, and which require cross-context coordination?
- When does token ownership distribute authority rather than price governance power?
- What evidence would show that forkability is practical rather than formal?
- How should nonparticipants and affected publics be represented?
