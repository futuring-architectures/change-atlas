# Does AI flatten hierarchy or intensify management?

## Central question

As AI expands what individuals and agents can do, will organizations remove managerial layers and distribute authority, or will they add orchestration, evaluation, governance, and more concentrated control?

## Position A: AI flattens hierarchy

AI widens individual capability, blurs roles, and lowers the coordination cost of small teams. Information and expertise become more accessible, weakening managers whose primary function is routing information, monitoring activity, or translating between specialists. Leadership shifts from control toward influence, coaching, and context.

The packet-switching position makes this mechanism architectural: AI could route work to capabilities, translate edge activity, monitor delivery, and negotiate interfaces without reserving a manager in every path. Its strongest claim—that network organizations will therefore prevail—remains a forecast.

## Position B: AI intensifies management

Agents require goals, rules, permissions, evaluation, exception handling, and conflict resolution. Organizations may become top-heavy because leaders can direct much larger populations of digital workers, while new stewards and orchestrators manage the resulting systems. More autonomy at the execution layer can therefore coexist with tighter cybernetic control at the top.

## Sources of disagreement

- Whether “management” means people supervision, coordination, assurance, authority, or meaning-making.
- Whether AI capability is reliable enough for delegation or still requires close review.
- Whether control moves to individual contributors, orchestration specialists, executives, or platform providers.
- Whether role breadth reduces coordination or merely moves it into interfaces, rules, and evaluation.
- Whether hierarchy's developmental and interpretive functions can be supplied elsewhere.

## Current assessment

The positions are not strict opposites. AI may flatten the routing of routine information while thickening assurance, goal-setting, and exception management. It may distribute execution while concentrating authority. The discriminating evidence is therefore not average span of control alone, but who sets objectives, changes rules, resolves failures, develops judgment, and can refuse or reverse an agent's action.

The [[packet-switched-organization-protocol-stack|Packet-switched organization protocol stack]] sharpens this distinction by decomposing managerial routing into capability discovery, translation, handshakes, reliability classes, monitoring, and recovery. Whether that architecture distributes authority or merely relocates control into the routing layer remains unresolved.

## Open questions

- Which managerial layers disappear in observed AI deployments, and which new control functions appear?
- Does broader individual scope increase authority or only workload?
- Can peer institutions, craft communities, or protocols replace hierarchy's developmental functions?
