# Agent-compatible infrastructure may reduce human agency

## Proposition

Agent-compatible infrastructure may improve machine performance while reducing human agency unless inspection and recourse are designed as first-class interfaces.

**Scope:** Infrastructure and interfaces designed for AI agents and their human principals.

**Status:** `strengthened`

## Why it may hold

- **How We Learn Architectures:** The collection establishes opposing interface requirements for agent operability and human control, but supplies no comparative deployed interface outcome.
- **How We Learn Sustaining:** Read-only observability, deferential chatbot defaults, language-policing risk, and human-capacity erosion strengthen the need for inspection, challenge, provenance, and recourse in agent-compatible systems.
- **How We Learn Transitions:** Epistemic fluency, selective refusal, and activity-alignment monitoring make human inspection, disagreement, recourse, and purpose revision explicit complements to machine-compatible infrastructure.

## Qualifications and counter-evidence

- The evidence remains heterogeneous and does not establish a universal or deterministic effect.

## What would change the status

- **Strengthen:** add independent observations that show the proposed mechanism and outcome within the stated scope.
- **Qualify:** identify repeatable boundary conditions or countervailing mechanisms and revise the statement to express them.
- **Contest:** add credible evidence that the predicted direction, mechanism, or scope is materially wrong.
- **Promote to Trend:** establish a dated directional pattern across independent observations, with explicit scope and period.
