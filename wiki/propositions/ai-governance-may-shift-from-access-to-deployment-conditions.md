# AI governance may shift from access to deployment conditions

## Proposition

As model capability commoditizes, effective governance may shift from controlling access to controlling deployment conditions, interfaces, and institutional use.

**Scope:** Governance of increasingly available AI models in institutional deployment.

**Status:** `strengthened`

## Why it may hold

- **How We Learn Futuring:** Falling capability cost and institutionally mediated deployment support the shift in emphasis. The corpus does not compare access-control and deployment-governance regimes.
- **How We Learn Architectures:** Agent experience, capability allocation, the two institutional clocks, and work-system adoption tensions relocate governance into interfaces, workflows, assurance, and institutional architecture. Regime-level comparison remains absent.
- **How We Learn Sustaining:** Ingredient technology, hands-on alignment access, beneficial-system evaluation, and human-capacity effects further relocate governance into deployment architecture and institutions.
- **How We Learn Transitions:** Selective AI limits, legitimacy-sensitive resistance, epistemic fluency, pace layers, and continued outcome testing strengthen the shift toward governing deployment conditions rather than capability access alone.

## Qualifications and counter-evidence

- The evidence remains heterogeneous and does not establish a universal or deterministic effect.

## What would change the status

- **Strengthen:** add independent observations that show the proposed mechanism and outcome within the stated scope.
- **Qualify:** identify repeatable boundary conditions or countervailing mechanisms and revise the statement to express them.
- **Contest:** add credible evidence that the predicted direction, mechanism, or scope is materially wrong.
- **Promote to Trend:** establish a dated directional pattern across independent observations, with explicit scope and period.
