# Removing entry-level production may weaken future expertise

## Proposition

Removing entry-level production work may weaken the future supply of expert judgment, but neither widespread removal nor the delayed supply effect is yet observed.

**Scope:** Career pathways in knowledge work exposed to AI automation.

**Status:** `qualified`

## Why it may hold

- **Power To Creators Gameplay:** Role forecasts, crutch effects, and the need for underlying experience identify a plausible expertise-pipeline mechanism. The corpus lacks longitudinal novice progression or labor-demand outcomes.

## Qualifications and counter-evidence

- **How We Learn Architectures (`no_change`):** The collection discusses learner scaffolding and changing expert roles but supplies no case in which removal of entry-level production reduces the later supply of expert judgment.
- **How We Learn Sustaining (`qualified`):** Missing invention entry paths, late-bloomer evaluation failure, and capacity erosion strengthen the expertise-pipeline mechanism, but no AI-driven removal of entry-level production is observed.
- **How We Learn Transitions (`qualified`):** Task-boundary work and epistemic fluency identify expert judgment that production metrics omit, but the collection does not observe entry-level work removal or later expert-supply effects.

## What would change the status

- **Strengthen:** add independent observations that show the proposed mechanism and outcome within the stated scope.
- **Qualify:** identify repeatable boundary conditions or countervailing mechanisms and revise the statement to express them.
- **Contest:** add credible evidence that the predicted direction, mechanism, or scope is materially wrong.
- **Promote to Trend:** establish a dated directional pattern across independent observations, with explicit scope and period.
