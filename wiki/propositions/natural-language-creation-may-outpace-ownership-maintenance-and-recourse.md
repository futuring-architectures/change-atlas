# Natural-language creation may outpace ownership, maintenance, and recourse

## Proposition

Natural-language creation appears to distribute creation capability faster than reliable evaluation; whether ownership, maintenance capacity, and recourse lag together remains unresolved.

**Scope:** Nonprofessional and professional creation of software through natural-language interfaces.

**Status:** `qualified`

## Why it may hold

- **Power To Creators Gameplay:** Citizen development and user-programmable agents establish capability-distribution mechanisms, while gatekeeping and platform cases expose control risks. Ownership, maintenance, and recourse outcomes are not compared.
- **How We Learn Architectures:** Disposable software, proliferating software supply, agent-mediated interfaces, and lower switching cost strengthen the gap between creation and durable ownership, integration, maintenance, and recourse.
- **How We Learn Sustaining:** Cognitive debt and machine-speed propagation from unowned design systems strengthen the gap between cheap creation and durable ownership, maintenance, context, and recourse.

## Qualifications and counter-evidence

- **How We Learn Transitions (`qualified`):** Cheap app generation and weakened artifact signals show capability distribution outrunning reliable evaluation. Ownership, maintenance capacity, and recourse remain plausible but are not jointly observed.

## What would change the status

- **Strengthen:** add independent observations that show the proposed mechanism and outcome within the stated scope.
- **Qualify:** identify repeatable boundary conditions or countervailing mechanisms and revise the statement to express them.
- **Contest:** add credible evidence that the predicted direction, mechanism, or scope is materially wrong.
- **Promote to Trend:** establish a dated directional pattern across independent observations, with explicit scope and period.
