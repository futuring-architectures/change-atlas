# Renewal-capacity measures may preserve capability

## Proposition

Organizations that measure renewal capacity alongside present throughput may preserve capability through technological transitions better than organizations optimized only for current output.

**Scope:** Organizations managing long-duration technological transitions.

**Status:** `strengthened`

## Why it may hold

- **How We Learn Sustaining:** S-curve renewal, career infrastructure, and continuous operational improvement converge on a renewal mechanism, but no throughput-versus-renewal comparison is observed.
- **How We Learn Transitions:** Pace layering, legacy expertise, institutional variance, and epistemic inclusion strengthen the mechanism linking present transition design to future renewal capacity; comparative organizational outcomes remain absent.

## Qualifications and counter-evidence

- The evidence remains heterogeneous and does not establish a universal or deterministic effect.

## What would change the status

- **Strengthen:** add independent observations that show the proposed mechanism and outcome within the stated scope.
- **Qualify:** identify repeatable boundary conditions or countervailing mechanisms and revise the statement to express them.
- **Contest:** add credible evidence that the predicted direction, mechanism, or scope is materially wrong.
- **Promote to Trend:** establish a dated directional pattern across independent observations, with explicit scope and period.
