# Capability-centered redesign may outperform role substitution

## Proposition

Organizations that redesign workflows and decision rights around changing capabilities may outperform organizations that insert agents into inherited roles.

**Scope:** Organizational redesign for AI-enabled work.

**Status:** `strengthened`

## Why it may hold

- **How We Learn Architectures:** Capability-centered redesign and activity-system diagnosis supply a coherent mechanism, but no comparative performance outcome.
- **How We Learn Sustaining:** Async replacement systems, people-centered resilience, and the sustaining-capacity loop strengthen workflow and institution redesign around capability. Comparative performance remains absent.
- **How We Learn Transitions:** Radiology task boundaries, network-and-culture effects, differential institutional speeds, and the transition-integration stack further specify workflow and decision-right redesign. Performance comparison remains absent.

## Qualifications and counter-evidence

- The evidence remains heterogeneous and does not establish a universal or deterministic effect.

## What would change the status

- **Strengthen:** add independent observations that show the proposed mechanism and outcome within the stated scope.
- **Qualify:** identify repeatable boundary conditions or countervailing mechanisms and revise the statement to express them.
- **Contest:** add credible evidence that the predicted direction, mechanism, or scope is materially wrong.
- **Promote to Trend:** establish a dated directional pattern across independent observations, with explicit scope and period.
