# Legacy expertise may be transition infrastructure

## Proposition

Legacy expertise may be necessary infrastructure for long technological transitions even when dominant narratives frame old systems as obsolete.

**Scope:** Long-lived technical and institutional systems undergoing transition.

**Status:** `strengthened`

## Why it may hold

- **After The Internet Culture:** The collection supplies an industrial-knowledge warning and a mainframe dependency case, but lacks comparative transition outcomes and methods for preserving tacit expertise.
- **How We Learn Sustaining:** Continuous semiconductor improvement by unnamed engineers, context preservation, and owned design systems strengthen legacy expertise as transition infrastructure rather than obsolete residue.
- **How We Learn Transitions:** Historical myth correction, unrealized earlier ideas, pace layering, and ambiguous task-boundary work strengthen the case that transition depends on knowledge carried across old and new systems.

## Qualifications and counter-evidence

- **Nature Of The Firm Organization (`no_change`):** The collection distinguishes process from judgment and information routing from meaning scaffolding, but adds no legacy-system transition case or method for preserving incumbent expertise.
- **Nature Of The Firm Culture (`no_change`):** The programmer-apprenticeship warning concerns future tacit learning, but the collection adds no legacy-system transition case, retention method, or outcome evidence for incumbent expertise.

## What would change the status

- **Strengthen:** add independent observations that show the proposed mechanism and outcome within the stated scope.
- **Qualify:** identify repeatable boundary conditions or countervailing mechanisms and revise the statement to express them.
- **Contest:** add credible evidence that the predicted direction, mechanism, or scope is materially wrong.
- **Promote to Trend:** establish a dated directional pattern across independent observations, with explicit scope and period.
