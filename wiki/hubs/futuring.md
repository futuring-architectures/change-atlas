# Futuring

## Orientation

This Hub synthesizes **How We Learn → Futuring**. The collection is skeptical of prophecy but not of preparation. Its core move is to replace a demand for one correct future with a disciplined cycle of present perception, historical mechanism, plural scenarios, bounded experiments, anticipatory safeguards, and revision.

## Synthesis

The first object of futuring is the present. Information monoculture, inherited labels, novelty, and institutional incentives create [[present-blindness|present blindness]]. The [[adaptive-futuring-cycle|Adaptive futuring cycle]] responds by making signals, assumptions, histories, scenarios, probes, and revisions inspectable.

Uncertainty does not erase responsibility. If impact at scale cannot be assessed and exposure is hard to reverse, the system needs [[anticipatory-friction|anticipatory friction]]. The [[uncertainty-to-action-matrix|Uncertainty-to-action matrix]] ties the action posture to evidence and reversibility rather than optimism or fear alone.

The AI cases expose a recurring forecasting error: benchmark capability is projected directly into social outcome. [[capability-deployment-gap|The capability-deployment gap]] keeps interfaces, institutions, demand, incentives, and human practice in the causal chain. Radiology is a counterexample to mechanical job substitution; cheap GPT-2-class training is a warning against assuming frontier control will persist.

Finally, the corpus treats future claims as political acts. Scaling stories, superintelligence metaphors, and automation inevitability allocate money and permission now. [[future-narratives-allocate-permission|Future narratives allocate permission]], whether or not their predictions later prove accurate.

## Emerging propositions

- Organizations that practice reversible probes and explicit revision may adapt better than organizations optimized around one forecast.
- As model capability commoditizes, governance may shift from controlling access to controlling deployment conditions, interfaces, and institutional use.
- Shared AI surfaces may preserve collective sensemaking better than one-person, one-model, one-history interaction.

These remain synthesis-backlog propositions for later Architecture, Sustaining, and Transition collections.
