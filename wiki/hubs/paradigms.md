# Paradigms

## Orientation

This Hub synthesizes the curated **After the Internet → Paradigms** collection. The 105 entries span March 2021 through June 2026 and contain unusually many forecasts, analogies, and conceptual claims. The central object is therefore not a single technology trajectory. It is the set of assumptions used to decide what AI and software are, what their output proves, where human agency belongs, and which economic bottlenecks move when production becomes cheaper.

Four maps recur. First, fluent performance is repeatedly mistaken for broader competence, understanding, or agency. Second, metaphors such as tool, mind, organism, worker, infrastructure, and discovery instrument actively steer evaluation and governance. Third, software work shows bounded movement from direct code production toward orchestration, integration, and assurance, while hidden human labor remains essential. Fourth, abundance removes one constraint only to make another—attention, trust, integration, oversight, or cost—more binding.

The collection supports one candidate Trend with dated cases from 2025–2026. It does not support the stronger claim that all software work, knowledge work, or human judgment is being displaced.

## Synthesis notes from this collection

- AI-driven content abundance clearly increases selection pressure, but this collection does not establish whether audience attention concentrates on incumbent creators.
- The 2010s “New Internet” reform movement lost momentum while incumbent platforms remained powerful, contesting—but not retiring—the possibility of a later culturally owned internet wave.
- AI may turn interfaces from explicit screens into inference and memory, but the corpus does not yet show which designs preserve consent, correction, and portable user context.
- Model dependence may turn AI products into continuously maintained operational systems rather than finite software projects.
- Intelligence per dollar may become a primary enterprise optimization target as deployment costs rise.

These propositions have stable identities in `system/synthesis-backlog.json`; later relevant collections must assess them rather than recreate them with new wording.

## Synthesis boundary

This map derives from 105 curated entries and 210 atomic claims. It preserves forecasts as forecasts, treats analogies as interpretive instruments rather than observations, and separates bounded cases from market-wide conclusions. The candidate Trend is deliberately scoped to observed software-development practices in 2025–2026; broader claims about employment, intelligence, consciousness, or inevitable centralization remain debates or backlog propositions.
