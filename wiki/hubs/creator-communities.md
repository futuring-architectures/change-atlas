# Creator communities

## Orientation

This Hub synthesizes **Power to Creators → Communities**. The collection rejects the idea that a community is simply an audience with comments enabled. Communities can make entrepreneurship imaginable, carry recommendation and trust, organize production, and support direct creator careers. They can also overload maintainers, blur labor boundaries, reproduce power laws, and turn belonging into a financial asset.

## Synthesis

The collection exposes an inversion: digital goods may be non-rival, but the human attention that maintains them is scarce. Growth can therefore strengthen a community while exhausting its creative nucleus. [[community-sustainability-audit|The audit]] traces that boundary across contribution, attention, decision rights, rewards, care, and exit.

It also separates **community agency** from **community aesthetics**. Stories, symbols, tokens, and participation can create belonging without distributing authority or value. Ownership can help, but it can also price and speculate on relationships that were previously nonfinancial.

## Emerging propositions not yet promoted

- In creator systems, maintainer attention and boundary work may become the depleted commons even when the digital output is infinitely reproducible.
- Direct audience relationships may support a creative middle class outside algorithmic feeds, but current evidence is selected success cases rather than distributional data.
