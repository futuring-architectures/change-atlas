# Cultural and social technology

## Definition

A cultural and social technology gives people access to accumulated human information while reorganizing, transforming, or coordinating it at social scale. Henry Farrell, Alison Gopnik, Cosma Shalizi, and James Evans apply the category to large AI models and compare them with writing, print, markets, bureaucracies, and representative democracy. The claim is stronger than saying a technical artifact has cultural consequences: culture and social coordination are part of how the system operates.

## Provenance

Henry Farrell first frames ChatGPT as an engine of cultural transmission because it is powerful at imitation, a central mechanism of culture. Farrell and co-authors later name large models as cultural and social technologies. L. M. Sacasas pushes the framing toward environment: AI can envelop and form users rather than remain an external implement.

## Aliases and related terms

- Cultural transmission technology
- Social coordination technology
- AI as environment
- Artificial system of human society

## Distinctions and contested meanings

- The category does not imply that models possess culture, society, or agency as persons.
- Cultural transmission is selective and transformative, not neutral copying; training data, objectives, interfaces, and institutions change what becomes visible.
- The analogy with markets or democracy concerns information and coordination functions, not moral equivalence.
- A bounded application may still behave like a tool even when the wider model ecosystem has environmental or institutional effects.
