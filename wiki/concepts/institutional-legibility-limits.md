# Institutional legibility limits

## Definition

Institutional legibility limits arise because a firm is not exhausted by the information made visible to its leaders. Habits, relationships, and distributed tacit skills can be operationally decisive while remaining difficult to surface in dashboards, targets, and formal descriptions.

The name is an Atlas synthesis. Steve Randy Waldman's source supplies the underlying account of the firm as a living institution whose quantitative representation is useful but incomplete.

## Provenance

- Important characteristics of complex institutions are said to reside in [[seeing-like-a-ceo-evi-bc1b8533b647#Claim 1|habits, relationships, and invisibly distributed skills]].
- Objective quantitative measures can inform leadership while remaining [[seeing-like-a-ceo-evi-bc1b8533b647#Claim 2|insufficient for governing a complex firm]].

## Aliases and related terms

- Limits of organizational legibility
- Measurement incompleteness
- Tacit institutional knowledge

## Distinctions and contested meanings

- The concept is not an argument against measurement. It identifies the gap between a representation and the institution represented.
- Unmeasured does not mean unknowable; observation, participation, narrative, and distributed judgment may expose different aspects.
- Invoking complexity can protect valuable tacit practice, but it can also shield poor performance or unaccountable authority from scrutiny.
- The present Evidence diagnoses insufficiency but does not specify a reliable complementary governance practice.
