# Capacity erosion through assistance

## Definition

Capacity erosion through assistance occurs when a system raises short-term performance while reducing the practice, attention, verification, persistence, participation, or exposed error through which people remain able to act independently and govern consequences. One mechanism is sequencing: if assistance supplies a view before the person states a prior, later outcomes cannot correct that prior with the same clarity.

## Provenance

Howard Esbin groups persistence, verification, and participation as mutually reinforcing capacities that AI assistance may erode. Venkatesh Rao offers a qualification: the changed attention pattern of prompting can reflect the legitimate shift from doing to managing rather than simple cognitive decline. Addy Osmani identifies entry-level software production as a learning infrastructure and proposes deliberate substitutes when agents remove some of its repetitions. Greg Ceccarelli adds a belief-correction mechanism: judgment depends partly on committing to a view, bearing a choice, and reconciling prediction with consequence.

## Aliases and related terms

- Assistance-induced deskilling
- Automation complacency
- Human-capacity atrophy
- Participation erosion

## Distinctions and contested meanings

- Lower continuous effort is not automatically erosion; it may be delegation to a reliable system.
- Capacity can move upward from execution into orchestration and judgment.
- Independent performance is not the only valuable outcome, but dependence changes bargaining power and resilience.
- Longitudinal measures are required; immediate task scores cannot establish erosion.
- Better assisted decisions and weaker learning can coexist; preserving every avoidable error is neither necessary nor ethical.
