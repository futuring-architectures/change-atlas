# Openness is not self-determination

## Definition

Transparency, reusability, and extensibility can make a technology open while leaving control over its development, infrastructure, terms, or governance concentrated. Technological self-determination asks a stronger question: whether affected people possess meaningful capacity to choose, modify, exit, and shape the system.

The distinction comes from Cory Doctorow's critique of “open AI.” This page preserves the distinction without treating every open technology as centrally controlled.

## Provenance

- Sector concentration is said to expose [[open-ai-isn-t-evi-c72f2bef72f1#Claim 2|the limits of openness alone]].
- The source judges systems marketed as open AI to offer [[open-ai-isn-t-evi-c72f2bef72f1#Claim 3|insufficient transparency, reusability, and extensibility]].

## Aliases and related terms

- Openwashing
- Openness without control
- Technological self-determination
- Exit, voice, and forkability

## Distinctions and contested meanings

- Openness has multiple dimensions. Open weights, open source, open standards, transparent governance, and permissionless access are not interchangeable.
- Self-determination may require collective governance or viable exit, not merely individual access to artifacts.
- Concentration can limit practical autonomy even when formal reuse rights exist; conversely, concentrated stewardship does not prove that an artifact is technically closed.
- The current Evidence offers a critique, not an operational test for adequate self-determination.
- Community ownership can combine openness with stronger practical control, but “the community” may still hide concentrated administration, unequal voice, coerced data contribution, or costly exit.
