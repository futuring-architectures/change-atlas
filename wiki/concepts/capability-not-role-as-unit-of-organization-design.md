# Capability, not role, as the unit of organization design

## Definition

This principle treats an AI system as a changing bundle of capabilities rather than a synthetic occupant of a human job. The unit of redesign becomes the work system: goals, workflows, decision rights, interfaces, evaluation, and the moving boundary between machine reliability and human judgment.

## Provenance

Mike Boysen rejects the employee metaphor because it imports agency and motivation. Sangeet Paul Choudary argues that calling agents co-workers anchors adoption in roles and hides the required architectural redesign. The wider corpus similarly criticizes automation that preserves yesterday's production logic.

## Aliases and related terms

- Capability allocation
- Capability-centered organization design
- Agentic work-system design
- Beyond the digital-labor metaphor

## Distinctions and contested meanings

- Capability is not autonomy, personhood, or accountability.
- Roles remain useful containers for rights, identity, development, and coordination even when task bundles change.
- Starting from capabilities does not mean optimizing the organization around what machines can do; purpose and stakeholder claims constrain redesign.
- Capability values move, so a one-time allocation is insufficient.
