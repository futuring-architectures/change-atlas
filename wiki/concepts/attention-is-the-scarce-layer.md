# Attention is the scarce layer

## Definition

As the supply of publishable and generatable content grows beyond any person’s capacity to consume it, production is no longer the only scarce resource. The binding problem moves toward selection: what is indexed, trusted, recommended, retrieved, seen, and remembered. Curation, search, ranking, fandom, and marketing become allocation mechanisms for finite attention.

## Provenance

Benedict Evans uses the scale of internet participation to establish that no one can read everything. Evan Armstrong describes an already unfinishable media backlog. Drew Austin states the supply-side consequence: as content grows relative to human attention, a given item is less likely to be seen. Sari Azout argues that chronological curation must become searchable and available on demand.

## Aliases and related terms

- Attention bottleneck
- Selection problem
- Information abundance
- Signal over noise

## Distinctions and contested meanings

- Attention is not fixed in all respects; interfaces, habits, and new media can change how much and what kind of attention people allocate.
- Scarcity is uneven. Some creators and topics receive excess attention while others remain invisible.
- More content can increase both competition and niche matching; abundance does not automatically imply concentration.
- The current corpus does not yet establish whether generative AI increases concentration, fragmentation, or total consumption.
