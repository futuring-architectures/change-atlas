# Refounding

## Definition

Refounding is a deliberate reinterpretation of an established organization for changed constraints. Unlike a pivot, it retains continuity with the original business or mission; unlike a reorganization, it implies changes in operating assumptions, work, authority, and capabilities rather than reporting lines alone.

## Provenance

Byrne Hobart distinguishes refounding from pivoting and uses selected corporate and institutional cases to describe periodic renewal. Bill Fischer's reorganization critique supplies a discriminating boundary: structural change without changed work styles, distributed autonomy, or mindset is weak evidence of transformation.

## Aliases and related terms

- Institutional renewal
- Organizational re-founding
- Business-model reinterpretation
- Transformation without abandonment

## Distinctions and contested meanings

- A turnaround restores viability; a refounding may occur while an organization still appears successful.
- A pivot abandons or substantially replaces the original business; a refounding reframes it for new constraints.
- A reorganization changes formal structure; a refounding must also alter operating behavior.
- The source cases are retrospective and selective. The concept does not establish how often refoundings succeed or whether the label is applied only after success.
