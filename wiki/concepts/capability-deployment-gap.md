# Capability-deployment gap

## Definition

The capability-deployment gap is the distance between a technical demonstration and a useful, adopted, safe, legitimate system. It contains interface design, workflow integration, incentives, institutions, demand, cost, trust, regulation, and human skill.

## Why it matters

Forecasts that move directly from benchmark capability to jobs, markets, or social outcomes skip the gap. Conversely, weak present applications do not prove the underlying capability has stopped improving.

The gap should not become a residual explanation for every missing impact. Slow conversion can coexist with a ceiling on useful inference: finite measurement, irreducible error, or weak demand may limit the value available to deploy even when benchmark capability and compute continue to rise.
