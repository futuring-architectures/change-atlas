# Design for emergence

## Definition

Design for emergence creates the conditions through which users can shape their own end result. The designer supplies primitives, constraints, feedback, and an intelligible adaptation experience, while treating the user's local knowledge as part of the system.

## Provenance

Kasey Klimes contrasts the posture with user-centered design that still reserves control over the final object for the designer. The distinction is not whether users were researched; it is whether they can alter the system in use.

## Aliases and related terms

- End-user adaptation
- User-configurable systems
- Meta-design
- Designing the design experience

## Distinctions and contested meanings

- Emergence is not absence of design. It relocates design effort into primitives, permissions, guardrails, feedback, and recoverability.
- User control is not automatically equitable; capability, time, and literacy affect who can adapt a system.
- Fixed outcomes remain appropriate where interoperability, safety, or rights require hard constraints.
- The concept complements [[user-programmable-agents|User-programmable agents]] but applies beyond AI.
