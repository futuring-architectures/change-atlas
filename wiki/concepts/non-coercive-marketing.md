# Non-coercive marketing

## Definition

Non-coercive marketing is Rob Hardy's name for marketing that informs and invites while leaving decision authority and timing with the prospective customer. It rejects manipulation, manufactured urgency, pestering, and funnels designed around an outcome already chosen by the seller. Hardy later renamed the approach trust-based marketing.

## Provenance

The 2022 primer defines the approach as opening doors, telling the truth about what lies behind them, surrendering the outcome, and trusting that aligned people will enter when ready.

## Aliases and related terms

- Trust-based marketing
- Marketing as invitation
- Permission and customer authority
- Non-manipulative marketing

## Distinctions and contested meanings

- All communication frames attention; an absence of overt pressure does not make a seller neutral.
- Truthfulness requires decisions about relevance, omission, uncertainty, and comparison.
- Surrendering an immediate outcome can still serve a long-term commercial strategy.
- The Concept is an ethical and operating position; the excerpt does not establish its revenue, trust, or retention effects.
