# Pace layering

## Definition

Pace layering interprets a system as components that change at different rates. Fast layers explore and respond; slower layers stabilize standards, rights, infrastructure, identity, and accumulated commitments. Transition is the work of coordinating them without demanding one universal speed.

## Provenance

Chris Coyier applies Stewart Brand's pace-layer model to browser technologies, asking whether HTML's slower movement benefits a system containing faster JavaScript. Tyler Cowen's AI integration account supplies an economic analogue: unfamiliar capability can arrive before institutions learn to combine it with human activity.

## Aliases and related terms

- Pace layers
- Multi-speed system
- Institutional clock gap
- Differential-rate architecture

## Distinctions and contested meanings

- Slow is not automatically wise; slow layers can preserve injustice and obsolete constraints.
- Fast is not automatically innovative; feedback can amplify shallow metrics.
- A layer's appropriate speed depends on reversibility, dependency, and consequence.
- Transition requires interfaces between layers, not only patience.
