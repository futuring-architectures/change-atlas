# Contextual decentralization

## Definition

Contextual decentralization evaluates a system by whether people closest to a problem can act, communicate, govern, and exit within the social context where consequences occur. It shifts the question from “How distributed is the infrastructure?” to “Which decisions can affected people actually make, and how do local units coordinate across contexts?”

## Provenance

Divya Siddarth, Danielle Allen, and E. Glen Weyl define decentralization’s value as empowering people to act decisively within social contexts and criticize decisions made by distant authorities. Varun Srinivasan supplies a narrower social-network threshold based on censorship-resistant communication. Ben Thompson adds the forkability and sovereignty promise of open protocols.

## Aliases and related terms

- Subsidiarity
- Local agency
- Composable local control
- Decentralization as decision rights

## Distinctions and contested meanings

- Technical distribution can improve resilience without transferring decision rights.
- Local control can preserve context while making cross-context coordination and universal rights harder.
- Individual exit or forkability differs from collective authority over a shared system.
- Token ownership is not equivalent to agency when participation, wealth, or governance power is concentrated.
