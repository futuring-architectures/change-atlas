# Network topology shapes cultural outcomes

## Definition

Network outcomes are not properties of connectivity alone. The number and pattern of connections, interaction cadence, feedback, ranking, and boundaries affect what spreads, how quickly groups coordinate, whether variation survives, and whether collective behavior stabilizes or cascades into failure.

The Concept rejects two shortcuts: that connecting more people necessarily improves collective intelligence, and that harmful outcomes are inherent to “social media” independent of design.

## Provenance

Pondering Durian explicitly states that topology, connection shape, monetization, products, communication modes, and algorithmic amplification all matter. Joe Bak-Coleman applies the principle to collective intelligence: network structure and interaction must be tuned for collective outcomes. Rohit Krishnan develops the density distinction, arguing that dense networks favor dominant ideas while sparse pockets preserve variation.

## Aliases and related terms

- Topology is policy
- Architecture shapes behavior
- Network effects on culture
- Dense and sparse information networks

## Distinctions and contested meanings

- Topology is one causal layer, not a substitute for incentives, norms, governance, or participant agency.
- Dense networks can improve diffusion and coordination while increasing homogenization and contagion.
- Sparse networks can preserve variation while also sheltering harmful ideas and reducing correction or learning.
- The current Evidence supports a design principle and a tension, not a universal law connecting one topology to one outcome.
