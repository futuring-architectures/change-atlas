# Co-acting group

## Definition

A co-acting group is a loose confederation of people who dip into collaborative interactions as a project unfolds. Unlike a true team, it does not assume stable membership, a shared identity, high interdependence, and persistent joint routines.

## Provenance

Constance Noonan Hadley and Mark Mortensen propose co-acting groups as an alternative when the social and coordination costs of a true team exceed the work's need for stable interdependence.

## Distinctions and contested meanings

- A co-acting group is not simply a badly functioning team; it is a different coordination form.
- Flexibility can lower unnecessary collaboration but weaken shared context, trust, accountability, and care.
- The concept does not imply that teams are obsolete. It sharpens the threshold for using them.
- AI can widen individual scope, but that does not by itself determine whether work needs a team or a co-acting group.
