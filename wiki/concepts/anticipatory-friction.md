# Anticipatory friction

## Definition

Anticipatory friction is a designed capacity to slow, bound, inspect, reverse, or contest a system because some failure is expected even when the exact failure cannot be predicted.

## Mechanism

The response to uncertainty is not a choice between unrestricted scale and total prohibition. Staged exposure, rate limits, independent review, mandatory pauses, logs, rollback, and recourse preserve learning without making everyone absorb an experiment at once.

## Boundary

Friction can become defensive bureaucracy, exclusion, or incumbent protection. It should be tied to stakes, reversibility, exposure, and evidence rather than discomfort with novelty alone. Sinofsky's critique supplies a useful adversarial test: concern and moral language should not be treated as demonstrated harm. That test does not eliminate anticipatory action where stakes are high or harm would be irreversible; it raises the burden of connecting friction to an explicit risk mechanism and proportionate response.
