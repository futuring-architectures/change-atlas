# Responsibility gap after automation

## Definition

A responsibility gap arises when a person remains nominally accountable but no longer performs the questioning, judgment, and learning needed to own the result. Approval becomes ceremonial while the system’s output governs.

## Distinctions and contested meanings

- Keeping a human click is not the same as preserving meaningful control.
- Responsibility without authority, time, information, or competence is blame transfer.
- Automation can also improve judgment by expanding evidence and freeing attention.
- The concept concerns role design, not a claim that manual work is inherently responsible.
- Reconstructed first-person authorship can restore meaningful ownership or conceal dependence and model contribution; responsibility must be tested through authority, competence, provenance, and recourse rather than attribution language alone.
