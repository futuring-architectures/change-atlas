# Power as an organizing design value

## Definition

A technological system can reproduce power not only through later control or misuse but through the values used to resolve design trade-offs during its construction. If power is the organizing value, repeated local decisions can entrench it in the resulting system.

## Provenance

Eryk Salvaggio argues that technology is built by actors with power, that an organizing value guides design trade-offs, and that using power as that value tends to reproduce power: [[are-other-ais-possible-evi-8e47b847a90f#Claim 1|technology's dependence on actors with power]], [[are-other-ais-possible-evi-8e47b847a90f#Claim 2|organizing values guiding design trade-offs]], and [[are-other-ais-possible-evi-8e47b847a90f#Claim 3|design decisions reproducing power]].

The Governance collection extends the mechanism from technical design to organizational structure. David Sherry contrasts distributed decision-making and stewardship with conventional firms that direct power upward: [[in-this-era-openness-wins-evi-46304a0ed935#Claim 1|distributed authority with stewardship]] and [[in-this-era-openness-wins-evi-46304a0ed935#Claim 3|power concentrated by firm structure]]. Cory Doctorow adds that formal openness does not guarantee self-determination when control remains concentrated: [[open-ai-isn-t-evi-c72f2bef72f1#Claim 1|openness without self-determination]] and [[open-ai-isn-t-evi-c72f2bef72f1#Claim 2|concentration limiting openness]].

## Aliases and related terms

- Embedded power
- Values in design
- Politics of technical systems

## Distinctions and contested meanings

- The source uses power broadly and does not distinguish economic, political, technical, and cultural power.
- Real design processes can contain multiple competing values rather than a single organizing principle.
- The claim describes a mechanism; it does not demonstrate the design history of a particular system.
- User adoption and design-embedded power are compatible explanations rather than automatic opposites.
- Distributed participation can relocate power without eliminating coordination, stewardship, or informal hierarchy.
- Formal openness and practical self-determination must be evaluated separately.
