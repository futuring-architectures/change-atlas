# Capability is not competence

## Definition

An AI system demonstrates capability when it can produce a specified result under bounded conditions. Competence is the broader ability to perform a practice reliably across changing context, ambiguous intent, interdependent tasks, exceptions, consequences, and recovery. A benchmark result is evidence about performance; it is not automatic evidence of professional competence, understanding, or agency.

## Provenance

Jon Evans calls unsupported movement from exam performance to job performance the “fundamental extrapolation error.” IEEE Spectrum’s account warns against mistaking performance for competence. Matt Rickard’s System 1 framing distinguishes fluent first-draft behavior from deliberate analysis, while Jon Stokes identifies the absence of a durable interlocutor model as a concrete limit on resolving ambiguity.

## Aliases and related terms

- Performance–competence gap
- Benchmark extrapolation error
- Task capability versus workflow reliability
- Apparent understanding

## Distinctions and contested meanings

- Capability is not trivial: repeated bounded performance can become a component of competence when embedded in tools, process, and oversight.
- Competence need not imply consciousness or human-like reasoning. A system can be operationally competent through nonhuman mechanisms.
- The gap is task-specific. Reliable compression, classification, or retrieval does not imply reliable counseling, diagnosis, management, or authorship.
- External memory, tools, and organizational controls can extend competence beyond the base model without changing what the model itself understands.
