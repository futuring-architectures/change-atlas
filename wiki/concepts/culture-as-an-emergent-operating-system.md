# Culture as an emergent operating system

## Definition

Culture is an emergent operating system: the accumulated pattern of what participants repeatedly do, reward, tolerate, narrate, and learn. Formal values are inputs only when they alter those interactions.

## Provenance

Reeves and Levin frame organizations as complex adaptive systems whose culture emerges from local behavior. Taylor Pearson adds path dependence: small decisions compound into trajectories. Chamorro-Premuzic shows a failure mechanism when organizations marginalize the very challenge their innovation rhetoric claims to value.

## Distinctions and contested meanings

- “Emergent” does not mean leaderless or beyond power. Leaders control consequential constraints and resources.
- Culture is not simply employee sentiment; it includes operating routines, informal sanctions, authority, and interpretation.
- Culture cannot be directly commanded, but it can be strongly shaped and sometimes coercively engineered.
- A coherent culture can support learning or enforce conformity; coherence is not evidence of desirability.
