# Customer capitalism

## Definition

Customer capitalism treats meeting customer needs as the firm's starting point rather than treating customers primarily as a means to extract shareholder value. In the source's formulation, the distinction is an operating logic: “what is in it for the customer?” produces different decisions from “what is in it for us?”

The term and contrast come from Steve Denning. This page records their provenance; candidate status does not endorse the source's performance claims.

## Provenance

- Denning distinguishes firm-centered value extraction from customer-centered need satisfaction as [[how-maximizing-shareholder-value-re-emerged-as-value-creation-evi-0034eca755fc#Claim 1|different operating logics]].
- He attributes exceptional value creation over the preceding two decades to firms he classifies as customer-capitalist: [[how-maximizing-shareholder-value-re-emerged-as-value-creation-evi-0034eca755fc#Claim 2|the claimed performance of customer capitalism]].
- John Hagel's account of corporate narrative adds a communicative mechanism: a long-term narrative can orient customer action and potentially rebuild trust: [[every-company-needs-a-narrative-evi-11a5c4229b7e#Claim 1|narrative that helps customers act]] and [[every-company-needs-a-narrative-evi-11a5c4229b7e#Claim 2|narrative as a trust opportunity]].

## Aliases and related terms

- Customer-centric management
- Customer-oriented firm purpose
- Outside-in operating logic

## Distinctions and contested meanings

- Customer capitalism is not identical to stakeholder capitalism; prioritizing customers can still conflict with employee, supplier, environmental, or public interests.
- Customer orientation can be an operating purpose, a marketing posture, or a measurement regime. The Evidence does not supply a test that separates them.
- Customer benefit and firm profitability can align without establishing that customer orientation caused the financial result.
- A compelling narrative can help customers act, but narrative quality is not evidence that the underlying operating logic is customer-centered.
