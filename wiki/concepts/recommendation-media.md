# Recommendation media

## Definition

Recommendation media is a distribution model in which a platform’s ranking system, rather than a user’s network of chosen connections, is the primary route by which content reaches an audience. The system can use social signals, but following is input rather than instruction. The platform defines the objective—often attention or engagement—and may tailor the result to each user.

## Provenance

Michael Mignano names the distinction between social media and recommendation media. Cory Doctorow supplies the complementary “end-to-end” critique: dominant platforms treat follows as suggestions. TikTok supplies a bounded case of the dense behavioral feedback available to recommendation systems.

## Aliases and related terms

- Algorithmic media
- For-you distribution
- Ranked feed
- Interest graph
- Recommendation-first platform

## Distinctions and contested meanings

- A recommendation system can use the social graph; the distinction concerns the primary allocator of distribution.
- Personalization and recommendation are not identical. A nonpersonalized popularity ranking can still be recommendation media.
- Recommendation can reduce overload and improve discovery while also transferring control from speaker and recipient to platform.
- The current Evidence identifies the mechanism but does not establish a corpus-level Trend in its market share or usage.
