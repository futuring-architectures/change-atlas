# Abundance relocates bottlenecks

## Definition

Radically cheaper production expands possible use cases, but it rarely makes an entire system unconstrained. It changes which complementary input is binding. Abundant content makes selection and attention scarce. Abundant code makes integration, evaluation, and trust scarce. Abundant model capability can make deployment cost, energy, latency, data, measurement, sensing, physical actuation, and governance more salient. Some constraints are complementary scarcities that can move again; others may be irreducible information or prediction limits.

## Provenance

Byrne Hobart supplies the general economic rule that radically cheaper products generate unexpectedly abundant uses. James Vincent describes machine-scale content challenging human curation. The MenuGen case shows code production becoming easier while deployment integration remains difficult. Ricky Ho’s enterprise claim shifts optimization from maximum intelligence toward intelligence per dollar. Sidu Ponnappa applies the mechanism to AI-native services: improved models can widen the economically viable work frontier while shifting constraint toward UX, adoption, cognitive load, and human judgment.

## Aliases and related terms

- Moving constraint
- Bottleneck migration
- Complementary scarcity
- Abundance–selection shift

## Distinctions and contested meanings

- A relocated bottleneck may be temporary; tools and institutions can make the complement cheaper in turn.
- Scarcity can be created by market power or regulation as well as by technical limits.
- Lower production cost may still reduce total cost even when a new bottleneck becomes more visible.
- The Concept explains constraint movement, not who captures the value or bears the new work.
