# Orbital stakeholders

## Definition

Orbital stakeholders are participants outside a firm's employee, supplier-contract, and shareholder core whose labor, attention, content, reputation, demand, complements, or community relationships materially affect the firm's value and resilience. “Orbital” describes position around a formal core, not lower importance.

## Provenance

Tom Critchlow uses the term while describing Instagram's surrounding economy and argues that permeable organizations need new mechanisms for stakeholder access, voice, and ownership. Henry Coutinho-Mason's Exit to Community discussion adds an ownership puzzle: community-powered digital businesses appear structurally compatible with cooperative forms, yet prominent high-growth digital startups rarely use them.

## Aliases and related terms

- Ecosystem stakeholder
- External contributor
- Complementor
- Community participant
- Stakeholder beyond the firm

## Distinctions and contested meanings

- A user is not automatically a stakeholder with equal claims; contribution, dependence, exposure to risk, and replaceability differ.
- Formal employment is neither necessary nor sufficient for meaningful voice.
- “Community” can conceal commercial dependence and unequal power when used as a brand category.
- Ownership, compensation, voice, portability, and recourse are different rights and need not travel together.
