# Capability-renewal infrastructure

## Definition

Capability-renewal infrastructure is the social and institutional substrate through which a practice persists beyond a breakthrough or one generation of experts. It includes entry paths, situated access, repeated practice, communities, operational improvement, prestige, capital recycling, late-entry routes, maintenance, and succession.

## Provenance

The Atlas term synthesizes several sources. Jason Crawford diagnoses the missing career structure for invention. Dietrich Vollrath emphasizes unnamed engineers who continually improved semiconductor production. Beren Millidge makes model access a precondition for practical alignment learning. Tom Blomfield describes expertise and capital cycling through successive startup generations.

## Aliases and related terms

- Capability pipeline
- Practice infrastructure
- Institutional learning substrate
- Regenerative expertise system

## Distinctions and contested meanings

- Infrastructure is not a fixed credential ladder; it can include informal communities and late bloomers.
- Access without maintenance, recognition, or progression can distribute tools without renewing competence.
- Protecting legacy expertise can sustain transition or entrench obsolete methods.
- Renewal asks who can enter and inherit a practice, not only whether current output is high.
