# Agentic participants and agentic markets

## Definition

An agentic participant represents a principal within an existing market: it searches, compares, selects, or transacts against offers that others have already composed. An agentic market, in Sangeet Paul Choudary's proposed definition, emerges when represented counterparties express objectives, constraints, and trade-offs and their interaction determines what executable offer is made.

The distinction changes the unit of analysis from an agent's task performance to the interaction between parties. A buyer may have a rich account of its needs while encountering a seller that exposes only fixed packages. Better buyer intelligence can improve selection without making supply more responsive.

## Aggregation and coordination

Aggregation concentrates where demand enters a market. Coordination changes how participants align their constraints and construct an exchange. The two can coexist: a dominant personal agent could aggregate demand and also negotiate with suppliers. Bilateral representation therefore does not by itself distribute ownership, decision power, or the gains from trade.

For travel, the proposed shift is from selecting a published package to negotiating around date flexibility, inventory, room preferences, and supplier targets. For procurement, it is from comparing quotations to adjusting specifications, delivery commitments, capacity, and margins together. These are prospective mechanisms; the article does not demonstrate completed transactions of this kind.

## Provenance

Choudary introduced the distinction in October 2026. The article reports consumer-agent products and Indeed tools on both sides of hiring, while its procurement observatory reports participants rather than agentic markets under this definition. Tools serving both sides are evidence of representation; direct interaction that changes the offer is a separate test.

## Distinctions and limits

- Machine-readable catalogues and APIs can support excellent [[agent-experience|Agent experience]] without permitting a counterparty to revise an offer.
- An automated purchase from a fixed catalogue remains agentic participation under this definition.
- Two agents are insufficient if each only selects predetermined choices or lacks authority to alter terms.
- Dynamic pricing alone does not establish mutual construction of an offer from both principals' objectives and trade-offs.
- A negotiable offer still needs feasible fulfillment, delegated authority, and enforceable commitments. Representation alone establishes none of those conditions.
- The source's report of no agentic procurement markets is limited to its stated observatory assessment, not a verified global absence.
