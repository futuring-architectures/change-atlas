# Ecosystem advantage

## Definition

Ecosystem advantage is useful capacity a firm obtains through access to relationships, participant activity, specialist producers, distribution channels, standards, tools, infrastructure, and complements that it does not fully own. It shifts strategy from optimizing only controlled assets and internal value chains toward shaping reciprocal external dependencies.

## Provenance

Greg Satell contrasts value-chain efficiency with advantage embedded in webs of connection and access. Patrick McKenzie's miniature case shows the mechanism in a functioning supply chain. Mark Zuckerberg's Llama argument makes complement mobilization an explicit sponsor strategy: a model used by many firms can attract tools and optimizations that one company would not build alone.

## Aliases and related terms

- Ecosystem strategy
- Access over ownership
- Complement advantage
- Networked capability

## Distinctions and contested meanings

- Access is not control. A critical capability can disappear, change terms, or become a bottleneck.
- Ecosystems can be orchestrated, federated, market-based, community-governed, or sponsor-controlled.
- More connections do not guarantee better information; Quibi's case shows that internal authority can discount external signals.
- An ecosystem may create collective value while distributing capture, voice, and risk asymmetrically.
