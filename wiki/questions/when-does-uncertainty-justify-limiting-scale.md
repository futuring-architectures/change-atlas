# When does uncertainty justify limiting scale?

## Question

What threshold of uncertainty should prevent a technology from being deployed to everyone at once? How should the answer change when harms are irreversible, exposure is involuntary, failure is hard to observe, or affected people lack recourse?

## Why it remains open

“We cannot know every effect” can excuse reckless scale or paralyze beneficial experimentation. The decision needs a proportional rule that distinguishes bounded learning from system-wide exposure.

## What would answer it

- Domain-specific evidence standards tied to stakes and reversibility.
- Comparative records of staged and unrestricted deployments.
- Explicit accounting of the harms of waiting as well as acting.
- Governance in which exposed communities can define stopping and repair conditions.
