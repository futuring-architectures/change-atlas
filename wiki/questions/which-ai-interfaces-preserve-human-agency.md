# Which AI interfaces preserve human agency?

## Why it matters

Copilots, agents, conversational interfaces, predictive interfaces, and heads-up displays distribute control differently. A system can reduce interaction effort while also making inference, memory, and objective-setting invisible. Agency requires more than a human being present: people need meaningful influence, legibility, recourse, and the ability to redirect or stop action.

## What is currently known

- The [[automation-posture-matrix|Automation posture matrix]] argues that interface posture should vary with predictability and consequence.
- Stripe Partners argues that machine learning creates opportunities for users to influence output beyond deterministic “user error” prevention: [[building-agency-into-the-relationships-between-humans-and-ai-evi-43b087f1ebc2#Claim 2|Building agency into human–AI relationships — claim 2]].
- Geoffrey Litt distinguishes routine delegation from expert augmentation: [[enough-ai-copilots-we-need-ai-huds-evi-3c09cc413bb5#Claim 2|Enough AI copilots! We need AI HUDs — claim 2]].
- Hiten Shah predicts interfaces based on inference and interaction memory: [[our-ui-is-being-replaced-behind-your-back-evi-150f7fe2bf50#Claim 2|Our UI is being replaced behind your back — claim 2]].
- The human-in-the-loop evidence warns that nominal oversight can reduce control and moral agency: [[ai-s-human-in-the-loop-isn-t-evi-734825a3f56e#Claim 1|AI's “human in the loop” isn't — claim 1]].
- [[cultural-and-social-technology|Cultural and social technology]] broadens agency beyond task control: an interface can transmit norms and form goals as well as execute instructions.
- [[your-ai-is-not-a-tool-evi-f725ec6bd434#Claim 1|Your AI Is Not a Tool — claim 1]] argues that sufficiently enveloping AI behaves more like an environment than an external implement.

## Missing or discriminating evidence

- Comparative user outcomes across agent, copilot, HUD, direct-manipulation, and invisible automation forms.
- Measures of goal influence, error detection, correction cost, learned dependence, and retained skill.
- Designs that make memory and inference inspectable, editable, portable, and revocable.
- Evidence from affected users rather than only designers and product strategists.

## Suggested next sources or actions

- Test the same consequential workflow with different interaction postures.
- Include agency, comprehension, override success, and recovery—not only task speed—in evaluation.
- Examine how organizational incentives determine whether a nominal user can really refuse automation.
