# How can institutions preserve tacit knowledge through transition?

## Why it matters

Technical replacement plans often model code, infrastructure, and cost while treating expertise as documentation that can be transferred on demand. Long-lived systems instead accumulate operational memory in maintainers, workarounds, relationships, and knowledge of rare failures. That knowledge can disappear before the dependency does.

## What is currently known

- [[legacy-mainframes-underwrite-digital-services|Legacy mainframes underwrite digital services]] records the continuing dependence of modern services on old systems and shrinking specialist cohorts.
- [[1979-evi-d9574be8c003#Claim 1|1979 — claim 1]] describes mainframe software, specialist expertise, and organizational underinvestment as a combined transition risk.
- [[the-end-of-industrial-society-evi-b3abf21e4826#Claim 2|The End of Industrial Society — claim 2]] argues more broadly that transitions change institutional forms and skill demands rather than simply swapping one technology for another.
- [[the-future-worth-building-is-human-evi-d29670f349f2#Claim 1|The Future Worth Building Is Human — claim 1]] characterizes productive knowledge as local and continuously revised; [[the-future-worth-building-is-human-evi-d29670f349f2#Claim 2|claim 2]] proposes ongoing model adaptation with practitioners rather than one-time extraction.
- [[the-future-worth-building-is-human-evi-d29670f349f2#Claim 6|The Future Worth Building Is Human — claim 6]] reports Toyota returning expert craftspeople to an automated line to grow the knowledge needed to teach machines, but supplies no comparative outcome.
- [[what-will-more-intelligence-actually-do-for-us-evi-6d1521adbc8d#Claim 7|What will more intelligence do? — claim 7]] describes production knowledge distributed across workers' practice; [[what-will-more-intelligence-actually-do-for-us-evi-6d1521adbc8d#Claim 8|claim 8]] predicts that dense sensing and AI-guided experimentation could make parts of that knowledge transferable, while [[what-will-more-intelligence-actually-do-for-us-evi-6d1521adbc8d#Claim 9|claim 9]] raises diffusion and excludability trade-offs.

## Missing or discriminating evidence

- Cases comparing successful and failed knowledge transfer during platform replacement.
- Methods for identifying tacit dependencies before experienced maintainers leave.
- Evidence on apprenticeships, paired operations, simulations, documentation, and phased migration.
- Measures of transition readiness that include recovery from rare events, not only feature parity.
- Governance arrangements that give maintainers authority to surface hidden risk.
- Evidence distinguishing knowledge captured by sensors from embodied, relational, contextual, or safety-critical knowledge that remains outside the record.
- Consent, ownership, surveillance, bargaining, and benefit-sharing arrangements for instrumented work.

## Suggested next sources or actions

- Add postmortems from public-sector and regulated-industry modernization programs.
- Map critical services to systems, specialist cohorts, vendors, and undocumented operating practices.
- Track which knowledge becomes obsolete, which remains infrastructural, and who decides.

## Resolution

_Open. The corpus now adds continuous co-adaptation, the Toyota craftspeople case, and instrumented AI-supported process experimentation as candidate mechanisms. It still does not compare strategies, establish long-term outcomes, or show when capture preserves living expertise rather than extracting a snapshot and weakening practitioner agency._
