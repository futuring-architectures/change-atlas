# Who controls the path between publication and attention?

## Why it matters

Publishing no longer implies delivery or visibility. Ranked feeds, recommendation systems, search engines, curators, and user-controlled filters mediate the path between a speaker and a willing recipient. Their choices shape who can build an audience, which knowledge accumulates, and whether platform objectives override explicit subscriptions.

## What is currently known

- [[recommendation-media|Recommendation media]] makes platform ranking the primary distributor rather than the social graph.
- [[dominant-social-platforms-substitute-ranking-for-requested-delivery|Dominant platforms have been characterized as treating follows as ranking input rather than delivery commands]].
- [[the-future-of-search-is-boutique-evi-ccdaac8e8841#Claim 2|The Future of Search Is Boutique — claim 2]] argues for retrieval on the user’s terms rather than dependence on publication timing.
- [[end-to-end-evi-f0a80b1bc636#Claim 1|End-to-End — claim 1]] states a best-effort delivery principle for messages a recipient requested.

The corpus establishes competing allocation principles—platform optimization, recipient request, curator judgment, and search—but does not determine their appropriate boundaries.

## Missing or discriminating evidence

- Comparative outcome data for chronological, subscription, search, and recommendation modes.
- User behavior when control over ranking objectives and feed modes is genuinely available.
- Creator dependence and audience portability across allocation systems.
- Effects on discovery, overload, diversity, manipulation, safety, and shared knowledge.
- Governance models for changing ranking objectives and resolving speaker-recipient-platform conflicts.

## Suggested next sources or actions

- Track products that expose multiple distribution modes with comparable usage data.
- Compare end-to-end delivery rights with safety and overload interventions.
- Record cases where audience portability changes bargaining power or platform behavior.
- Distinguish user-controlled agents from platform-controlled personalization in future AI-filtering evidence.

## Resolution

_Open. The current corpus supports the control problem but not a universal allocation rule._
