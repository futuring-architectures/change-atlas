# What rights follow from ecosystem contribution?

## Why it matters

Ecosystems expand useful capacity beyond employment and ownership, but formal rights often remain inside the firm. Treating every user as an owner is neither feasible nor necessarily desired; treating essential contributors as ordinary consumers can hide dependence and extraction. The missing step is a defensible connection between contribution, exposure to risk, and particular rights.

## What is currently known

- [[orbital-stakeholders|Orbital stakeholders]] names material contributors and dependents beyond the formal core.
- [[participation-is-infrastructure|Participation is infrastructure]] shows that user activity can become part of system operation.
- [[permeable-organizations-exploring-the-boundary-of-the-firm-evi-8bdbc7ca9ce6#Claim 2|Permeable Organizations — claim 2]] proposes access, voice, and ownership.
- [[exit-to-community-evi-bffdd32df955#Claim 1|Exit to Community — claim 1]] reports that cooperatives remained unusual among prominent digital startups; [[exit-to-community-evi-bffdd32df955#Claim 2|claim 2]] argues that community-powered models make this surprising.
- [[delivering-people-evi-acaef3d02086#Claim 1|Delivering People — claim 1]] describes unpaid user production becoming a structural source of captured value.

## Missing or discriminating evidence

- Cases linking different contribution types to compensation, ownership, voice, portability, or appeal.
- Participant preferences: some contributors may value payment or exit over governance work.
- Longitudinal outcomes for cooperative, trust, token, profit-sharing, and conventional ownership forms.
- Boundary tests for casual use, cultural contribution, critical complement, and economic dependence.
- Evidence on coordination cost, investment incentives, adverse selection, and capture inside community governance.

## Suggested next sources or actions

- Apply the [[ecosystem-boundary-audit|Ecosystem boundary audit]] to one platform and one modular supply chain.
- Separate rights to information, income, voice, ownership, portability, appeal, and fork rather than treating “stake” as one bundle.
- Compare the rights contributors request with those firms assume they value.

## Resolution

_Open. The collection establishes the contribution-rights mismatch and several proposed rights, but not a general allocation rule._
