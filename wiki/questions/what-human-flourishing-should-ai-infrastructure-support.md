# What human flourishing should AI infrastructure support?

## Why it matters

Infrastructure quietly defines which activities are easy, legible, rewarded, or impossible. If AI is treated only as a productivity instrument, its deployment can optimize activity while leaving unspecified the human capacities and relationships the system should sustain.

## What is currently known

- [[the-ai-debate-were-not-having-evi-709fe1681265#Claim 1|The AI debate we're not having — claim 1]] argues that prevailing debate is too narrow; [[the-ai-debate-were-not-having-evi-709fe1681265#Claim 2|claim 2]] asks how AI can become infrastructure for human flourishing.
- [[95-theses-on-innovation-evi-5a08afa6b495#Claim 2|95 Theses on Innovation — claim 2]] frames innovation as a means to human progress rather than an end.
- [[the-other-invisible-hand-evi-b29d7bc541cf#Claim 1|The Other Invisible Hand — claim 1]] connects institutional design to the people and capacities it selects.
- [[how-im-preparing-my-parentsand-myselfto-be-fluent-in-ai-evi-49b353f8c559#Claim 2|How I’m Preparing My Parents — claim 2]] makes inclusion in epistemic fluency a design obligation.

## Missing or discriminating evidence

- Participatory definitions of flourishing across affected groups and cultures.
- Longitudinal measures of agency, learning, relationships, livelihood, and health alongside productivity.
- Evidence about which capacities atrophy or expand under sustained AI mediation.
- Governance arrangements that let public values alter infrastructure design.

## Suggested next sources or actions

- Compare AI deployments that optimize the same task under different human-outcome measures.
- Document which stakeholders can define, contest, and revise those measures.
- Track capability distribution and dependency over time, not only immediate output.

## Resolution

_Open. The corpus establishes the need to specify human ends but does not supply a shared definition or evaluated infrastructure model._
