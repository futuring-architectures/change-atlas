# What business model can support a prosocial social network?

## Why it matters

Network experiences are shaped by the behavior their economics rewards. Advertising can reward attention and agitation; token ownership may reward participation while adding speculation; subscriptions can align a product with paying users while excluding others. A feature-level intervention may fail if the revenue and ownership layers continue to reward the behavior it is meant to prevent.

## What is currently known

- [[a-prosocial-social-network-evi-3008443b1aee#Claim 1|A Prosocial Social Network — claim 1]] attributes persistent agitation to prevailing business models rather than to the medium itself.
- [[a-prosocial-social-network-evi-3008443b1aee#Claim 2|Claim 2]] states that a changed business model changes incentives and can change experience.
- [[protocols-and-creator-daos-evi-61d7a22c66ed#Claim 2|Protocols and Creator DAOs — claim 2]] proposes participant ownership as a path from transaction toward community.
- [[you-can-give-people-what-they-want-or-you-can-give-them-web3-evi-6cfbeebb7dc9#Claim 2|You Can Give People What They Want — claim 2]] identifies complex financial participation as an adoption barrier.
- [[the-rise-of-the-aligned-web-evi-355708350079#Claim 2|The Rise of the Aligned Web — claim 2]] argues that transparent payment relationships may reduce hidden incentive practices, but its qualification notes that transparency alone does not eliminate surveillance or engagement optimization.

The corpus supplies hypotheses and critiques, not a demonstrated durable model.

## Missing or discriminating evidence

- Longitudinal cases comparing social outcomes under advertising, subscription, cooperative, token, public, and hybrid models.
- Measures of prosocial behavior that do not collapse into engagement or growth.
- Cost, access, and distributional effects of each funding model.
- Governance of model objectives, moderation, data use, and participant value capture.
- Failure cases in which ostensibly aligned ownership reproduced concentration or speculation.

## Suggested next sources or actions

- Track named networks with materially different revenue and ownership structures.
- Separate business-model effects from topology, product design, moderation, and user composition using the [[network-outcome-design-stack|network outcome design stack]].
- Record who pays, who works, who owns, who decides, and who can exit in every future case.

## Resolution

_Open. No business model is promoted as prosocial without observed outcomes and boundary conditions._
