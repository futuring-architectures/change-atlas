# When should infrastructure providers control content?

## Why it matters

Infrastructure providers can become powerful chokepoints because their decisions can remove an entire service rather than moderate individual content. Treating them as neutral conduits preserves user and application-layer choice, but absolute neutrality may conflict with legal duties or direct operational harms.

## What is currently known

- The EFF position is that users should decide what they access wherever feasible: [[beyond-platforms-private-censorship-parler-and-the-stack-evi-c5e5b2dff047#Claim 1|user choice over content access]].
- It argues that infrastructure companies should avoid content-control chokepoints and defend a conduit role: [[beyond-platforms-private-censorship-parler-and-the-stack-evi-c5e5b2dff047#Claim 2|infrastructure providers as conduits]].
- Online speech systems may not fit inherited platform or publisher categories, suggesting that rules should follow actual system operation: [[online-speech-and-publishing-evi-709567530c09#Claim 1|online speech as a distinct institutional form]] and [[online-speech-and-publishing-evi-709567530c09#Claim 2|rules grounded in system operation]].

The pilot contains a clear neutrality position but no comparably developed case for infrastructure intervention. It therefore supports an open Question rather than a balanced Debate.

## Missing or discriminating evidence

- A layer-by-layer taxonomy of infrastructure roles and substitutability.
- Cases distinguishing content judgment from direct security, payment, hosting, or legal risk.
- Evidence about the reversibility and due process of infrastructure decisions.
- Competing normative positions and jurisdiction-specific obligations.

## Suggested next sources or actions

- Collect cases across hosting, app stores, payments, DNS, connectivity, and security services.
- Compare the reach of an intervention with availability of realistic alternatives.
- Identify conditions under which conduit treatment fails to address direct harm.

## Resolution

_Open. The present corpus is normatively one-sided and does not justify a general rule._
