# How should firms measure value when software is easy to replace?

## Why it matters

If alternatives become cheap to build and switching becomes easier, renewal can no longer be assumed to demonstrate value. Vendors may have to accept accountability for outcomes, while buyers need measures that distinguish software contribution from changes in people, process, demand, and context.

## What is currently known

- [[easier-to-build-easier-to-replace-evi-cc6e7a684039#Claim 1|Easier to build, Easier to replace? — claim 1]] predicts pressure to demonstrate measurable economic change.
- [[easier-to-build-easier-to-replace-evi-cc6e7a684039#Claim 2|Easier to build, Easier to replace? — claim 2]] predicts movement from access toward accountability.
- [[the-vibe-coding-trap-evi-6bc4abe83e11#Claim 1|The vibe coding trap — claim 1]] narrows the threat to poorly differentiated SaaS.

## Missing or discriminating evidence

- Comparative switching rates and total switching costs before and after AI-assisted building.
- Contract forms that link payment to outcomes without rewarding metric gaming.
- Cases separating product contribution from implementation and organizational redesign.
- Evidence about whether data, trust, distribution, and integration remain stronger moats than code.

## Suggested next sources or actions

Track software categories with credible AI-built substitutes, outcome-based pricing, and observable buyer results. Compare them with high-integration or regulated categories.
