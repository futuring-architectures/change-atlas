# When does scale change the governing principle?

## Why it matters

AI can make practices cheap, pervasive, and continuously executable. If scale changes reach, reversibility, chilling effects, or the distribution of power, rules designed for occasional human use may govern the wrong object even when each individual action looks familiar.

## What is currently known

- Benedict Evans argues that AI can make previously small-scale practices [[generative-ai-and-intellectual-property-evi-f3ed589d9de7#Claim 1|practical at massive scale]].
- He proposes that [[generative-ai-and-intellectual-property-evi-f3ed589d9de7#Claim 2|a difference in scale can become a difference in principle]], illustrated by the contrast between police carrying wanted photographs and ubiquitous facial-recognition cameras.
- [[governance-category-mismatch|Governance category mismatch]] explains why inherited categories may fail after that transition, but it does not identify the threshold.

The present Evidence states the principle and an analogy. It does not supply a general threshold test or a measured transition.

## Missing or discriminating evidence

- Which dimensions matter: frequency, coverage, cost, speed, persistence, identifiability, coordination, or irreversibility.
- Cases where increased scale changed legal treatment, and comparable cases where it did not.
- Whether thresholds should depend on aggregate harm, individual rights, market structure, or institutional capacity.
- How to distinguish a true change in kind from rhetoric attached to a large quantitative increase.

## Suggested next sources or actions

- Compare facial recognition, copyright reproduction, automated persuasion, and high-frequency decision systems.
- Build a matrix of scale dimensions against harms, rights, and governance responses.
- Search for counter-cases in which pervasive automation did not require a new governing principle.

## Resolution

_Open. The collection supports the question but not a cross-domain threshold._
