# Who governs user-authored agents?

## Question

When a user can teach an agent how to act, who owns and can inspect that accumulated behavior? Who authorizes tools, protects other people’s data, resolves conflicting instructions, bears liability, and preserves portability when the provider changes?

## Why it remains open

User programmability mixes roles that conventional software kept separate: developer, operator, employer, data controller, and affected party. Editable prompts increase control but do not settle security, collective rights, maintenance, or recourse.

## What would answer it

- Comparative governance models for personal, workplace, community, and public agents.
- Rights over persistent context, generated procedures, logs, and behavioral portability.
- Clear allocation of decision rights and liability across user, provider, toolmaker, and deployer.
- Evidence that affected nonusers can contest agent actions.
