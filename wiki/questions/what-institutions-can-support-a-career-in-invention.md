# What institutions can support a career in invention?

## Why it matters

If invention depends on rare personal wealth, an entrepreneurial company, or a research role with different incentives, societies may underproduce practical inventors and lose people whose promise appears late. A career structure can renew capability, but it can also narrow what counts as legitimate invention.

## What is currently known

- [[invention-lacked-a-professional-career-path|Invention lacked a professional career path]] identifies missing entry, employment, community, and prestige.
- [[the-case-for-opsimaths-evi-2a5a50d7380a#Claim 1|The case for opsimaths — claim 1]] argues that institutions poorly recognize future late bloomers.
- [[the-silicon-and-industrial-revolutions-evi-6a3a0212c5f6#Claim 2|The Silicon and Industrial Revolutions — claim 2]] makes cumulative production improvement part of invention's success.

## Missing or discriminating evidence

- Comparative inventor careers across labs, fellowships, companies, public agencies, and independent practice.
- Selection systems that identify capability without privileging conventional early achievement.
- Funding and prestige structures tied to useful invention rather than patents or startup formation alone.
- Evidence about inclusion, failure tolerance, and long-term progression.

## Suggested next sources or actions

Map historical and current inventor institutions, their entry requirements, funding duration, output definitions, and participant trajectories.
