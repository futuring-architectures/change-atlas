# Who gets to author organizational culture?

## Why it matters

Calling culture emergent can hide power; calling it designed can hide participation. Leaders control resources, incentives, selection, and official narrative, while workers and affected communities enact, reinterpret, resist, and bear the result.

## What is currently known

- [[culture-as-an-emergent-operating-system|Culture as an emergent operating system]] rejects direct managerial installation.
- [[culture-influence-stack|The Culture influence stack]] identifies the levers through which power still shapes emergence.
- [[narrative-as-organizational-firmware|Narrative as organizational firmware]] shows why a durable story can coordinate and constrain interpretation.
- [[how-to-manage-disruptive-talent-evi-7f4e63b0bcc7#Claim 2|How To Manage Disruptive Talent — claim 2]] shows dissent being neutralized by organizational immunity.

## Missing or discriminating evidence

- Cases where workers or communities can revise an official narrative without managerial sponsorship.
- Governance arrangements for cultural decisions, dissent, and counter-narratives.
- Evidence linking participation in cultural authorship with trust, performance, inclusion, or adaptation.
- Failure cases in which narrative coherence suppressed warnings or legitimate difference.

## Suggested next sources or actions

- Map formal and informal authors of a consequential organizational story.
- Record who can challenge the story and what happens when they do.
- Compare stated beneficiaries with those who bear costs and hold revision rights.

## Resolution

_Open. The collection identifies influence and narrative mechanisms but not legitimate authorship rules._
