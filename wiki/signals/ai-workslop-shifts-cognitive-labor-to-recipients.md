# AI workslop shifts cognitive labor to recipients

## Observation

In a 2025 workplace report, recipients of low-quality AI-generated output described having to infer the sender's intent, reconstruct missing context, verify claims, and redo the work. Apparent production savings for one participant became interpretation and repair work for others.

## Scope and date

- Report publication: 2025-09-22.
- Scope: reported workplace experiences labeled “workslop” by the authors.
- Unit of observation: recipient burden, not firm-level productivity.

## Evidence

- [[ai-generated-workslop-is-destroying-productivity-evi-58ee7347e6af#Claim 1|AI-Generated “Workslop” Is Destroying Productivity — claim 1]] records the recipient tasks.
- [[ai-generated-workslop-is-destroying-productivity-evi-58ee7347e6af#Claim 2|AI-Generated “Workslop” Is Destroying Productivity — claim 2]] identifies shifted rather than eliminated cognitive labor.

## Limitations and alternative readings

- The excerpt does not supply the complete sample design or a prevalence estimate.
- Poorly contextualized human work can produce the same burden; AI changes the cost and volume of producing it.
- High-quality AI use can reduce total effort when task ownership, review, and recipient needs are clear.

## Interpretation

Productivity must be measured across the workflow, not at the point where an artifact is generated. Fast production can be locally efficient and systemically wasteful when validation and context reconstruction are externalized.
