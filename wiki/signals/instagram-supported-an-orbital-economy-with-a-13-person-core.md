# Instagram supported an orbital economy with a 13-person core

## Observation

Tom Critchlow reports that Instagram employed 13 people when Facebook acquired it, while influencers, early adopters, and passionate users had already formed a wider economy around the product. The value-creating organizational field was therefore larger than the legal and employment boundary of the company.

## Why it matters

Headcount can radically understate an organization's productive dependencies. A small core may coordinate code, capital, and policy while users and complementors supply culture, demand, distribution, and content. Governance designed only around employees and shareholders can miss those dependencies.

## Limits

- The 2020 source is retrospective; `observed_at` denotes the 2012 acquisition year.
- The excerpt does not quantify the external economy or establish the economic dependence of each participant.
- An orbital economy does not by itself establish an entitlement to ownership or governance rights.
