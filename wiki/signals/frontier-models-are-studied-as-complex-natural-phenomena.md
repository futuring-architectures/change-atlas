# Frontier models are studied as complex natural phenomena

## Observation

A 2024 research report describes scientists experimenting on the largest language models as they would on complex, partly unexplained phenomena. In 2025, Anthropic researchers published a mechanistic-interpretability study that reverse-engineered selected Claude 3.5 Haiku behaviors using biological and neuroscience analogies, while explicitly presenting only partial hypotheses about internal mechanisms.

## Scope and timing

- Scope: Frontier-language-model research and the cited interpretability program; not all AI research.
- Observation dates: 2024-03-04 and 2025-03-27.
- Date confidence: High for both publication dates.

## Evidence

- [[large-language-models-can-do-jaw-dropping-things-but-nobody-knows-exactly-why-evi-1835a056d920#Claim 1|Large language models can do jaw-dropping things — claim 1]] reports the experimental stance toward model behavior.
- [[on-the-biology-of-a-large-language-model-evi-c3db139e9d1d#Claim 2|On the Biology of a Large Language Model — claim 2]] reports empirical reverse engineering of complex learned mechanisms.

## Limitations and alternative readings

- “Natural phenomenon” and “biology” are methodological metaphors; models remain engineered artifacts with known training algorithms.
- Partial explanation is not total ignorance. Many components and causal interventions are understood even when a complete account is unavailable.
- The selected sources may foreground frontier-model complexity more than routine model engineering does.
