# TikTok turns viewing into a dense behavioral feedback loop

## Observation

In May 2022, Scott Galloway reported an average TikTok session of approximately 11 minutes and an average video length of approximately 25 seconds. That implied about 26 viewing episodes per session, with scrolling, pausing, replaying, liking, commenting, sharing, following, and watch duration producing hundreds of behavioral signals. The source interprets these signals as inputs to algorithmic refinement and personalization.

## Scope and timing

- Scope: TikTok viewing sessions and recommendation inputs reported in 2022.
- Observation date: 2022-05-20 publication date; the underlying measurement period is not stated.
- Date confidence: Publication date verified; metric period and population unresolved.

## Evidence

- [[tiktok-boom-evi-9e4f0ee6a2a7#Claim 1|TikTok Boom — claim 1]] reports session length, video length, episode count, and signal density.
- [[tiktok-boom-evi-9e4f0ee6a2a7#Claim 2|TikTok Boom — claim 2]] interprets the signals as recommendation-system inputs.

## Limitations and alternative readings

- The excerpt does not identify the dataset, geography, age distribution, or variation among users.
- Signal density does not establish which inputs the production system actually uses or their causal weight.
- A high-feedback interface can improve relevance while also increasing platform power over attention; the observation does not adjudicate the value of that outcome.
