# Students resisted discarding AI prototypes in an entrepreneurship class

## Observation

Steve Blank reports that all eight teams in the Spring 2026 Lean LaunchPad class presented AI-assisted products on day one that resembled demonstrations instructors previously expected near the end of the quarter. Instructors initially read that visible completeness as progress, then found that teams had substituted AI answers for customer validation and used prototypes to solicit reactions and compliments rather than investigate problems.

Many students resisted discarding initial products despite contrary customer evidence, and instructors intervened. Blank interprets that resistance as greater psychological pivot cost even though technical changes were cheap. He also reports less nuanced understanding and difficulty defending assumptions after teams delegated interview summarization. One team produced a new product each week without accumulating its learning, so frequent changes did not reliably demonstrate adaptation either.

The same review reports benefits: AI helped teams identify better questions and relevant people to ask, accelerating hypothesis testing. The case therefore concerns how assistance was used and evaluated, alongside its production speed.

## Scope and timing

- Scope: One Stanford entrepreneurship course cohort of eight teams, described by its instructor.
- Observation period: Spring 2026; exact session and intervention dates unspecified.
- Publication date: 2026-09-23.
- Evidence form: Retrospective firsthand teaching account and after-action review, without a controlled comparison or scored learning assessment.

## Limitations and alternative readings

- The account does not establish that AI caused every observed failure or that these failures generalize to other cohorts, experienced founders, or other kinds of product.
- Product attachment and leading customer interviews predate AI. Cheap polish may amplify them, but the causal contribution is unmeasured.
- Finished-looking demonstrations establish neither production readiness nor customer demand.
- The psychological-cost explanation and learning-debt diagnosis interpret reported behavior; neither is a measured long-term effect.
- The replacement pedagogy and durability of instructor intervention are not evaluated in this installment.
