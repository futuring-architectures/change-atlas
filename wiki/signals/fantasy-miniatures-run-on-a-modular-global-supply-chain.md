# Fantasy miniatures run on a modular global supply chain

## Observation

Patrick McKenzie documents a supply chain in which independent sculptors, small printing firms, marketplaces, printer and board manufacturers, software services, logistics providers, and payment platforms jointly deliver customized fantasy miniatures. Subscription aggregation and multinational pay-ins and payouts knit the participants together without a single vertically integrated producer.

## Why it matters

Software and financial infrastructure can make coordination capabilities available as services. This lets small firms specialize and compose a product that previously favored integrated manufacturing. It also creates a dependency map in which orchestration, discovery, payment, hardware, and software bottlenecks may sit in different organizations.

- [[plastic-and-payments-in-the-fantasy-supply-chain-evi-8368069fd3ef#Claim 1|Plastic and Payments — claim 1]] identifies the aggregated subscription and multinational payment structure.
- [[plastic-and-payments-in-the-fantasy-supply-chain-evi-8368069fd3ef#Claim 2|claim 2]] interprets the resulting business model as non-vertically integrated.

## Limits

- This is one detailed niche case, not a measured prevalence claim.
- Some layers remain concentrated or integrated, including marketplaces, board manufacturing, and payment infrastructure.
- Modularity can relocate rather than remove dependency and bargaining power.
