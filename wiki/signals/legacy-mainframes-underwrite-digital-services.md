# Legacy mainframes underwrite digital services

## Observation

Steven Sinofsky reports that online travel and banking continued to depend on IBM mainframes and on people maintaining them. New customer-facing layers did not replace the underlying infrastructure in one step; legacy systems and expertise remained part of the transition.

## Why it matters

Technological change is layered and uneven. Work that appears obsolete from the interface layer can remain essential at the substrate layer. Transition capacity therefore includes stewardship, translation, and migration—not only invention and adoption.

## Evidence

- [[death-of-software-nah-evi-84cd7cb167b1#Claim 1|Death of Software. Nah. — claim 1]] states the need for legacy specialists during long, uneven transitions.
- [[death-of-software-nah-evi-84cd7cb167b1#Claim 2|claim 2]] gives online banking and travel as mainframe-dependent cases.

## Limits

- The source supplies a sector-level account rather than a system inventory or measured dependency.
- Continued dependence does not show that every legacy component should be preserved indefinitely.
- The optimal balance among maintenance, encapsulation, migration, and replacement remains case-specific.
