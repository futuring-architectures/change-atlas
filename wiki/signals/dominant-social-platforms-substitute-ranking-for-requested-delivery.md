# Dominant social platforms substitute ranking for requested delivery

## Observation

Cory Doctorow characterized Facebook, Twitter, TikTok, YouTube, and other dominant platforms as treating the people a user follows as recommendation input rather than as a command to deliver those speakers’ messages. In this design, a subscription expresses one signal among many; platform ranking determines what reaches the user.

## Scope and timing

- Scope: Ranked feeds on named large social and media platforms.
- Observation date: 2023-01-15 publication date.
- Date confidence: Publication date and named platform scope verified; individual product surfaces vary.

## Evidence

- [[end-to-end-evi-f0a80b1bc636#Claim 2|End-to-End — claim 2]] reports the substitution of ranked recommendation for requested delivery.

## Limitations and alternative readings

- Some platforms retain chronological, following-only, notification, or direct-message surfaces.
- Ranking can reduce overload and improve relevance; the observation does not establish that unranked delivery is preferable in every context.
- One source names several platforms but does not provide a platform-by-platform audit or longitudinal comparison.
