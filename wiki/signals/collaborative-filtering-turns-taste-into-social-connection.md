# Collaborative filtering turns taste into social connection

## Observation

Pattie Maes's early collaborative-filtering work moved beyond personalized recommendation. Firefly used patterns of shared taste to help people find one another, making an inferred cultural affinity into a possible social relationship. A database of preferences therefore became a mechanism for constructing a social graph before social networking became a mainstream product category.

## Why it matters

The case shows that classification is generative. A system that decides which people are statistically similar does not merely describe culture; it creates opportunities for some relationships and not others. The selection method becomes part of the social topology.

## Limits

- The source is a retrospective account published in 2022; `observed_at` refers to the 1994 prototype period described by the source.
- Shared taste does not establish compatibility, trust, or mutual desire for connection.
- The case predates contemporary ranking, advertising, and large-scale platform governance.
