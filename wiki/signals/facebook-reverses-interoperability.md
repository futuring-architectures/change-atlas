# Facebook reverses its interoperability posture

## Observation

Facebook reportedly moved from offering a suite of interoperability tools to reducing those tools and resisting rival attempts to interoperate. The case is relevant because the same source interprets the reversal as an incentive change between entrant and incumbent positions.

## Scope and timing

- Scope: Facebook's interoperability practices as reported by Cory Doctorow.
- Observation date: Before 2021-07-05; the excerpt does not date the individual product or enforcement changes.
- Date confidence: Low for the underlying events; the source publication date is inferred and the event sequence is undated.

## Evidence

- [[tech-monopolies-and-the-insufficient-necessity-of-interoperability-evi-c8bbfe1c2ca0#Claim 3|Interoperability — claim 3]] reports the reduction of tools and opposition to rival interoperability.
- The associated [[tech-monopolies-and-the-insufficient-necessity-of-interoperability-evi-c8bbfe1c2ca0#Claim 1|entrant incentive]] and [[tech-monopolies-and-the-insufficient-necessity-of-interoperability-evi-c8bbfe1c2ca0#Claim 2|incumbent incentive]] are interpretations, not additional observations.

## Limitations and alternative readings

- The curated excerpt does not identify which tools changed, when they changed, or the stated reasons for enforcement against rivals.
- One company case cannot establish a general Trend from openness to closure.
- Product retirement, privacy, security, and strategic exclusion are possible competing explanations that require underlying event evidence.
