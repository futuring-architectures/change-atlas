# AI production hides a human assembly line

## Observation

Josh Dzieza’s 2023 report describes a distributed labor apparatus behind commercial AI. Coherent work is decomposed into tasks, arranged in production pipelines, and divided between humans and machines while the product’s futuristic surface makes that apparatus difficult to see.

## Scope and timing

- Scope: The data, annotation, and operational production systems documented in the source; not every AI model or application.
- Observation date: Reported on 2023-06-20; the underlying labor practices predate publication but are not separately dated in the excerpt.
- Date confidence: High for publication, lower for the start and duration of each reported practice.

## Evidence

- [[ai-is-a-lot-of-work-evi-4ba3bef3f292#Claim 1|AI Is a Lot of Work — claim 1]] reports the hidden manufacturing apparatus and its workers.
- [[ai-is-a-lot-of-work-evi-4ba3bef3f292#Claim 2|AI Is a Lot of Work — claim 2]] interprets the task decomposition as a digital assembly line.

## Limitations and alternative readings

- The Evidence does not quantify how much frontier-model or application work follows this structure.
- Task decomposition may coexist with highly skilled, relational, and iterative work not captured by the assembly-line metaphor.
- Later automation can move, transform, or conceal human labor rather than eliminate it.
