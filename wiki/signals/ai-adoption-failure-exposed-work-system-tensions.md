# AI adoption failure exposed work-system tensions

## Observation

A July 2026 activity-theory account treats failed or low AI adoption as a diagnostic signal: a new tool can expose contradictions among goals, roles, rules, incentives, and division of labor that were latent before the tool arrived. Training the user more intensely leaves those system tensions intact.

A September 2026 practitioner account adds several cases consistent with that mechanism. It reports a UK government Copilot trial in which satisfaction coexisted with weak usage and no robust productivity evidence, two processes where touch time was measured in minutes but elapsed time in days or weeks, and a reconciliation redesign that removed queues and handoffs rather than merely accelerating matching. The same source reports large gains across other deployments, but those aggregated results are vendor-selected and not independently documented.

## Scope and timing

- Scope: a system-level explanatory account of organizational AI adoption.
- Observation dates: 2026-07-02 for the activity-system account and 2026-09-04 for the enterprise case account; the cited government trial occurred in 2025.
- Date confidence: publication dates are verified. The UK trial is reported secondhand, while the other operational cases are self-reported by the implementing vendor.

## Limitations and alternative readings

- Technology defects, inadequate training, incentives, and individual preferences can also depress adoption.
- The excerpt supplies a lens rather than comparative outcome evidence.
- Not every surfaced tension should be resolved in favor of adoption.
- The later cases are not a representative sample, and the implementing vendor selected and markets the successful examples.
- Before using the Copilot figures comparatively, inspect the primary government report and its task-quality methods.
