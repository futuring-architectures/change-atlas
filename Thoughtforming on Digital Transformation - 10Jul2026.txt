# **Thoughtforming** **on Digital Transformation**

In the [Thoughtforms link log](https://thoughtforms.substack.com/), we collect remarkable stories at the intersection of technology, business, design, and culture. As it turns out, these are remarkably hype-free and nicely distanced from The-Current-Thing.

So, why not line up all of these stories? Have them record the narrative of *digital* as it calmly chips away at our behaviour and expectations and the social, public, and civic institutions we inherited from the outgoing industrial paradigm.

Here's your Google Doc telling of great futures, of new shapes of prosperity, and of the novel ways for us to get there. It’s by no means final: maybe there is an article left out or a topic we missed. Feel invited to comment directly or get in touch at [hello@futuring-architectures.com](mailto:hello@futuring-architectures.com) 🙏

## ⚡ Paradigms

### [Moore's Law for Everything](https://moores.samaltman.com/)

*\[L\]abor is the driving cost at many levels of the supply chain. If robots can build a house on land you already own from natural resources mined and refined onsite, using solar power, the cost of building that house is close to the cost to rent the robots. And if those robots are made by other robots, the cost to rent them will be much less than it was when humans made them.*

### [The Robots Are Coming for Phil in Accounting](https://www.nytimes.com/2021/03/06/business/the-robots-are-coming-for-phil-in-accounting.html)

*This shift may be related to the popularity of “so-so automation” — technology that is just barely good enough to replace human workers, but not good enough to create new jobs or make companies significantly more productive. This simple, substitutive kind of automation threatens not just individual workers, but the economy as a whole.*

### [Market-Making on the Internet](https://stratechery.com/2021/market-making-on-the-internet/)

*Web 2.0 assumed that your one identity would connect together the different pieces of your web existence. However, just as the future of social networking is about different identities for different contexts, interoperability via markets is about linking together distinct user bases in a way that is appropriate for different services, all under the control of the user who is paying for the privilege.*

### [Against Scarcity](https://davidphelps.substack.com/p/against-scarcity)

*\[O\]ur online worlds should enable a post-scarcity infinitude of freely shared ideas, goods, and protocols, but of course that would only make sense if they were untethered to the actual world where we need money to sleep and eat. Is the web’s artificial replication of scarcity economics an early-stage quirk, or is it a more fundamental reflection of the fact that we can only visit virtual worlds, but ultimately have to live in one of rocks and trees?*

### [The Death of The Middle](https://eriktorenberg.substack.com/p/the-death-of-the-middle)

*The internet is changing our preferences — we’re getting more interested in either exactly what we want, or whatever’s most frictionless. Aggregate or specialize. In other words, give people everything they want or the one thing they need. Everything in the middle gets slaughtered.*

### [The Atom, The Bit And The Gene: Silicon Valley’s Innovator’s Dilemma](https://digitaltonto.com/2021/the-atom-the-bit-and-the-gene-silicon-valleys-innovators-dilemma/)
