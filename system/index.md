## Concepts

- [[wiki/concepts/abundance-relocates-bottlenecks|Abundance relocates bottlenecks]] — When technology makes one input radically cheaper, constraint does not disappear; it moves toward complementary resources such as attention, trust, integration, oversight, energy, or capital.
- [[wiki/concepts/attention-is-the-scarce-layer|Attention is the scarce layer]] — When content supply exceeds human consumption capacity, selection and allocation of attention become the binding layer of the information system.
- [[wiki/concepts/capability-is-not-competence|Capability is not competence]] — Success on an output, benchmark, or bounded task does not by itself establish reliable performance across the full workflow, context, and accountability of competent practice.
- [[wiki/concepts/co-acting-group|Co-acting group]] — A loose confederation whose members enter and leave collaboration as work unfolds, used where a stable and highly interdependent true team is unnecessary.
- [[wiki/concepts/contextual-decentralization|Contextual decentralization]] — Decentralization evaluated by whether affected people gain usable decision agency in their social context, not only by the distribution of technical components.
- [[wiki/concepts/cultural-and-social-technology|Cultural and social technology]] — A technology that reorganizes accumulated human information and coordinates social action, making culture and institutions part of its operation rather than merely downstream effects.
- [[wiki/concepts/culture-as-an-emergent-operating-system|Culture as an emergent operating system]] — Culture is the path-dependent pattern produced by repeated behavior, interaction, incentives, selection, and interpretation—not a value statement that management can install directly.
- [[wiki/concepts/customer-capitalism|Customer capitalism]] — An operating logic in which a firm starts from customer needs and treats financial value as an outcome rather than its primary purpose.
- [[wiki/concepts/ecosystem-advantage|Ecosystem advantage]] — Advantage produced by access to external capabilities, relationships, signals, distribution, and complements that no firm needs to own in full.
- [[wiki/concepts/governance-category-mismatch|Governance category mismatch]] — A governance error in which inherited institutional categories fail to describe how a new technological system actually operates.
- [[wiki/concepts/hierarchy-as-meaning-infrastructure|Hierarchy as meaning infrastructure]] — Hierarchy can route context, interpretation, identity, and development as well as information, so replacing information flow does not replace every managerial function.
- [[wiki/concepts/institutional-legibility-limits|Institutional legibility limits]] — The limit created when measurable representations omit the habits, relationships, and distributed skills through which an institution actually functions.
- [[wiki/concepts/metaphors-steer-ai-inquiry|Metaphors steer AI inquiry]] — Calling AI a mind, tool, organism, worker, database, or infrastructure changes which questions appear natural, which tests seem valid, and where responsibility is located.
- [[wiki/concepts/narrative-as-organizational-firmware|Narrative as organizational firmware]] — A durable organizational narrative can coordinate identity and interpretation beneath surface communications, while repeated tactical patches accumulate narrative debt.
- [[wiki/concepts/network-topology-shapes-cultural-outcomes|Network topology shapes cultural outcomes]] — The structure and interaction rules of a network influence which information spreads, which behaviors coordinate, and which cultural differences survive.
- [[wiki/concepts/non-coercive-marketing|Non-coercive marketing]] — Rob Hardy's approach to marketing as truthful invitation: leave authority, timing, and outcome with the prospective customer rather than manufacture pressure for a predetermined decision.
- [[wiki/concepts/openness-is-not-self-determination|Openness is not self-determination]] — The distinction between access-oriented openness and the power to determine how a technology is controlled, changed, and governed.
- [[wiki/concepts/orbital-stakeholders|Orbital stakeholders]] — People and organizations outside a firm's formal boundary whose contributions, dependencies, risks, or relationships materially affect the system's ability to create value.
- [[wiki/concepts/participation-is-infrastructure|Participation is infrastructure]] — Participants do not merely consume a network; their labor, signals, norms, content, and relationships can become part of the system that makes it useful.
- [[wiki/concepts/power-as-an-organizing-design-value|Power as an organizing design value]] — The idea that repeated design trade-offs reproduce whichever value organizes a technological system, including power itself.
- [[wiki/concepts/productive-friction|Productive friction]] — Resistance, delay, effort, or incompleteness that contributes to learning, judgment, exploration, memory, or safety rather than merely wasting resources.
- [[wiki/concepts/programmable-organization|Programmable organization]] — A prospective organization whose workflows and coordination can be described, composed, and executed through software and AI-mediated control layers.
- [[wiki/concepts/recommendation-media|Recommendation media]] — A media-distribution model in which platform-defined algorithms, rather than the social graph, primarily decide what content reaches each user.
- [[wiki/concepts/refounding|Refounding]] — A deliberate reinterpretation of an established organization for changed constraints that preserves continuity with its original business while altering how it operates.
- [[wiki/concepts/structural-uncertainty|Structural uncertainty]] — Uncertainty that changes the playing field or object of action rather than conditions within a known structure.
- [[wiki/concepts/the-human-in-the-loop-paradox|The human-in-the-loop paradox]] — Automation introduced because humans are fallible cannot depend for safety on a perfectly attentive, informed, and empowered human monitor.
- [[wiki/concepts/the-technium|The technium]] — Kevin Kelly's name for the interdependent system of technologies whose combinations and feedback produce emergent tendencies beyond any single artifact.
- [[wiki/concepts/variance-amplifying-institutions|Variance-amplifying institutions]] — Institutions that create positive feedback and select for extremity, surprise, virality, and uncapped outcomes rather than norm adherence and predictable ranges.

## Debates

- [[wiki/debates/does-ai-automate-thought-or-reproduce-patterns|Does AI automate thought or reproduce patterns?]] — The corpus disagrees over whether language-model performance is best understood as cognitive automation, special intelligence, fuzzy retrieval, or convincing simulation without reasoning.
- [[wiki/debates/does-ai-flatten-hierarchy-or-intensify-management|Does AI flatten hierarchy or intensify management?]] — AI can widen individual scope and reduce coordination layers while also increasing orchestration, assurance, rule-setting, and top-level control.
- [[wiki/debates/does-cheap-intelligence-reduce-the-need-for-human-collaboration|Does cheap intelligence reduce the need for human collaboration?]] — AI can reduce the need to ask colleagues for production help while making collective commitment, evaluation, learning, and accountable judgment more important.
- [[wiki/debates/does-network-density-strengthen-collective-intelligence-or-erode-variation|Does network density strengthen collective intelligence or erode variation?]] — Dense connection can accelerate diffusion and coordination while homogenizing culture, amplifying contagion, and reducing the protected spaces where small ideas develop.
- [[wiki/debates/does-progress-require-acceleration-or-productive-friction|Does progress require acceleration or productive friction?]] — The corpus disputes whether technological progress is primarily blocked by delay and timidity or enabled by effort, patience, constraint, and learning through failure.
- [[wiki/debates/is-ai-a-tool-mind-organism-or-infrastructure|Is AI a tool, mind, organism, or infrastructure?]] — Competing AI metaphors assign different units of analysis, evaluation methods, expectations, and locations of responsibility.
- [[wiki/debates/what-kind-of-decentralization-creates-agency|What kind of decentralization creates agency?]] — Whether agency comes primarily from open protocols and forkability, censorship-resistant reachability, local decision rights, ownership, or broader public accountability.
- [[wiki/debates/platform-power-and-antitrust|What should antitrust optimize for in platform markets?]] — Whether platform policy should prioritize revealed consumer choice and benefits or market structure, contestability, and incumbent control.
- [[wiki/debates/who-captures-the-gains-from-automation|Who captures the gains from automation?]] — Automation can expand productive capability while distributing ownership, income, agency, deskilling, and hidden labor very unevenly.
- [[wiki/debates/will-cheaper-software-commoditize-advantage-or-deepen-scale-moats|Will cheaper software commoditize advantage or deepen scale moats?]] — Lower software-production costs may erode application margins and proprietary moats, or increase the advantage of firms that control compute, models, distribution, data, and integration.

- [[wiki/questions/how-can-firms-distribute-control-without-losing-coordination|How can firms distribute control without losing coordination?]] — Which combinations of local autonomy, shared standards, orchestration, identity, and escalation let an ecosystem distribute decision power while remaining coherent and accountable?

- [[wiki/questions/how-should-products-manage-model-drift|How should products manage model drift?]] — How should AI products be designed, budgeted, and governed when provider models, behavior, price, and performance can change beneath a stable product interface?
- [[wiki/questions/how-would-ai-information-filtering-reshape-shared-reality|How would AI information filtering reshape shared reality?]] — What happens to individual agency and shared culture when people can delegate large parts of their information diet to AI filters?
- [[wiki/questions/what-business-model-can-support-a-prosocial-social-network|What business model can support a prosocial social network?]] — Which revenue, ownership, and governance arrangements can sustain a social network without making agitation, surveillance, or financialization its dominant incentive?
- [[wiki/questions/what-replaces-hierarchy-as-meaning-infrastructure|What replaces hierarchy as meaning infrastructure?]] — If AI and distributed work reduce hierarchy's information-routing role, which institutions will supply context, judgment, development, identity, and responsibility?
- [[wiki/questions/what-replaces-human-review-when-software-exceeds-human-attention|What replaces human review when software exceeds human attention?]] — What evidence, controls, and accountability can establish trust when generated production code grows faster than humans can inspect it?
- [[wiki/questions/what-rights-follow-from-ecosystem-contribution|What rights follow from ecosystem contribution?]] — When people outside a firm supply essential labor, data, content, demand, trust, or complements, which combinations of information, compensation, voice, portability, ownership, and recourse should follow?
- [[wiki/questions/when-does-scale-change-the-governing-principle|When does scale change the governing principle?]] — Which thresholds make the mass automation of a previously limited practice different in principle rather than merely larger in quantity?
- [[wiki/questions/when-should-infrastructure-providers-control-content|When should infrastructure providers control content?]] — Which conditions justify infrastructure-layer intervention in content, and which decisions should remain with users or application-layer services?
- [[wiki/questions/which-ai-interfaces-preserve-human-agency|Which AI interfaces preserve human agency?]] — Which interface forms let people shape goals, understand state, correct inference, and retain responsibility instead of becoming junior operators of opaque automation?
- [[wiki/questions/which-frictions-should-technology-preserve|Which frictions should technology preserve?]] — How can designers distinguish waste and exclusion from effort, delay, constraint, and failure that build learning, judgment, safety, or durable capability?
- [[wiki/questions/who-controls-the-path-between-publication-and-attention|Who controls the path between publication and attention?]] — When publication is abundant but visibility is scarce, which rights should speakers, recipients, curators, and platforms have over distribution?
- [[wiki/questions/who-gets-to-author-organizational-culture|Who gets to author organizational culture?]] — If culture emerges but leaders control consequential levers and narratives, whose interpretation governs and how can participants contest it?
