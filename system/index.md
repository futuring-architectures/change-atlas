## Concepts

- [[wiki/concepts/abundance-relocates-bottlenecks|Abundance relocates bottlenecks]] — When technology makes one input radically cheaper, constraint does not disappear; it moves toward complementary resources such as attention, trust, integration, oversight, energy, or capital.
- [[wiki/concepts/action-without-prediction|Action without prediction]] — Decision practice that does not require one forecast to be correct: preserve options, run bounded probes, monitor consequences, and revise.
- [[wiki/concepts/activity-alignment-gap|Activity-alignment gap]] — The gap between a system continuing to operate successfully and continuing to advance the purpose it should serve.
- [[wiki/concepts/agent-experience|Agent experience]] — The usability of a tool, platform, or protocol from the perspective of an AI agent acting under human authority.
- [[wiki/concepts/anticipatory-friction|Anticipatory friction]] — Reversible pauses, speed limits, staged exposure, and recourse designed in because breakage is expected even when its exact form is unknown.
- [[wiki/concepts/architecture-is-the-scaling-constraint|Architecture is the scaling constraint]] — When component production accelerates, coherent boundaries, constraints, evaluation, and integration become the limiting architecture.
- [[wiki/concepts/attention-is-the-scarce-layer|Attention is the scarce layer]] — When content supply exceeds human consumption capacity, selection and allocation of attention become the binding layer of the information system.
- [[wiki/concepts/automation-is-an-arrangement-of-power|Automation is an arrangement of power]] — Automation outcomes follow from how people, capital, tasks, ownership, and decision rights are arranged—not from technical capability alone.
- [[wiki/concepts/capability-is-not-competence|Capability is not competence]] — Success on an output, benchmark, or bounded task does not by itself establish reliable performance across the full workflow, context, and accountability of competent practice.
- [[wiki/concepts/capability-not-role-as-unit-of-organization-design|Capability, not role, as the unit of organization design]] — A design principle that starts from changing human and machine capabilities, then rebuilds workflows, boundaries, and authority instead of placing agents inside inherited jobs.
- [[wiki/concepts/capability-deployment-gap|Capability-deployment gap]] — The distance between what a model can demonstrate and what a sociotechnical system can deploy usefully, safely, legitimately, and at scale.
- [[wiki/concepts/capability-renewal-infrastructure|Capability-renewal infrastructure]] — The institutions, communities, access, practice, prestige, maintenance, and succession through which a capability remains learnable and renewable.
- [[wiki/concepts/capacity-erosion-through-assistance|Capacity erosion through assistance]] — The possibility that repeated assistance improves immediate output while weakening the persistence, verification, participation, and practice required for independent future action.
- [[wiki/concepts/co-acting-group|Co-acting group]] — A loose confederation whose members enter and leave collaboration as work unfolds, used where a stable and highly interdependent true team is unnecessary.
- [[wiki/concepts/cognitive-debt|Cognitive debt]] — The accumulated gap between a system's operating complexity and the context people and tools can reliably recover to understand and change it.
- [[wiki/concepts/community-as-a-retention-layer|Community as a retention layer]] — Content can attract attention while belonging, identity, and direct relationships sustain participation beyond consumption of any single artifact.
- [[wiki/concepts/contextual-decentralization|Contextual decentralization]] — Decentralization evaluated by whether affected people gain usable decision agency in their social context, not only by the distribution of technical components.
- [[wiki/concepts/contextual-defensibility|Contextual defensibility]] — Durable advantage after technical commoditization can reside in situated problem framing, data models, domain history, judgment, and a repeatedly refined point of view.
- [[wiki/concepts/creator-as-platform-dependent-entrepreneur|Creator as platform-dependent entrepreneur]] — The creator label promises independent self-expression while requiring continuous marketability through platforms that retain control over distribution and metrics.
- [[wiki/concepts/creator-attention-is-a-depletable-commons|Creator attention is a depletable commons]] — Digital outputs may be non-rival while the attention, maintenance, moderation, and boundary work surrounding them is scarce and exhaustible.
- [[wiki/concepts/cultural-and-social-technology|Cultural and social technology]] — A technology that reorganizes accumulated human information and coordinates social action, making culture and institutions part of its operation rather than merely downstream effects.
- [[wiki/concepts/culture-as-an-emergent-operating-system|Culture as an emergent operating system]] — Culture is the path-dependent pattern produced by repeated behavior, interaction, incentives, selection, and interpretation—not a value statement that management can install directly.
- [[wiki/concepts/customer-capitalism|Customer capitalism]] — An operating logic in which a firm starts from customer needs and treats financial value as an outcome rather than its primary purpose.
- [[wiki/concepts/database-reality-inversion|Database-reality inversion]] — The institutional tendency to make lived practice conform to a data model when the model no longer represents reality.
- [[wiki/concepts/design-for-emergence|Design for emergence]] — A design posture that gives users the means and authority to construct context-specific outcomes instead of fixing one end state in advance.
- [[wiki/concepts/ecosystem-advantage|Ecosystem advantage]] — Advantage produced by access to external capabilities, relationships, signals, distribution, and complements that no firm needs to own in full.
- [[wiki/concepts/epistemic-fluency|Epistemic fluency]] — The situated ability to question, guide, verify, and decide what to trust in an AI-mediated environment.
- [[wiki/concepts/extended-mind|Extended mind]] — The view that cognition is enacted through a hybrid system of brain, body, tools, people, representations, and institutions.
- [[wiki/concepts/future-narratives-allocate-permission|Future narratives allocate permission]] — Claims about what technology will inevitably do authorize present investment, removal, delay, or control before the forecast is proven.
- [[wiki/concepts/governance-category-mismatch|Governance category mismatch]] — A governance error in which inherited institutional categories fail to describe how a new technological system actually operates.
- [[wiki/concepts/hierarchy-as-meaning-infrastructure|Hierarchy as meaning infrastructure]] — Hierarchy can route context, interpretation, identity, and development as well as information, so replacing information flow does not replace every managerial function.
- [[wiki/concepts/high-resolution-taste-markets|High-resolution taste markets]] — Plural discovery systems can allocate attention across many taste vectors, allowing different creators to be perceived as the best for different publics.
- [[wiki/concepts/ingredient-technology|Ingredient technology]] — A general capability that becomes valuable only when surrounded by domain-specific workflow, interface, data, policy, and operating code.
- [[wiki/concepts/innovation-ecology|Innovation ecology]] — An innovation system organized around deliberate variation, selection, diffusion, reinvestment, and renewal rather than one linear funnel or heroic inventor.
- [[wiki/concepts/innoveracy|Innoveracy]] — The inability to distinguish novelty, creation, invention, and innovation, producing confused explanations of business success and economic growth.
- [[wiki/concepts/institutional-legibility-limits|Institutional legibility limits]] — The limit created when measurable representations omit the habits, relationships, and distributed skills through which an institution actually functions.
- [[wiki/concepts/intent-as-executable-material|Intent as executable material]] — Natural language, examples, and context increasingly function as executable specifications rather than merely descriptions passed to a separate implementation layer.
- [[wiki/concepts/liquid-super-team|Liquid Super Team]] — A proposed network of independent people who temporarily combine complementary capabilities while retaining individual autonomy.
- [[wiki/concepts/metaphors-steer-ai-inquiry|Metaphors steer AI inquiry]] — Calling AI a mind, tool, organism, worker, database, or infrastructure changes which questions appear natural, which tests seem valid, and where responsibility is located.
- [[wiki/concepts/narrative-as-organizational-firmware|Narrative as organizational firmware]] — A durable organizational narrative can coordinate identity and interpretation beneath surface communications, while repeated tactical patches accumulate narrative debt.
- [[wiki/concepts/network-topology-shapes-cultural-outcomes|Network topology shapes cultural outcomes]] — The structure and interaction rules of a network influence which information spreads, which behaviors coordinate, and which cultural differences survive.
- [[wiki/concepts/non-coercive-marketing|Non-coercive marketing]] — Rob Hardy's approach to marketing as truthful invitation: leave authority, timing, and outcome with the prospective customer rather than manufacture pressure for a predetermined decision.
- [[wiki/concepts/openness-is-not-self-determination|Openness is not self-determination]] — The distinction between access-oriented openness and the power to determine how a technology is controlled, changed, and governed.
- [[wiki/concepts/orbital-stakeholders|Orbital stakeholders]] — People and organizations outside a firm's formal boundary whose contributions, dependencies, risks, or relationships materially affect the system's ability to create value.
- [[wiki/concepts/pace-layering|Pace layering]] — A systems view in which components change at different speeds because fast experimentation and slow stability perform different functions.
- [[wiki/concepts/participation-is-infrastructure|Participation is infrastructure]] — Participants do not merely consume a network; their labor, signals, norms, content, and relationships can become part of the system that makes it useful.
- [[wiki/concepts/platform-brand-employment|Platform-brand employment]] — An employer can lend legitimacy, investment, and distribution that grows an employee's public audience, making employment partly an audience-building partnership.
- [[wiki/concepts/power-as-an-organizing-design-value|Power as an organizing design value]] — The idea that repeated design trade-offs reproduce whichever value organizes a technological system, including power itself.
- [[wiki/concepts/present-blindness|Present blindness]] — The future-facing error produced when attention noise, novelty, inherited labels, and tacit assumptions prevent accurate perception of current conditions.
- [[wiki/concepts/productive-friction|Productive friction]] — Resistance, delay, effort, or incompleteness that contributes to learning, judgment, exploration, memory, or safety rather than merely wasting resources.
- [[wiki/concepts/programmable-organization|Programmable organization]] — A prospective organization whose workflows and coordination can be described, composed, and executed through software and AI-mediated control layers.
- [[wiki/concepts/recommendation-media|Recommendation media]] — A media-distribution model in which platform-defined algorithms, rather than the social graph, primarily decide what content reaches each user.
- [[wiki/concepts/refounding|Refounding]] — A deliberate reinterpretation of an established organization for changed constraints that preserves continuity with its original business while altering how it operates.
- [[wiki/concepts/responsibility-gap-after-automation|Responsibility gap after automation]] — Automation can preserve a human role formally while hollowing out the judgment and ownership needed to remain responsible for outcomes.
- [[wiki/concepts/signal-collapse-after-cheap-production|Signal collapse after cheap production]] — When an artifact becomes cheap to produce, its existence stops reliably signaling the knowledge, commitment, judgment, or capability previously required to make it.
- [[wiki/concepts/structural-uncertainty|Structural uncertainty]] — Uncertainty that changes the playing field or object of action rather than conditions within a known structure.
- [[wiki/concepts/task-boundary-work|Task-boundary work]] — The ambiguous coordination, interpretation, exception, and specification work between benchmarkable tasks that helps make a job whole.
- [[wiki/concepts/taste-after-technical-commoditization|Taste after technical commoditization]] — When execution becomes cheap and widely accessible, selection, distinction, cultural fit, and status can become more important sources of value.
- [[wiki/concepts/the-human-in-the-loop-paradox|The human-in-the-loop paradox]] — Automation introduced because humans are fallible cannot depend for safety on a perfectly attentive, informed, and empowered human monitor.
- [[wiki/concepts/the-technium|The technium]] — Kevin Kelly's name for the interdependent system of technologies whose combinations and feedback produce emergent tendencies beyond any single artifact.
- [[wiki/concepts/type-two-growth|Type 2 growth]] — Growth through betterment—freedom, wisdom, quality, complexity, choice, and capability—rather than increasing material throughput alone.
- [[wiki/concepts/user-programmable-agents|User-programmable agents]] — Agents whose persistent behavior, context, tools, and boundaries can be inspected and shaped by the people on whose behalf they act.
- [[wiki/concepts/variance-amplifying-institutions|Variance-amplifying institutions]] — Institutions that create positive feedback and select for extremity, surprise, virality, and uncapped outcomes rather than norm adherence and predictable ranges.
- [[wiki/concepts/verification-is-the-scarce-layer|Verification is the scarce layer]] — When plausible output becomes cheap, trustworthy use depends on the capacity to test claims, fit, provenance, consequences, and responsibility.
- [[wiki/concepts/vibe-as-contextual-judgment|Vibe as contextual judgment]] — Vibe is contextual appraisal of plausibility, fit, tone, and significance—useful for orientation but unsafe as a substitute for factual or accountable judgment.

## Debates

- [[wiki/debates/can-growth-continue-without-material-expansion|Can growth continue without material expansion?]] — Qualitative betterment may continue after material throughput is constrained, but intangible gains still depend on infrastructure and can coexist with rebound and unequal distribution.
- [[wiki/debates/do-niches-democratize-creative-opportunity|Do niches democratize creative opportunity?]] — More taste vectors and creator tools can widen entry while reproducing power laws, platform dependence, financial insecurity, and continuous self-marketing.
- [[wiki/debates/does-ai-automate-thought-or-reproduce-patterns|Does AI automate thought or reproduce patterns?]] — The corpus disagrees over whether language-model performance is best understood as cognitive automation, special intelligence, fuzzy retrieval, or convincing simulation without reasoning.
- [[wiki/debates/does-ai-expand-expertise-or-hollow-it-out|Does AI expand expertise or hollow it out?]] — The same abstraction that expands individual reach can remove the practice, feedback, and responsibility through which expertise develops.
- [[wiki/debates/does-ai-flatten-hierarchy-or-intensify-management|Does AI flatten hierarchy or intensify management?]] — AI can widen individual scope and reduce coordination layers while also increasing orchestration, assurance, rule-setting, and top-level control.
- [[wiki/debates/does-ai-progress-require-scaling-or-new-paradigms|Does AI progress require scaling or new paradigms?]] — Rapid improvement within the current paradigm coexists with arguments that human-like learning and paradigm-generating science require a qualitative break.
- [[wiki/debates/does-cheap-code-retire-technical-debt-or-amplify-architectural-debt|Does cheap code retire technical debt or amplify architectural debt?]] — Cheap generation and remediation could make code disposable, or increase change volume until coherence, verification, and architecture become the dominant debt.
- [[wiki/debates/does-cheap-creation-democratize-expression-or-cheapen-culture|Does cheap creation democratize expression or cheapen culture?]] — Generative tools widen access while enabling overproduction, disposable work, imitation, and new status hierarchies.
- [[wiki/debates/does-cheap-implementation-improve-innovation-or-obscure-understanding|Does cheap implementation improve innovation or obscure understanding?]] — Cheap implementation enables more experiments, but can hide whether builders understand users, systems, and consequences while weakening familiar evaluation signals.
- [[wiki/debates/does-cheap-intelligence-reduce-the-need-for-human-collaboration|Does cheap intelligence reduce the need for human collaboration?]] — AI can reduce the need to ask colleagues for production help while making collective commitment, evaluation, learning, and accountable judgment more important.
- [[wiki/debates/does-cheap-software-distribute-agency-or-relocate-control|Does cheap software distribute agency or relocate control?]] — Natural-language building can return control to domain users, but model, agent, distribution, and governance layers can become new chokepoints.
- [[wiki/debates/does-community-ownership-create-agency-or-financialize-belonging|Does community ownership create agency or financialize belonging?]] — Tokens and shared ownership may align participants and distribute upside, but can also turn taste, identity, and belonging into speculative assets.
- [[wiki/debates/does-network-density-strengthen-collective-intelligence-or-erode-variation|Does network density strengthen collective intelligence or erode variation?]] — Dense connection can accelerate diffusion and coordination while homogenizing culture, amplifying contagion, and reducing the protected spaces where small ideas develop.
- [[wiki/debates/does-progress-require-acceleration-or-productive-friction|Does progress require acceleration or productive friction?]] — The corpus disputes whether technological progress is primarily blocked by delay and timidity or enabled by effort, patience, constraint, and learning through failure.
- [[wiki/debates/does-relational-ai-expand-agency-or-dissolve-shared-worlds|Does relational AI expand agency or dissolve shared worlds?]] — Conversational systems can widen access and reflective partnership while anthropomorphic, personalized interfaces intensify attachment and fragment shared deliberation.
- [[wiki/debates/is-ai-a-tool-mind-organism-or-infrastructure|Is AI a tool, mind, organism, or infrastructure?]] — Competing AI metaphors assign different units of analysis, evaluation methods, expectations, and locations of responsibility.
- [[wiki/debates/is-technology-resistance-an-obstacle-or-design-input|Is technology resistance an obstacle or design input?]] — Persistent resistance may block beneficial learning, or reveal harms, values, and legitimacy conditions that transition design has failed to address.
- [[wiki/debates/should-ai-agents-be-designed-as-workers-or-capabilities|Should AI agents be designed as workers or capabilities?]] — Worker metaphors make agents organizationally legible, but capability framing may reveal deeper workflow, authority, and system redesign.
- [[wiki/debates/should-ai-creators-be-paid-for-inputs-or-outputs|Should AI creators be paid for inputs or outputs?]] — Training licenses compensate controlled inputs once, while output-linked systems might support ongoing creative ecosystems but face difficult attribution and governance.
- [[wiki/debates/what-kind-of-decentralization-creates-agency|What kind of decentralization creates agency?]] — Whether agency comes primarily from open protocols and forkability, censorship-resistant reachability, local decision rights, ownership, or broader public accountability.
- [[wiki/debates/platform-power-and-antitrust|What should antitrust optimize for in platform markets?]] — Whether platform policy should prioritize revealed consumer choice and benefits or market structure, contestability, and incumbent control.
- [[wiki/debates/who-captures-the-gains-from-automation|Who captures the gains from automation?]] — Automation can expand productive capability while distributing ownership, income, agency, deskilling, and hidden labor very unevenly.
- [[wiki/debates/will-cheaper-software-commoditize-advantage-or-deepen-scale-moats|Will cheaper software commoditize advantage or deepen scale moats?]] — Lower software-production costs may erode application margins and proprietary moats, or increase the advantage of firms that control compute, models, distribution, data, and integration.

## Questions

- [[wiki/questions/can-human-oversight-handle-rare-catastrophic-failures|Can human oversight handle rare catastrophic failures?]] — What oversight design remains effective when automated systems are usually correct, difficult to characterize, and occasionally fail severely?
- [[wiki/questions/how-can-ai-assistance-preserve-persistence-verification-and-participation|How can AI assistance preserve persistence, verification, and participation?]] — Immediate output can rise while the human capacities required to persist, check, contest, and participate decay; interfaces need longitudinal capacity measures.
- [[wiki/questions/how-can-ai-mediated-work-preserve-apprenticeship-and-meaning|How can AI-mediated work preserve apprenticeship and meaning?]] — If workers increasingly ask machines instead of colleagues, which practices preserve tacit learning, recognition, judgment, and belonging?
- [[wiki/questions/how-can-firms-distribute-control-without-losing-coordination|How can firms distribute control without losing coordination?]] — Which combinations of local autonomy, shared standards, orchestration, identity, and escalation let an ecosystem distribute decision power while remaining coherent and accountable?
- [[wiki/questions/how-can-institutions-preserve-tacit-knowledge-through-transition|How can institutions preserve tacit knowledge through transition?]] — How can organizations replace or transform legacy systems without losing the people, practices, context, and failure knowledge that keep essential services working?
- [[wiki/questions/how-can-participation-scale-without-exhausting-creators|How can participation scale without exhausting creators?]] — Which boundaries, roles, funding, tools, and governance let communities grow without converting success into unbounded maintainer work?
- [[wiki/questions/how-can-private-exploration-coexist-with-collective-accountability|How can private exploration coexist with collective accountability?]] — Creative work may need low-observability space to avoid performative conformity, while shared work still requires coordination, review, and responsibility.
- [[wiki/questions/how-is-judgment-cultivated-when-production-is-cheap|How is judgment cultivated when production is cheap?]] — If tools perform more execution, which practices develop formulation, taste, verification, restraint, and responsibility rather than merely approving output?
- [[wiki/questions/how-should-firms-measure-value-when-software-is-easy-to-replace|How should firms measure value when software is easy to replace?]] — Lower switching and building costs weaken access, seats, and renewal as proxies for economic value, but outcome accountability is difficult to attribute.
- [[wiki/questions/how-should-products-manage-model-drift|How should products manage model drift?]] — How should AI products be designed, budgeted, and governed when provider models, behavior, price, and performance can change beneath a stable product interface?
- [[wiki/questions/how-would-ai-information-filtering-reshape-shared-reality|How would AI information filtering reshape shared reality?]] — What happens to individual agency and shared culture when people can delegate large parts of their information diet to AI filters?
- [[wiki/questions/what-business-model-can-support-a-prosocial-social-network|What business model can support a prosocial social network?]] — Which revenue, ownership, and governance arrangements can sustain a social network without making agitation, surveillance, or financialization its dominant incentive?
- [[wiki/questions/what-human-flourishing-should-ai-infrastructure-support|What human flourishing should AI infrastructure support?]] — Which capabilities, relationships, rights, and forms of agency should AI infrastructure deliberately preserve and expand?
- [[wiki/questions/what-institutions-can-support-a-career-in-invention|What institutions can support a career in invention?]] — Invention needs entry, practice, funding, recognition, community, and progression without forcing every inventor into research, engineering management, or entrepreneurship.
- [[wiki/questions/what-makes-a-creator-middle-class-durable|What makes a creator middle class durable?]] — Which combinations of discovery, direct relationships, recurring income, runway, bargaining power, and portable infrastructure sustain more than selected stars?
- [[wiki/questions/what-replaces-hierarchy-as-meaning-infrastructure|What replaces hierarchy as meaning infrastructure?]] — If AI and distributed work reduce hierarchy's information-routing role, which institutions will supply context, judgment, development, identity, and responsibility?
- [[wiki/questions/what-replaces-human-review-when-software-exceeds-human-attention|What replaces human review when software exceeds human attention?]] — What evidence, controls, and accountability can establish trust when generated production code grows faster than humans can inspect it?
- [[wiki/questions/what-rights-follow-from-ecosystem-contribution|What rights follow from ecosystem contribution?]] — When people outside a firm supply essential labor, data, content, demand, trust, or complements, which combinations of information, compensation, voice, portability, ownership, and recourse should follow?
- [[wiki/questions/when-does-scale-change-the-governing-principle|When does scale change the governing principle?]] — Which thresholds make the mass automation of a previously limited practice different in principle rather than merely larger in quantity?
- [[wiki/questions/when-does-uncertainty-justify-limiting-scale|When does uncertainty justify limiting scale?]] — What combination of stakes, irreversibility, exposure, missing evidence, and weak recourse should block or stage large-scale deployment?
- [[wiki/questions/when-should-infrastructure-providers-control-content|When should infrastructure providers control content?]] — Which conditions justify infrastructure-layer intervention in content, and which decisions should remain with users or application-layer services?
- [[wiki/questions/when-should-resistance-change-or-stop-a-technology-transition|When should resistance change or stop a technology transition?]] — What evidence distinguishes correctable uncertainty, legitimate refusal, and a design or governance condition that should slow, redirect, or stop deployment?
- [[wiki/questions/which-ai-interfaces-preserve-human-agency|Which AI interfaces preserve human agency?]] — Which interface forms let people shape goals, understand state, correct inference, and retain responsibility instead of becoming junior operators of opaque automation?
- [[wiki/questions/which-frictions-should-technology-preserve|Which frictions should technology preserve?]] — How can designers distinguish waste and exclusion from effort, delay, constraint, and failure that build learning, judgment, safety, or durable capability?
- [[wiki/questions/who-controls-the-path-between-publication-and-attention|Who controls the path between publication and attention?]] — When publication is abundant but visibility is scarce, which rights should speakers, recipients, curators, and platforms have over distribution?
- [[wiki/questions/who-gets-to-author-organizational-culture|Who gets to author organizational culture?]] — If culture emerges but leaders control consequential levers and narratives, whose interpretation governs and how can participants contest it?
- [[wiki/questions/who-governs-user-authored-agents|Who governs user-authored agents?]] — When users shape persistent agent behavior, who owns the instructions and context, sets limits, bears liability, and supplies recourse?
